After this page you will be able to ask where a place is and how to get there, and to understand a simple answer: left, right, straight on, near, far, here and there. You will also know why Ukrainian says <Uk>на вокза́лі</Uk> for "at the station" but <Uk>на вокза́л</Uk> for "to the station".

## Asking where something is

### Де …?

The simplest question is <Uk>Де</Uk> (where) followed by the place, in its dictionary form (the nominative). There is no word for *is*: Ukrainian leaves it out in the present tense. [Questions](/grammar/questions/) introduces <Uk>де</Uk> and the other question words.

<ExampleList title="Де + place">
  <Example uk="Де вокза́л?" en="Where is the station?" />
  <Example uk="Де зупи́нка?" en="Where is the stop?" note="A bus, tram or trolleybus stop." />
  <Example uk="Де тут туале́т?" en="Where is the toilet here?" note="тут (here) is optional, but common." />
</ExampleList>

To a stranger, start politely with <Uk>Ви́бачте</Uk> (Excuse me) and add <Uk>будь ла́ска</Uk> (please). A common polite opening is <Uk>Ви не підка́жете, …?</Uk>, literally "Won't you tell me…?".

<ExampleList title="Polite ways to ask">
  <Example uk="Ви́бачте, де апте́ка?" en="Excuse me, where is the pharmacy?" note="Formal or plural: ви́бачте is the ви form." />
  <Example uk="Скажі́ть, будь ла́ска, де по́шта?" en="Could you tell me where the post office is, please?" note="Formal or plural: скажі́ть is the ви form." />
  <Example uk="Ви не підка́жете, де метро́?" en="Could you tell me where the metro is?" note="Formal (one person): ви." />
</ExampleList>

### Чи є тут …?

To ask whether there is a place nearby at all, use <Uk>Чи є тут …?</Uk> (Is there … here?). The word <Uk>є</Uk> means "there is". The place stays in the dictionary form. The answer *no* uses <Uk>нема́є</Uk> (there isn't) with the **genitive** case, so the ending changes. [Negation](/grammar/negation/) explains this.

<ExampleList title="Is there … here?">
  <Example uk="Чи є тут апте́ка?" en="Is there a pharmacy here?" />
  <Example uk="Чи є поблизу́ банкома́т?" en="Is there a cash machine near here?" note="поблизу́: nearby." />
  <Example uk="Ні, тут нема́є апте́ки." en="No, there isn't a pharmacy here." note="апте́ки: genitive, after нема́є." />
</ExampleList>

## Asking how to get there

### Як дістатися до …?

<Uk>Як діста́тися до …?</Uk> means "How do I get to …?". <Uk>До</Uk> (to) always takes the **genitive**, so the place changes its ending. Usage guides prefer <Uk>діста́тися</Uk> to <Uk>добра́тися</Uk> [src: goroh-usage]. You can also ask more specifically: <Uk>Як дійти́ до…?</Uk> (on foot) or <Uk>Як дої́хати до…?</Uk> (by transport).

<ExampleList title="Як діста́тися до + genitive">
  <Example uk="Як діста́тися до вокза́лу?" en="How do I get to the station?" note="вокза́л → до вокза́лу." />
  <Example uk="Як діста́тися до це́нтру?" en="How do I get to the centre?" note="центр → до це́нтру." />
  <Example uk="Як діста́тися до апте́ки?" en="How do I get to the pharmacy?" note="апте́ка → до апте́ки." />
  <Example uk="Як діста́тися до ста́нції метро́?" en="How do I get to the metro station?" note="ста́нція → до ста́нції. метро́ never changes its form." />
</ExampleList>

The examples show the forms you need. Masculine nouns have two possible genitive endings, <Uk>-а</Uk> and <Uk>-у</Uk>, and the choice must be learned word by word. [Cases, an overview](/grammar/case-overview/) introduces the genitive.

## Understanding the answer

### Left, right, straight on

Directions often use a polite command (the imperative) in the <Uk>ви</Uk> form, ending in <Uk>-іть</Uk> or <Uk>-йте</Uk>.

<ExampleList title="Which way">
  <Example uk="Іді́ть пря́мо." en="Go straight on." note="Formal or plural: іді́ть is the ви form of іти́ (to go, on foot)." />
  <Example uk="Поверні́ть ліво́руч." en="Turn left." note="Formal or plural: поверні́ть is the ви form." />
  <Example uk="Поверні́ть право́руч." en="Turn right." note="Formal or plural: поверні́ть is the ви form." />
  <Example uk="Апте́ка ліво́руч." en="The pharmacy is on the left." />
</ExampleList>

You will also hear <Uk>налі́во</Uk> (to the left) and <Uk>напра́во</Uk> (to the right). Usage guides recommend <Uk>ліво́руч</Uk> and <Uk>право́руч</Uk> instead [src: goroh-usage].

### Near, far, here, there

<ExampleList title="Where it is">
  <Example uk="Це дале́ко?" en="Is it far?" />
  <Example uk="Ні, це бли́зько." en="No, it's near." />
  <Example uk="Це недале́ко, п’ять хвили́н пі́шки." en="It's not far, five minutes on foot." note="п’ять хвили́н: after п’ять, the noun is in the genitive plural." />
  <Example uk="Зупи́нка там." en="The stop is over there." />
  <Example uk="Банкома́т тут, за ро́гом." en="The cash machine is here, round the corner." note="за ро́гом: за with the instrumental, a place." />
  <Example uk="Апте́ка бі́ля вокза́лу." en="The pharmacy is near the station." note="бі́ля (near, next to) takes the genitive." />
  <Example uk="По́шта навпро́ти апте́ки." en="The post office is opposite the pharmacy." note="навпро́ти (opposite) takes the genitive." />
</ExampleList>

<Uk>Тут</Uk> means *here* and <Uk>там</Uk> means *there*. To point something out, use <Uk>ось</Uk>: <Uk>Ось вокза́л.</Uk> (Here's the station.)

## Place or direction

Ukrainian keeps two ideas apart that English often mixes:

- **A place**, where something is. The question is <Uk>де?</Uk> (where?). After <Uk>у</Uk>/<Uk>в</Uk> or <Uk>на</Uk>, the noun is in the **locative**.
- **A direction**, where someone is going. The question is <Uk>куди́?</Uk> (where to?). After <Uk>у</Uk>/<Uk>в</Uk> or <Uk>на</Uk>, the noun is in the **accusative**. With <Uk>до</Uk>, it is in the genitive.

<ExampleList title="Place and direction">
  <Example uk="Я на вокза́лі." en="I'm at the station." note="вокза́лі: locative. A place." />
  <Example uk="Мені́ тре́ба на вокза́л." en="I need to get to the station." note="вокза́л: accusative, the same as the dictionary form. A direction." />
  <Example uk="Мені́ тре́ба до вокза́лу." en="I need to get to the station." note="до + genitive: also a direction." />
  <Example uk="Ми в це́нтрі." en="We're in the centre." note="це́нтрі: locative." />
  <Example uk="Ми ї́демо в центр." en="We're going to the centre." note="центр: accusative. Going by transport." />
</ExampleList>

<Uk>Мені́ тре́ба …</Uk> means "I need to …" and is a handy way to say where you want to go. Which nouns take <Uk>в</Uk> and which take <Uk>на</Uk> has to be learned word by word: <Uk>на вокза́лі</Uk>, <Uk>на по́шті</Uk>, but <Uk>в апте́ці</Uk>, <Uk>у це́нтрі</Uk>. [Common prepositions](/grammar/prepositions/) has a list, and explains when to write <Uk>у</Uk> or <Uk>в</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §23].

## Dialogue: finding a pharmacy

A visitor stops a passer-by in the street. They are strangers, so they use <Uk>ви</Uk>.

<ExampleList title="Де тут апте́ка?">
  <Example uk="— Ви́бачте, ви не підка́жете, де тут апте́ка?" en="“Excuse me, could you tell me where there's a pharmacy here?”" note="Formal (one person): ви." />
  <Example uk="— Іді́ть пря́мо, по́тім поверні́ть ліво́руч." en="“Go straight on, then turn left.”" note="Formal (one person): іді́ть, поверні́ть." />
  <Example uk="— Це дале́ко?" en="“Is it far?”" />
  <Example uk="— Ні, це бли́зько. Апте́ка навпро́ти по́шти." en="“No, it's near. The pharmacy is opposite the post office.”" />
  <Example uk="— Дя́кую!" en="“Thank you!”" />
  <Example uk="— Будь ла́ска." en="“You're welcome.”" />
</ExampleList>

## Common mistakes and limits

<Note type="mistake">
  **Using <Uk>де</Uk> for a direction.** With verbs of going, ask <Uk>куди́</Uk>: <Uk>Куди́ ви ї́дете?</Uk> (Where are you going? Formal or plural: <Uk>ви</Uk>.) Use <Uk>де</Uk> only for where something is.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Leaving the noun unchanged after <Uk>до</Uk>.** <Uk>До</Uk> needs the genitive: <Uk>до апте́ки</Uk>, <Uk>до вокза́лу</Uk>, not *<Uk>до апте́ка</Uk>*, *<Uk>до вокза́л</Uk>*.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Mixing up place and direction.** <Uk>Я на вокза́лі</Uk> (I'm at the station) uses the locative. <Uk>Мені́ тре́ба на вокза́л</Uk> (I need to get to the station) uses the accusative.
</Note>

<Note type="remember">
  This page uses only the polite <Uk>ви</Uk> forms, because you will usually ask strangers. Directions with public transport, street names and ordinal numbers (the first street, the second turning) come later. If the answer is too fast, say <Uk>Пові́льніше, будь ла́ска</Uk> (More slowly, please) or <Uk>Повторі́ть, будь ла́ска</Uk> (Please say that again; formal or plural).
</Note>
