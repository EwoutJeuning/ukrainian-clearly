After this page you will be able to name the days of the week, say *today*, *tomorrow* and *yesterday*, ask and tell the time, and say at what time something happens: <Uk>о дру́гій</Uk> (at two), <Uk>о сьо́мій ве́чора</Uk> (at seven in the evening).

## Days of the week

The names of the days are written with a small letter. Most of them are feminine; <Uk>понеді́лок</Uk>, <Uk>вівто́рок</Uk> and <Uk>четве́р</Uk> are masculine.

To say *on Monday*, *on Tuesday* and so on, use <Uk>у</Uk> or <Uk>в</Uk> with the **accusative** [src: ukma-ukrainian-foreign-language, Lesson 9]. The feminine days change their ending from <Uk>-а</Uk> or <Uk>-я</Uk> to <Uk>-у</Uk> or <Uk>-ю</Uk>. The masculine ones don't change. Notice that the stress of <Uk>середа́</Uk> moves: <Uk>у се́реду</Uk>.

The table shows <Uk>у</Uk> because each phrase starts on its own, before a consonant. In a sentence, <Uk>у</Uk> often becomes <Uk>в</Uk>, for example after a vowel [src: pravopys-2026, §23]. [Common prepositions](/grammar/prepositions/) gives the rules.

<ExampleList title="Days in sentences">
  <Example uk="Яки́й сього́дні день?" en="What day is it today?" />
  <Example uk="Сього́дні субо́та." en="Today is Saturday." />
  <Example uk="Я працю́ю в понеді́лок." en="I'm working on Monday." note="в after a vowel, before a consonant." />
  <Example uk="Ми ї́демо в Ки́їв у п’я́тницю." en="We're going to Kyiv on Friday." note="у between consonants." />
  <Example uk="Магази́н не працю́є в неді́лю." en="The shop is closed on Sunday." note="Literally “doesn't work on Sunday”." />
</ExampleList>

<Uk>Учо́ра</Uk> and <Uk>вчо́ра</Uk> are the same word: its first letter alternates like the preposition <Uk>у</Uk>/<Uk>в</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §23]. At the start of a sentence, write <Uk>учо́ра</Uk>; after a vowel, <Uk>вчо́ра</Uk>.

The verb *to be* is left out in the present, but not in the past or the future. In the past it agrees with the noun in gender: <Uk>була́</Uk> for a feminine day, <Uk>був</Uk> for a masculine one. [Past tense](/grammar/past-tense/) and [Future tense](/grammar/future-tense/) explain these forms.

<ExampleList title="Yesterday, today, tomorrow">
  <Example uk="Учо́ра була́ п’я́тниця." en="Yesterday was Friday." note="п’я́тниця is feminine: була́." />
  <Example uk="Сього́дні субо́та." en="Today is Saturday." note="No verb in the present." />
  <Example uk="За́втра бу́де неді́ля." en="Tomorrow is Sunday." note="Literally “will be”." />
  <Example uk="Я була́ вчо́ра вдо́ма." en="I was at home yesterday." note="Said by a woman. вчо́ра after a vowel." />
  <Example uk="До за́втра!" en="See you tomorrow!" />
</ExampleList>

## What time is it?

### Котра година?

To ask the time, say <Uk>Котра́ годи́на?</Uk> or <Uk>Котра́ зара́з годи́на?</Uk> (What time is it now?). Literally it means "Which hour is it?", so the answer uses **ordinal numbers**: *first*, *second*, *third* and so on. They are feminine, to agree with <Uk>годи́на</Uk> (hour). In the answer, <Uk>годи́на</Uk> is usually left out [src: goroh-usage].

<ExampleList title="Asking and telling the time">
  <Example uk="Ви́бачте, котра́ годи́на?" en="Excuse me, what time is it?" note="Formal or plural: ви́бачте is the ви form." />
  <Example uk="Пе́рша." en="One o'clock." note="Literally “the first”." />
  <Example uk="Дру́га." en="Two o'clock." />
  <Example uk="Дев’я́та." en="Nine o'clock." />
  <Example uk="Дру́га два́дцять." en="Twenty past two. (2:20)" note="The minutes are an ordinary number: два́дцять." />
</ExampleList>

The hour is an ordinal (*the second*), and the minutes are an ordinary number (*twenty*) [src: ukma-ukrainian-foreign-language, Lesson 9].

<Note type="mistake">
  **Answering with an ordinary number.** The usage guides say <Uk>Котра́ годи́на?</Uk>, not *<Uk>Скі́льки зара́з годи́н?</Uk>*, and the answer <Uk>во́сьма</Uk>, not *<Uk>ві́сім</Uk>* [src: goroh-usage].
</Note>

## At what time?

### О + the hour

To say *at* a time, use <Uk>о</Uk> with the **locative** of the ordinal: <Uk>о дру́гій</Uk> (at two). All the hours end in <Uk>-ій</Uk>. You can add <Uk>годи́ні</Uk>, but it is usually left out [src: ukma-ukrainian-foreign-language, Lesson 9]. Before a vowel, <Uk>о</Uk> becomes <Uk>об</Uk>: <Uk>об одина́дцятій</Uk> (at eleven) [src: goroh-usage]. The question is <Uk>О котрі́й?</Uk> (At what time?).

Notice that *third* has a soft ending: <Uk>тре́тя</Uk>, <Uk>о тре́тій</Uk>.

<ExampleList title="At what time?">
  <Example uk="О котрі́й годи́ні конце́рт?" en="What time is the concert?" />
  <Example uk="Конце́рт о сьо́мій." en="The concert is at seven." />
  <Example uk="Поба́чимося о дру́гій?" en="Shall we meet at two?" note="Literally “Will we see each other at two?”" />
  <Example uk="Уро́к об одина́дцятій." en="The lesson is at eleven." note="об before a vowel." />
  <Example uk="По́тяг о дру́гій три́дцять." en="The train is at half past two. (2:30)" note="Hour, then minutes, as in the answer to Котра́ годи́на?" />
</ExampleList>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Using <Uk>у</Uk> or <Uk>в</Uk> with the hour.** The usage guides give <Uk>о тре́тій</Uk> and <Uk>О котрі́й годи́ні?</Uk>, not *<Uk>в три</Uk>* or *<Uk>в скі́льки годи́н</Uk>* [src: goroh-usage].
</Note>

## Parts of the day

To make clear which seven o'clock you mean, add the part of the day in the **genitive**: <Uk>о сьо́мій ра́нку</Uk> (at seven in the morning), <Uk>о сьо́мій ве́чора</Uk> (at seven in the evening) [src: goroh-usage].

<GrammarTable
  caption="Parts of the day"
  headers={['Part of the day', 'After a time', 'When? (in the …)']}
  rows={[
    ['ра́нок (morning)', 'ра́нку', 'вра́нці, ура́нці'],
    ['день (day, afternoon)', 'дня', 'вдень, уде́нь'],
    ['ве́чір (evening)', 'ве́чора', 'уве́чері'],
    ['ніч (night)', 'но́чі', 'вночі́, уночі́'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[0, 1, 2]}
/>

The words in the last column answer <Uk>коли́?</Uk> (when?). Their first letter alternates between <Uk>у</Uk> and <Uk>в</Uk>, like the preposition. Before another <Uk>в</Uk>, it is always <Uk>у</Uk>: <Uk>уве́чері</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §23].

<ExampleList title="Morning, afternoon, evening, night">
  <Example uk="По́тяг о сьо́мій ра́нку." en="The train is at seven in the morning." />
  <Example uk="Магази́н працю́є до деся́тої ве́чора." en="The shop is open until ten in the evening." note="до + genitive: деся́тої." />
  <Example uk="Сього́дні вра́нці було́ хо́лодно." en="It was cold this morning." note="вра́нці after a vowel." />
  <Example uk="За́втра уве́чері я вдо́ма." en="Tomorrow evening I'm at home." />
</ExampleList>

## Dialogue: making plans

Two friends arrange to meet. They use <Uk>ти</Uk>, the informal *you*.

<ExampleList title="О котрі́й?">
  <Example uk="— Ти ві́льна в субо́ту?" en="“Are you free on Saturday?”" note="Informal: ти, to a woman (ві́льна)." />
  <Example uk="— У субо́ту ні, а в неді́лю так." en="“Not on Saturday, but on Sunday, yes.”" />
  <Example uk="— Поба́чимося в неді́лю? О котрі́й тобі́ зру́чно?" en="“Shall we meet on Sunday? What time suits you?”" note="Informal: тобі́ (to you)." />
  <Example uk="— О дру́гій дня." en="“At two in the afternoon.”" />
  <Example uk="— До́бре. До неді́лі!" en="“OK. See you on Sunday!”" />
</ExampleList>

## Common mistakes and limits

<Note type="mistake">
  **A capital letter for the day.** Days of the week are written with a small letter: <Uk>у понеді́лок</Uk>, not *<Uk>у Понеді́лок</Uk>*.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Forgetting the accusative after <Uk>у</Uk>/<Uk>в</Uk>.** *On Friday* is <Uk>у п’я́тницю</Uk>, not *<Uk>у п’я́тниця</Uk>*.
</Note>

<Note type="remember">
  This page uses the simplest way to tell the time: the hour, then the minutes. Ukrainian also has phrases like *half past* and *a quarter to*, and dates (<Uk>два́дцять четве́ртого се́рпня</Uk>, on the 24th of August) need the genitive. Both come later. The ordinals here are feminine because they agree with <Uk>годи́на</Uk>.
</Note>
