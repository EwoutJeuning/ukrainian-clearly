After this page you will be able to say *please*, *thank you* and *sorry*, choose between <Uk>ти</Uk> and <Uk>ви</Uk> in the phrases you use most, ask for something politely, and keep a conversation going when you don't understand.

## Please: будь ласка

<Uk>Будь ла́ска</Uk> is literally "be [so] kind": <Uk>будь</Uk> is a command form of <Uk>бу́ти</Uk> (to be), and <Uk>ла́ска</Uk> means kindness. It has three everyday uses:

- **please**, with a request;
- **you're welcome**, as a reply to thanks;
- **here you are**, when you hand something to someone.

In a request, it can go at the start, at the end, or after the first word, set off by commas.

<ExampleList title="Будь ла́ска">
  <Example uk="Ка́ву, будь ла́ска." en="A coffee, please." note="ка́ву: accusative of ка́ва. The verb “give” is understood." />
  <Example uk="Скажі́ть, будь ла́ска, де тут апте́ка?" en="Could you tell me where there's a pharmacy here?" note="Formal or plural: ви. Literally “Tell, please, where…”" />
  <Example uk="— Дя́кую! — Будь ла́ска." en="“Thank you!” “You're welcome.”" />
</ExampleList>

## Thank you: дякую + to whom

<Uk>Дя́кую</Uk> is the *I* form of <Uk>дя́кувати</Uk> (to thank), so it literally means "I thank". To say whom you thank, use the **dative** case, the case for "to someone": <Uk>дя́кую вам</Uk>, <Uk>дя́кую тобі́</Uk>. To say what for, add <Uk>за</Uk> + the accusative.

<ExampleList title="Thanking">
  <Example uk="Дя́кую." en="Thank you." />
  <Example uk="Ду́же дя́кую." en="Thank you very much." note="ду́же: very." />
  <Example uk="Дя́кую вам." en="Thank you." note="Formal or plural: ви. вам is the dative of ви." />
  <Example uk="Дя́кую тобі́." en="Thank you." note="Informal: ти. тобі́ is the dative of ти." />
  <Example uk="Дя́кую за допомо́гу." en="Thank you for your help." note="за + accusative: допомо́га → допомо́гу." />
</ExampleList>

## Sorry and excuse me

Ukrainian uses the same words to apologise and to get someone's attention, as English does with *excuse me*.

- <Uk>Ви́бачте</Uk> and <Uk>Проба́чте</Uk> are command forms meaning "forgive [me]". They are the <Uk>ви</Uk> forms; to someone you say <Uk>ти</Uk> to, say <Uk>Ви́бач</Uk> or <Uk>Проба́ч</Uk>.
- <Uk>Перепро́шую</Uk> is the *I* form of <Uk>перепро́шувати</Uk> (to apologise): "I apologise". It does not show <Uk>ти</Uk> or <Uk>ви</Uk>, so you can say it to anyone.

<ExampleList title="Apologising and getting attention">
  <Example uk="Ви́бачте!" en="Sorry! / Excuse me!" note="Formal or plural: ви." />
  <Example uk="Ви́бач, я не знав." en="Sorry, I didn't know." note="Informal: ти. Said by a man; a woman says не зна́ла." />
  <Example uk="Проба́чте, будь ла́ска." en="I'm sorry. / Excuse me, please." note="Formal or plural: ви." />
  <Example uk="Перепро́шую, де тут ви́хід?" en="Excuse me, where is the exit?" note="ви́хід: exit." />
</ExampleList>

## Ти or ви in practice

Many polite phrases show <Uk>ти</Uk> or <Uk>ви</Uk>, either in a pronoun or in the verb ending. The <Uk>ви</Uk> form of a command usually ends in <Uk>-іть</Uk> or <Uk>-те</Uk>, and the <Uk>ти</Uk> form usually ends in <Uk>-и</Uk> or <Uk>-й</Uk>, or has no ending at all. If you are not sure which to use with someone, use <Uk>ви</Uk>. [Pronouns](/grammar/pronouns/) explains who gets which.

Notice that <Uk>будь ла́ска</Uk> stays the same in both columns.

## Polite requests

### Command + будь ласка

The most direct request is a command form with <Uk>будь ла́ска</Uk>, as in the table above. The command form on its own can sound abrupt in English, but with <Uk>будь ла́ска</Uk> it is an ordinary polite request.

### Чи можна + infinitive: may I…?

<Uk>Мо́жна</Uk> means "it is allowed, it is possible". Put the infinitive (the dictionary form of a verb) after it, and start with <Uk>чи</Uk> to make a yes/no question. [Questions](/grammar/questions/) explains <Uk>чи</Uk>. The sentence does not say who will do the action, so it does not show <Uk>ти</Uk> or <Uk>ви</Uk>.

<ExampleList title="Чи мо́жна…?">
  <Example uk="Чи мо́жна тут сі́сти?" en="May I sit here?" note="сі́сти: to sit down." />
  <Example uk="Чи мо́жна заплати́ти ка́рткою?" en="Can I pay by card?" note="ка́рткою: instrumental of ка́ртка, “by means of a card”." />
  <Example uk="Чи мо́жна тут фотографува́ти?" en="Is it allowed to take photos here?" />
</ExampleList>

To say that something is not allowed, use <Uk>не мо́жна</Uk>: <Uk>Тут не мо́жна фотографува́ти.</Uk> (You can't take photos here.) [Negation](/grammar/negation/) explains <Uk>не</Uk>.

### Чи не могли б ви + infinitive: could you…?

For a request to someone you say <Uk>ви</Uk> to, use <Uk>Чи не могли́ б ви</Uk> + infinitive. <Uk>Могли́</Uk> is a past form of <Uk>могти́</Uk> (can, to be able), and <Uk>б</Uk> turns it into *would*: "Could you not…?" As in English *Couldn't you…?*, the <Uk>не</Uk> does not make the request negative.

<ExampleList title="Чи не могли́ б ви…?">
  <Example uk="Чи не могли́ б ви допомогти́?" en="Could you help?" note="Formal or plural: ви." />
  <Example uk="Чи не могли́ б ви повтори́ти?" en="Could you say that again?" note="Formal or plural: ви." />
  <Example uk="Чи не могли́ б ви говори́ти пові́льніше?" en="Could you speak more slowly?" note="Formal or plural: ви. пові́льніше: more slowly." />
</ExampleList>

## When you don't understand

These sentences are worth learning by heart. Most of them use patterns from earlier in this page.

<ExampleList title="Understanding and not understanding">
  <Example uk="Я не розумі́ю." en="I don't understand." />
  <Example uk="Я не зрозумі́в." en="I didn't catch that." note="Said by a man; a woman says не зрозумі́ла. Literally “I didn't understand.”" />
  <Example uk="Повторі́ть, будь ла́ска." en="Say that again, please." note="Formal or plural: ви." />
  <Example uk="Говорі́ть, будь ла́ска, пові́льніше." en="Please speak more slowly." note="Formal or plural: ви." />
  <Example uk="Що означа́є це сло́во?" en="What does this word mean?" />
  <Example uk="Як це украї́нською?" en="How do you say this in Ukrainian?" note="Literally “How [is] this in Ukrainian?”" />
  <Example uk="Я тро́хи розмовля́ю украї́нською." en="I speak a little Ukrainian." />
  <Example uk="Ви розмовля́єте англі́йською?" en="Do you speak English?" note="Formal or plural: ви." />
</ExampleList>

<Uk>Украї́нською</Uk> and <Uk>англі́йською</Uk> are short for <Uk>украї́нською мо́вою</Uk>, <Uk>англі́йською мо́вою</Uk>: the instrumental case, "by means of the Ukrainian language".

## Dialogue

A visitor in a café points at a word on the menu. The visitor and the waiter say <Uk>ви</Uk> to each other.

<ExampleList title="In a café (ви)">
  <Example uk="— Ви́бачте, що означа́є це сло́во?" en="“Excuse me, what does this word mean?”" note="Formal: ви, to one person." />
  <Example uk="— «Узва́р»? Це напі́й із сухофру́ктів." en="“Uzvar? It's a drink made from dried fruit.”" />
  <Example uk="— Ви́бачте, я не зрозумі́ла. Повторі́ть, будь ла́ска, пові́льніше." en="“Sorry, I didn't catch that. Please say it again, more slowly.”" note="Formal: ви, to one person. Said by a woman." />
  <Example uk="— Це напі́й. Із сухофру́ктів." en="“It's a drink. From dried fruit.”" />
  <Example uk="— А, зрозумі́ло. Узва́р, будь ла́ска." en="“Ah, I see. An uzvar, please.”" />

  <Example uk="— Дя́кую!" en="“Thank you!”" />

## Common mistakes and limits

<Note type="mistake">
  **Saying *<Uk>я вибача́юся</Uk>*.** The ending <Uk>-ся</Uk> means "oneself", so this literally says "I forgive myself". Usage guides recommend <Uk>ви́бачте</Uk>, <Uk>проба́чте</Uk> or <Uk>перепро́шую</Uk> [src: goroh-usage].
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **The wrong case after <Uk>дя́кую</Uk>.** <Uk>Дя́кувати</Uk> takes the dative: <Uk>дя́кую тобі́</Uk>, <Uk>дя́кую вам</Uk>, not *<Uk>дя́кую тебе́</Uk>* or *<Uk>дя́кую вас</Uk>* [src: goroh-usage].
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Saying *<Uk>я говорю́ по-українськи</Uk>*.** Usage guides recommend <Uk>я розмовля́ю украї́нською (мо́вою)</Uk> [src: goroh-usage].
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Saying *<Uk>вели́ке дя́кую</Uk>* for "thank you very much".** Usage guides advise against it [src: goroh-usage]. Say <Uk>ду́же дя́кую</Uk>.
</Note>

<Note type="remember">
  This page uses <Uk>ви</Uk> for strangers and <Uk>ти</Uk> for friends. When to move from <Uk>ви</Uk> to <Uk>ти</Uk> with someone depends on the people and the situation, so follow the other person's lead. Asking for the things you need comes next, in [Everyday needs](/sentences/everyday-needs/).
</Note>
