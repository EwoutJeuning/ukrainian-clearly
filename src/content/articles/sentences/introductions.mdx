After this page you will be able to introduce yourself in Ukrainian: say your name and ask for someone else's, say where you are from and where you live, say what you do, and say that you are pleased to meet someone.

Each section gives a pattern with a slot for your own words, such as <Uk>Мене́ звуть</Uk> + name. Learn the pattern and you can fill the slot with anything that fits.

## Your name

### Мене звуть + name

The usual way to give your name is <Uk>Мене́ звуть</Uk> + your name. Literally it means "They call me…".

- <Uk>звуть</Uk> is the *they* form of the verb <Uk>зва́ти</Uk> (to call).
- <Uk>мене́</Uk> is a case form of <Uk>я</Uk>: *me*, the person being called. Because the verb has no subject, the sentence simply means "I'm called…".
- The name is usually in its dictionary form: <Uk>Мене́ звуть Оле́на.</Uk>

To ask someone's name, put <Uk>як</Uk> (how) in front and replace <Uk>мене́</Uk> with the right form of *you*: <Uk>тебе́</Uk> for <Uk>ти</Uk>, <Uk>вас</Uk> for <Uk>ви</Uk>. You will also hear the question with the infinitive <Uk>зва́ти</Uk>: <Uk>Як вас зва́ти?</Uk> It means the same. [Pronouns](/grammar/pronouns/) and [Questions](/grammar/questions/) show both versions.

To ask about someone else, use the form of <Uk>він</Uk> or <Uk>вона́</Uk> that goes in the same slot: <Uk>його́</Uk> (him) or <Uk>її́</Uk> (her).

<ExampleList title="Мене́ звуть…">
  <Example uk="Мене́ звуть Оле́на." en="My name is Olena." note="Literally “They call me Olena.”" />
  <Example uk="Мене́ звуть Тара́с." en="My name is Taras." />
  <Example uk="Як тебе́ звуть?" en="What's your name?" note="Informal: ти." />
  <Example uk="Як вас звуть?" en="What's your name?" note="Formal or plural: ви." />
  <Example uk="Як його́ звуть?" en="What's his name?" />
  <Example uk="Як її́ звуть?" en="What's her name?" />
</ExampleList>

### Я + name, моє ім’я + name

Two shorter patterns also work. <Uk>Я Оле́на.</Uk> is literally "I [am] Olena." Ukrainian leaves out *am* in the present tense, as [Sentence structure](/grammar/sentence-structure/) explains. <Uk>Моє́ ім’я́ — Оле́на.</Uk> means "My name is Olena." Here the dash stands where English has *is*, because both sides are nouns.

<Uk>Ім’я́</Uk> (name) is neuter, so *my* is <Uk>моє́</Uk>, not <Uk>мій</Uk>. <Uk>Ім’я́</Uk> means a first name. A surname is <Uk>прі́звище</Uk>.

<ExampleList title="Other ways to give your name">
  <Example uk="Я Андрі́й." en="I'm Andrii." />
  <Example uk="Моє́ ім’я́ — Оле́на." en="My name is Olena." />
  <Example uk="Моє́ прі́звище — Шевче́нко." en="My surname is Shevchenko." />
</ExampleList>

## Nice to meet you

<Uk>Приє́мно</Uk> means "[it is] pleasant". Ukrainian needs no *it is* here: the word alone makes a sentence. Add <Uk>ду́же</Uk> (very) to make it stronger, or add <Uk>познайо́митися</Uk> (to get acquainted) to say exactly what is pleasant.

To answer, say <Uk>Мені́ теж.</Uk> (Me too.) <Uk>Мені́</Uk> is the dative case of <Uk>я</Uk>, "to me": literally "To me too [it is pleasant]". You can also say <Uk>Взає́мно.</Uk> (Likewise.)

<ExampleList title="Nice to meet you">
  <Example uk="Ду́же приє́мно." en="Very nice to meet you." note="Literally “Very pleasant.”" />
  <Example uk="Приє́мно познайо́митися." en="Nice to meet you." note="Literally “Pleasant to get acquainted.”" />
  <Example uk="Мені́ теж." en="Me too." />
  <Example uk="Взає́мно." en="Likewise." />
</ExampleList>

## Where you are from: з + country or city

To say where you come from, use <Uk>з</Uk> (from) with the place name in the **genitive** case. The genitive ending often replaces the last vowel: <Uk>Кана́да</Uk> → <Uk>з Кана́ди</Uk>. [Common prepositions](/grammar/prepositions/) has more on <Uk>з</Uk> and the genitive.

The question is <Uk>Зві́дки</Uk> (where from) + the person: <Uk>Зві́дки ти?</Uk>, <Uk>Зві́дки ви?</Uk>

<ExampleList title="Я з…">
  <Example uk="Зві́дки ви?" en="Where are you from?" note="Formal or plural: ви." />
  <Example uk="Зві́дки ти?" en="Where are you from?" note="Informal: ти." />
  <Example uk="Я з Кана́ди." en="I'm from Canada." note="Кана́да → з Кана́ди." />
  <Example uk="Я з А́нглії." en="I'm from England." note="А́нглія → з А́нглії." />
  <Example uk="Я з Австра́лії." en="I'm from Australia." note="Австра́лія → з Австра́лії." />
  <Example uk="Я з Ло́ндона." en="I'm from London." note="Ло́ндон → з Ло́ндона." />
  <Example uk="Я зі Льво́ва." en="I'm from Lviv." note="Львів → зі Льво́ва." />
</ExampleList>

The word for *from* has three forms: <Uk>з</Uk>, <Uk>із</Uk> and <Uk>зі</Uk>. Before a vowel and before most single consonants it is <Uk>з</Uk>. Before some groups of consonants, such as the <Uk>льв</Uk> in <Uk>Льво́ва</Uk>, it is <Uk>зі</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §25]. For the place names on this page, <Uk>з</Uk> is what you need, except for <Uk>зі Льво́ва</Uk>.

## Where you live: живу в / у + place

<Uk>Я живу́</Uk> means "I live". The place takes <Uk>в</Uk> or <Uk>у</Uk> (in) and the **locative** case, which often ends in <Uk>-і</Uk>: <Uk>Ки́їв</Uk> → <Uk>у Ки́єві</Uk>, <Uk>Ло́ндон</Uk> → <Uk>у Ло́ндоні</Uk>.

The question is <Uk>Де</Uk> (where) + the verb: <Uk>Де ти живе́ш?</Uk>, <Uk>Де ви живете́?</Uk>

<ExampleList title="Я живу́ в…">
  <Example uk="Де ви живете́?" en="Where do you live?" note="Formal or plural: ви." />
  <Example uk="Я живу́ в Ки́єві." en="I live in Kyiv." note="Ки́їв → в Ки́єві." />
  <Example uk="Я живу́ в Ло́ндоні." en="I live in London." />
  <Example uk="А я живу́ у Льво́ві." en="And I live in Lviv." note="у before the в sound at the start of Льво́ві." />
</ExampleList>

As with <Uk>з</Uk>, the choice between <Uk>у</Uk> and <Uk>в</Uk> is about easy pronunciation. After a vowel and before a single consonant it is usually <Uk>в</Uk>; before <Uk>в</Uk>, <Uk>ф</Uk> and groups like <Uk>льв</Uk> it is <Uk>у</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §23]. Both mean the same.

## What you do

### Я + job

The simplest way is <Uk>Я</Uk> + the noun, again with no word for *am*. Many job words have a masculine and a feminine form, so choose the one that matches you.

<ExampleList title="Я студе́нт, я студе́нтка">
  <Example uk="Я студе́нт." en="I'm a student." note="Said by a man." />
  <Example uk="Я студе́нтка." en="I'm a student." note="Said by a woman." />
  <Example uk="Я вчи́тель." en="I'm a teacher." note="Said by a man." />
  <Example uk="Я вчи́телька." en="I'm a teacher." note="Said by a woman." />
  <Example uk="Я лі́кар." en="I'm a doctor." note="A woman can also say лі́карка." />
</ExampleList>

### Я працюю + job in the instrumental

You can also say <Uk>Я працю́ю</Uk> (I work) + the job in the **instrumental** case, "I work as…". For masculine nouns the ending is often <Uk>-ом</Uk> or <Uk>-ем</Uk>. The question uses the instrumental of <Uk>хто</Uk>: <Uk>Ким ви працю́єте?</Uk> (What do you do?, literally "As whom do you work?")

<ExampleList title="Я працю́ю…">
  <Example uk="Ким ви працю́єте?" en="What do you do?" note="Formal or plural: ви." />
  <Example uk="Я працю́ю лі́карем." en="I work as a doctor." note="лі́кар → лі́карем." />
  <Example uk="Я працю́ю вчи́телем." en="I work as a teacher." note="Said by a man. вчи́тель → вчи́телем." />
  <Example uk="Я працю́ю в о́фісі." en="I work in an office." note="в + locative: a place, not a job." />
</ExampleList>

### Learning Ukrainian

Two sentences you will use a lot as a learner. <Uk>Вивча́ти</Uk> (to study, to learn) takes the accusative: <Uk>украї́нську</Uk>, short for <Uk>украї́нську мо́ву</Uk> (the Ukrainian language). <Uk>Розмовля́ти</Uk> (to speak, to talk) takes the instrumental: <Uk>украї́нською</Uk>, "in Ukrainian".

<ExampleList title="Talking about your Ukrainian">
  <Example uk="Я вивча́ю украї́нську мо́ву." en="I'm learning Ukrainian." />
  <Example uk="Я тро́хи розмовля́ю украї́нською." en="I speak a little Ukrainian." />
  <Example uk="Ви розмовля́єте англі́йською?" en="Do you speak English?" note="Formal or plural: ви." />
</ExampleList>

## Dialogue

Two people meet on a language course. They are about the same age and use <Uk>ти</Uk> from the start.

<ExampleList title="On a course (ти)">
  <Example uk="— Приві́т! Я Тара́с. А як тебе́ звуть?" en="“Hi! I'm Taras. And what's your name?”" note="Informal: ти." />
  <Example uk="— Мене́ звуть Кейт. Ду́же приє́мно." en="“My name is Kate. Nice to meet you.”" />
  <Example uk="— Мені́ теж. Зві́дки ти?" en="“Me too. Where are you from?”" note="Informal: ти." />
  <Example uk="— Я з А́нглії, з Ло́ндона. А ти?" en="“I'm from England, from London. And you?”" note="Informal: ти." />
  <Example uk="— Я зі Льво́ва, але́ живу́ в Ки́єві. Ти студе́нтка?" en="“I'm from Lviv, but I live in Kyiv. Are you a student?”" note="Informal: ти." />
  <Example uk="— Ні, я працю́ю вчи́телькою. А украї́нську мо́ву вивча́ю тут." en="“No, I work as a teacher. And I'm learning Ukrainian here.”" />
</ExampleList>

## Common mistakes and limits

<Note type="mistake">
  **Adding a word for *is*.** Say <Uk>Я Оле́на</Uk> or <Uk>Моє́ ім’я́ — Оле́на</Uk>, not *<Uk>Моє́ ім’я́ є Оле́на</Uk>*. In sentences like these, Ukrainian leaves the verb out.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Forgetting the genitive after <Uk>з</Uk>.** Say <Uk>Я з Кана́ди</Uk>, not *<Uk>Я з Кана́да</Uk>*.
</Note>

<Note type="remember">
  Feminine forms of job words are not always made the same way, and some job words have no common feminine form. Learn each one as you need it, and check it in a dictionary. Being polite in a conversation comes next, in [Politeness](/sentences/politeness/).
</Note>
