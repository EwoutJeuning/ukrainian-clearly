After this page you will be able to say hello and goodbye in Ukrainian, choose a greeting that fits the time of day and the person, ask someone how they are, and answer when someone asks you.

Most greetings are short fixed phrases, but they are built from patterns you will meet again. Each section below shows the pattern first, then the phrases.

## Hello: one informal word, and "good" + time of day

### Привіт: hi

<Uk>Приві́т</Uk> means *hi*. It is informal: use it with friends, family, children, and other people you say <Uk>ти</Uk> to. [Pronouns](/grammar/pronouns/) explains the difference between <Uk>ти</Uk> and <Uk>ви</Uk>.

### Добрий + time of day

With everyone else, the safe choice is "good" plus the time of day. The pattern is <Uk>до́брий</Uk> + a masculine noun, so the adjective has its masculine ending <Uk>-ий</Uk>. [Adjectives and agreement](/grammar/adjectives-and-agreement/) explains this matching.

These greetings do not show <Uk>ти</Uk> or <Uk>ви</Uk>, so they suit anyone: a neighbour, a shop assistant, a teacher or a friend. If you are not sure which greeting to use, <Uk>До́брий день!</Uk> is a good default during the day.

You will also hear the same nouns in another case: <Uk>До́брого ра́нку!</Uk>, <Uk>До́брого дня!</Uk>, <Uk>До́брого ве́чора!</Uk> These are genitive forms, the case used for wishing someone something. They mean the same as the greetings in the table.

### Вітаю: I greet you

<Uk>Віта́ю!</Uk> is the *I* form of the verb <Uk>віта́ти</Uk> (to greet), so it literally means "I greet [you]". Like <Uk>до́брий день</Uk>, it does not show <Uk>ти</Uk> or <Uk>ви</Uk>. You may also see it at the start of emails and messages.

  <Example uk="Приві́т!" en="Hi!" note="Informal: for people you say ти to." />
  <Example uk="До́брий ра́нок!" en="Good morning!" />
  <Example uk="До́брий день!" en="Hello! / Good afternoon!" note="Literally “good day”." />
  <Example uk="До́брий ве́чір!" en="Good evening!" />
  <Example uk="Віта́ю!" en="Hello! / Greetings!" note="Literally “I greet [you]”." />
</ExampleList>

## Goodbye: до + "until when"

Several goodbyes use the preposition <Uk>до</Uk> (until, up to) with a noun or adverb. <Uk>До</Uk> takes the genitive case, as [Common prepositions](/grammar/prepositions/) shows.

<ExampleList title="Goodbyes with до">
  <Example uk="До поба́чення!" en="Goodbye!" note="Literally “until seeing [each other again]”. Suits anyone." />
  <Example uk="До зу́стрічі!" en="See you!" note="Literally “until the meeting”. зу́стрічі is the genitive of зу́стріч." />
  <Example uk="До за́втра!" en="See you tomorrow!" note="за́втра: tomorrow." />
</ExampleList>

Other goodbyes are verbs, and the verb shows whether you are saying <Uk>ти</Uk> or <Uk>ви</Uk>. <Uk>Бува́й</Uk> is said to one person you say <Uk>ти</Uk> to. <Uk>Бува́йте</Uk> is the <Uk>ви</Uk> form.

<ExampleList title="Goodbyes that show ти or ви">
  <Example uk="Бува́й!" en="Bye!" note="Informal: ти." />
  <Example uk="Бува́йте!" en="Bye!" note="Formal or plural: ви." />
</ExampleList>

Late in the evening, when someone is going to bed, say <Uk>На добра́ніч!</Uk> (Good night!). It is a goodbye, not a greeting: when you meet someone in the evening, say <Uk>До́брий ве́чір!</Uk>

## How are you?

### Як справи?

The most useful question is <Uk>Як спра́ви?</Uk>, literally "How are things?" (<Uk>спра́ви</Uk> is the plural of <Uk>спра́ва</Uk>, a matter or affair.) On its own it does not show <Uk>ти</Uk> or <Uk>ви</Uk>. To make it personal, add *your* or *with you*:

- <Uk>Як твої́ спра́ви?</Uk> or <Uk>Як у те́бе спра́ви?</Uk> to someone you say <Uk>ти</Uk> to.
- <Uk>Як ва́ші спра́ви?</Uk> or <Uk>Як у вас спра́ви?</Uk> to someone you say <Uk>ви</Uk> to.

<Uk>У те́бе</Uk> and <Uk>у вас</Uk> are the same "at you" pattern as in <Uk>у ме́не є</Uk> (I have). See [Everyday needs](/sentences/everyday-needs/).

### Як ся маєш?

Another common question uses the verb <Uk>ма́ти</Uk> (to have) with the small word <Uk>ся</Uk> (oneself): <Uk>Як ся ма́єш?</Uk> and <Uk>Як ся ма́єте?</Uk> mean "How are you?", literally "How do you have yourself?" The verb ending shows <Uk>ти</Uk> (<Uk>ма́єш</Uk>) or <Uk>ви</Uk> (<Uk>ма́єте</Uk>). Usage guides list both <Uk>Як ва́ші (твої́) спра́ви?</Uk> and <Uk>Як ся ма́єте (ма́єш)?</Uk> as good Ukrainian [src: goroh-usage].

With friends you can also just say <Uk>Як ти?</Uk> (How are you?).

<ExampleList title="Asking how someone is">
  <Example uk="Як спра́ви?" en="How are things? / How are you?" />
  <Example uk="Як у те́бе спра́ви?" en="How are you doing?" note="Informal: ти. Literally “How are things with you?”" />
  <Example uk="Як ва́ші спра́ви?" en="How are you doing?" note="Formal or plural: ви." />
  <Example uk="Як ся ма́єш?" en="How are you?" note="Informal: ти." />
  <Example uk="Як ся ма́єте?" en="How are you?" note="Formal or plural: ви." />
  <Example uk="Як ти?" en="How are you?" note="Informal: ти." />
</ExampleList>

### Answering

Answers are usually an adverb, often followed by <Uk>дя́кую</Uk> (thank you). To ask back, use <Uk>А</Uk> (and, but) with the pronoun: <Uk>А ти?</Uk>, <Uk>А ви?</Uk>, or with the "at you" form: <Uk>А в те́бе?</Uk>, <Uk>А у вас?</Uk>

<ExampleList title="Replies, from good to bad">
  <Example uk="Чудо́во!" en="Great!" />
  <Example uk="До́бре, дя́кую." en="Fine, thanks." />
  <Example uk="Непога́но." en="Not bad." />
  <Example uk="Норма́льно." en="OK." />
  <Example uk="Так собі́." en="So-so." />
  <Example uk="Пога́но." en="Bad. / Not good." />
</ExampleList>

<ExampleList title="Asking back">
  <Example uk="До́бре, дя́кую. А ти?" en="Fine, thanks. And you?" note="Informal: ти." />
  <Example uk="Непога́но. А в те́бе?" en="Not bad. And you?" note="Informal: ти. Literally “And with you?”" />
  <Example uk="Дя́кую, до́бре. А у вас?" en="Fine, thank you. And you?" note="Formal or plural: ви." />
</ExampleList>

The small word before <Uk>те́бе</Uk> and <Uk>вас</Uk> changes between <Uk>у</Uk> and <Uk>в</Uk> for easier pronunciation. After a vowel and before a consonant it is usually <Uk>в</Uk> (<Uk>А в те́бе?</Uk>). Before <Uk>в</Uk> it is <Uk>у</Uk> (<Uk>А у вас?</Uk>) [src: pravopys-2026, §23].

## Common mistakes and limits

<Note type="mistake">
  **Saying *<Uk>Як ді́ла?</Uk>*** This copies the Russian question. Usage guides recommend <Uk>Як ва́ші спра́ви?</Uk>, <Uk>Як твої́ спра́ви?</Uk> or <Uk>Як ся ма́єте?</Uk> instead [src: goroh-usage].
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Using <Uk>Приві́т</Uk> or <Uk>Бува́й</Uk> with strangers.** These go with <Uk>ти</Uk>. With people you say <Uk>ви</Uk> to, use <Uk>До́брий день!</Uk> and <Uk>До поба́чення!</Uk>
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Greeting someone with <Uk>На добра́ніч</Uk>.** It is only for saying goodnight when someone goes to bed. In the evening, say hello with <Uk>До́брий ве́чір!</Uk>
</Note>

<Note type="remember">
  The greetings with <Uk>до́брий</Uk>, the goodbyes with <Uk>до</Uk> and the question <Uk>Як спра́ви?</Uk> work with everyone. Only the words that show <Uk>ти</Uk> or <Uk>ви</Uk> need a choice. Introducing yourself comes next, in [Introductions](/sentences/introductions/).
</Note>
