After this page you will be able to say what you want and what you need, say what you have and don't have, ask someone whether they have something, and ask for water, the toilet or help.

There are four patterns on this page. Each one controls the case of the noun that follows it, so the notes on the examples point out the case.

## I want: хочу

<Uk>Хо́чу</Uk> is the *I* form of <Uk>хоті́ти</Uk> (to want). After it you can put either a verb in the infinitive or a noun in the **accusative**.

  caption="хоті́ти (to want) in the present tense"
  headers={['Person', 'Form']}
  rows={[
    ['я', 'хо́чу'],
    ['ти', 'хо́чеш'],
    ['він, вона́', 'хо́че'],
    ['ми', 'хо́чемо'],
    ['ви', 'хо́чете'],
    ['вони́', 'хо́чуть'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[0, 1]}
/>

<ExampleList title="Хо́чу + infinitive or noun">
  <Example uk="Я хо́чу пи́ти." en="I'm thirsty." note="Literally “I want to drink.”" />
  <Example uk="Я хо́чу ї́сти." en="I'm hungry." note="Literally “I want to eat.”" />
  <Example uk="Я хо́чу спа́ти." en="I'm sleepy." note="Literally “I want to sleep.”" />
  <Example uk="Я хо́чу ка́ву." en="I want a coffee." note="ка́ву: accusative of ка́ва." />
  <Example uk="Що ти хо́чеш?" en="What do you want?" note="Informal: ти." />
  <Example uk="Ви хо́чете чай?" en="Would you like some tea?" note="Formal or plural: ви. чай looks the same in the accusative." />
</ExampleList>

### I would like: хотів би, хотіла б

To make a wish softer, use the past form of <Uk>хоті́ти</Uk> with the small word <Uk>би</Uk> or <Uk>б</Uk> (would). The past form shows the speaker's gender: a man says <Uk>хоті́в</Uk>, a woman says <Uk>хоті́ла</Uk>. The form is <Uk>би</Uk> after a consonant and usually <Uk>б</Uk> after a vowel.

<ExampleList title="Я хоті́в би…">
  <Example uk="Я хоті́в би ка́ву." en="I'd like a coffee." note="Said by a man." />
  <Example uk="Я хоті́ла б ка́ву." en="I'd like a coffee." note="Said by a woman." />
</ExampleList>

## I need: мені треба, мені потрібен

In these patterns the person who needs something is not the subject. It goes into the **dative** case, "to me": <Uk>мені́</Uk> (to me), <Uk>тобі́</Uk> (to you, informal), <Uk>вам</Uk> (to you, formal or plural). Literally, Ukrainian says "To me it is necessary".

### Мені треба + infinitive

<Uk>Тре́ба</Uk> and <Uk>потрі́бно</Uk> both mean "it is necessary". They never change their form. Put an infinitive after them to say what you need to do.

<ExampleList title="Мені́ тре́ба…">
  <Example uk="Мені́ тре́ба йти." en="I have to go." />
  <Example uk="Мені́ тре́ба подзвони́ти." en="I need to make a phone call." />
  <Example uk="Мені́ потрі́бно відпочи́ти." en="I need to rest." />
  <Example uk="Тобі́ тре́ба відпочи́ти." en="You need to rest." note="Informal: ти." />
</ExampleList>

### Мені потрібен + noun

To say that you need a thing, use <Uk>потрі́бен</Uk> (needed). It is an adjective, so it agrees with the noun, and the noun stays in the **nominative**. [Adjectives and agreement](/grammar/adjectives-and-agreement/) explains this matching.

<GrammarTable
  caption="потрі́бен agrees with the thing needed"
  headers={['Gender of the noun', 'Form', 'Example']}

    ['Masculine', 'потрі́бен', 'Мені́ потрі́бен лі́кар.'],
    ['Feminine', 'потрі́бна', 'Мені́ потрі́бна допомо́га.'],

The examples mean: *I need a doctor. I need help. I need a taxi. I need money.* (<Uk>Гро́ші</Uk>, money, is always plural.)

## I have, I don't have: у мене є, у мене немає

The everyday way to say "I have" is <Uk>у ме́не є</Uk>, literally "at me there is". The thing you have is the subject, so it is in the **nominative**. To say "I don't have", replace <Uk>є</Uk> with <Uk>нема́є</Uk>, and the thing goes into the **genitive**. [Negation](/grammar/negation/) explains this pattern.

Change <Uk>ме́не</Uk> to talk about other people: <Uk>у те́бе</Uk> (you, informal), <Uk>у вас</Uk> (you, formal or plural), <Uk>у ньо́го</Uk> (him), <Uk>у не́ї</Uk> (her). To ask whether someone has something, use the same words with a question mark.

<ExampleList title="У ме́не є, у ме́не нема́є">
  <Example uk="У ме́не є квито́к." en="I have a ticket." note="квито́к: nominative." />
  <Example uk="У ме́не нема́є квитка́." en="I don't have a ticket." note="квитка́: genitive." />
  <Example uk="У ме́не нема́є готі́вки." en="I don't have any cash." note="готі́вка → готі́вки: genitive." />
  <Example uk="У вас є вода́?" en="Do you have any water?" note="Formal or plural: ви." />
  <Example uk="У те́бе є ру́чка?" en="Have you got a pen?" note="Informal: ти." />
</ExampleList>

<Uk>Я ма́ю</Uk> (I have), from <Uk>ма́ти</Uk>, is also correct: <Uk>Я ма́ю квито́к.</Uk> The negative is <Uk>Я не ма́ю</Uk> + genitive. [Pronouns](/grammar/pronouns/) mentions both.

## Asking for things

### Дайте, будь ласка + accusative

<Uk>Да́йте</Uk> is the <Uk>ви</Uk> command form of <Uk>да́ти</Uk> (to give). The thing you ask for is in the accusative. If you want *a bottle of* or *a glass of* something, the second noun is in the genitive, like English *of*.

### Можна + noun?

You can also ask with <Uk>Мо́жна</Uk> (may I have…?) and the noun in the accusative. This does not show <Uk>ти</Uk> or <Uk>ви</Uk>. [Politeness](/sentences/politeness/) has more polite request patterns.

<ExampleList title="Asking for things">
  <Example uk="Да́йте, будь ла́ска, пля́шку води́." en="A bottle of water, please." note="Formal or plural: ви. пля́шку: accusative; води́: genitive, “of water”." />
  <Example uk="Мо́жна меню́, будь ла́ска?" en="Could I have the menu, please?" note="меню́ never changes its form." />
  <Example uk="Мо́жна раху́нок, будь ла́ска?" en="Could I have the bill, please?" />
</ExampleList>

## Toilet, water, help

### The toilet

Ask <Uk>Де туале́т?</Uk> (Where is the toilet?). With <Uk>ви́бачте</Uk> and <Uk>тут</Uk> (here) it becomes a polite question to a stranger. <Uk>Чи є тут…?</Uk> asks whether there is one at all.

<ExampleList title="Finding the toilet">
  <Example uk="Ви́бачте, де тут туале́т?" en="Excuse me, where's the toilet here?" note="Formal or plural: ви (in ви́бачте)." />
  <Example uk="Чи є тут туале́т?" en="Is there a toilet here?" />
</ExampleList>

### Water

<ExampleList title="Asking for water">
  <Example uk="Я хо́чу пи́ти." en="I'm thirsty." />
  <Example uk="У вас є вода́?" en="Do you have any water?" note="Formal or plural: ви." />
  <Example uk="Да́йте, будь ла́ска, пля́шку води́." en="A bottle of water, please." note="Formal or plural: ви." />
</ExampleList>

### Help

<Uk>Допоможі́ть</Uk> is the <Uk>ви</Uk> command form of <Uk>допомогти́</Uk> (to help). Shouted on its own, it means "Help!". To ask one person you say <Uk>ти</Uk> to, the form is <Uk>Допоможи́</Uk>.

<ExampleList title="Asking for help">
  <Example uk="Допоможі́ть!" en="Help!" note="Formal or plural: ви." />
  <Example uk="Допоможі́ть, будь ла́ска." en="Please help me." note="Formal or plural: ви." />
  <Example uk="Допоможи́ мені́, будь ла́ска." en="Please help me." note="Informal: ти. мені́: dative, “to me”." />
  <Example uk="Мені́ потрі́бна допомо́га." en="I need help." />
  <Example uk="Вам потрі́бна допомо́га?" en="Do you need help?" note="Formal or plural: ви. вам: dative of ви." />
  <Example uk="Мені́ пога́но." en="I feel unwell." note="Literally “To me [it is] bad.”" />
  <Example uk="Мені́ потрі́бен лі́кар." en="I need a doctor." />
</ExampleList>

## Dialogue

A guest arrives at a friend's flat after a long journey. They say <Uk>ти</Uk> to each other.

<ExampleList title="At a friend's flat (ти)">
  <Example uk="— Захо́дь! Тобі́ щось тре́ба?" en="“Come in! Do you need anything?”" note="Informal: ти." />
  <Example uk="— Так, я ду́же хо́чу пи́ти. У те́бе є вода́?" en="“Yes, I'm very thirsty. Have you got any water?”" note="Informal: ти." />
  <Example uk="— Є. Ось, будь ла́ска." en="“Yes. Here you are.”" />
  <Example uk="— Дя́кую! А де туале́т?" en="“Thanks! And where's the toilet?”" />
  <Example uk="— Он там." en="“Over there.”" />
  <Example uk="— І ще одне́: мені́ потрі́бен рушни́к. У ме́не нема́є рушника́." en="“And one more thing: I need a towel. I haven't got one.”" note="рушни́к: nominative after потрі́бен; рушника́: genitive after нема́є." />
  <Example uk="— Звича́йно. Ось рушни́к." en="“Of course. Here's a towel.”" />
</ExampleList>

## Common mistakes and limits

<Note type="mistake">
  **The nominative after <Uk>нема́є</Uk>.** Say <Uk>У ме́не нема́є квитка́</Uk>, not *<Uk>У ме́не нема́є квито́к</Uk>*. What you don't have goes into the genitive.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Making the person the subject with <Uk>тре́ба</Uk>.** Say <Uk>Мені́ тре́ба йти</Uk>, not *<Uk>Я тре́ба йти</Uk>*. The person who needs something is in the dative.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Forgetting agreement with <Uk>потрі́бен</Uk>.** It matches the thing needed: <Uk>потрі́бен лі́кар</Uk>, <Uk>потрі́бна допомо́га</Uk>, <Uk>потрі́бні гро́ші</Uk>.
</Note>

<Note type="remember">
  <Uk>Тре́ба</Uk> and <Uk>потрі́бно</Uk> can also be followed by a noun, and then the case of the noun depends on the sentence. Until you meet that pattern, use <Uk>тре́ба</Uk> with an infinitive and <Uk>потрі́бен</Uk> with a noun.
</Note>
