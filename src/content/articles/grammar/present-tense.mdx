After this page you will be able to put a Ukrainian verb into the present tense. You will know the two groups of endings and how to tell which group a verb belongs to. You will have seen three common verbs in full, and you will know that the stress can move from one form to another.

## One tense for "I read" and "I am reading"

Ukrainian has one present tense where English has two. <Uk>Я чита́ю</Uk> can mean "I read" or "I am reading", and the context decides.

The verb changes its ending for each person: *I*, *you* (informal), *he/she/it*, *we*, *you* (formal or plural) and *they*. That makes six forms. Changing a verb's endings like this is called **[conjugation](/glossary/#conjugation)**. In English only *he/she/it* gets a different ending (*reads*), so Ukrainian needs more endings to learn. (A rough comparison.)

To make the present tense, take the dictionary form (the infinitive, which ends in <Uk>-ти</Uk>), remove the ending and add the present-tense endings. Which endings you add depends on the verb's group.

## Two groups of endings

Ukrainian verbs fall into two groups, called the **first** and **second conjugation**. The two groups share the *I* ending, <Uk>-у</Uk> or <Uk>-ю</Uk>. The other endings have a different vowel:

- **First group:** <Uk>е</Uk> or <Uk>є</Uk> in the endings (<Uk>чита́єш</Uk>, <Uk>пи́шеш</Uk>), and <Uk>-уть</Uk> or <Uk>-ють</Uk> for *they*.
- **Second group:** <Uk>и</Uk> or <Uk>ї</Uk> in the endings (<Uk>гово́риш</Uk>), and <Uk>-ать</Uk> or <Uk>-ять</Uk> for *they*.

The two spellings of each ending (<Uk>-у</Uk> or <Uk>-ю</Uk>, <Uk>-еш</Uk> or <Uk>-єш</Uk>) are really one ending. The second spelling is used after a vowel or a soft consonant.

### Which group is a verb in?

The infinitive does not always tell you. Many verbs in <Uk>-ати</Uk> are in the first group and many in <Uk>-ити</Uk> are in the second, but there are exceptions in both directions. The safest guide is the *you* (<Uk>ти</Uk>) form: <Uk>-еш</Uk> or <Uk>-єш</Uk> means the first group, and <Uk>-иш</Uk> or <Uk>-їш</Uk> the second.

Dictionaries tell you the group. On <Uk>Горох</Uk>, the inflection page of an imperfective verb says <Uk>1 дієвідмі́на</Uk> (first conjugation) or <Uk>2 дієвідмі́на</Uk> (second conjugation), and lists all the present-tense forms with their stress.

## First group: "to read"

<Uk>Чита́ти</Uk> is a regular first-group verb. Remove <Uk>-ти</Uk> to get <Uk>чита-</Uk>. The stem ends in a vowel, so the endings are spelled with <Uk>ю</Uk> and <Uk>є</Uk>.

<GrammarTable
  caption="To read: present tense (first group)"
  headers={['Person', 'Ukrainian', 'English']}
  rows={[
    ['I', 'я чита́ю', 'I read, I am reading'],
    ['you (informal)', 'ти чита́єш', 'you read'],
    ['he / she / it', 'він / вона́ / воно́ чита́є', 'he / she / it reads'],
    ['we', 'ми чита́ємо', 'we read'],
    ['you (formal or plural)', 'ви чита́єте', 'you read'],
    ['they', 'вони́ чита́ють', 'they read'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[1]}
/>

The stress stays on the same syllable, <Uk>-та́-</Uk>, in every form. Many common verbs work exactly like this, for example <Uk>зна́ти</Uk> (to know: <Uk>зна́ю</Uk>, <Uk>зна́єш</Uk>), <Uk>ма́ти</Uk> (to have: <Uk>ма́ю</Uk>, <Uk>ма́єш</Uk>) and <Uk>розмовля́ти</Uk> (to talk: <Uk>розмовля́ю</Uk>, <Uk>розмовля́єш</Uk>).

## First group with a change: "to write"

<Uk>Писа́ти</Uk> is also in the first group, but two things change. The <Uk>с</Uk> of the stem becomes <Uk>ш</Uk> in every present-tense form. And the stress moves.

<GrammarTable
  caption="To write: present tense (first group, stress moves)"
  headers={['Person', 'Ukrainian', 'English']}
  rows={[
    ['I', 'я пишу́', 'I write, I am writing'],
    ['you (informal)', 'ти пи́шеш', 'you write'],
    ['he / she / it', 'він / вона́ / воно́ пи́ше', 'he / she / it writes'],
    ['we', 'ми пи́шемо', 'we write'],
    ['you (formal or plural)', 'ви пи́шете', 'you write'],
    ['they', 'вони́ пи́шуть', 'they write'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[1]}
/>

<Note type="remember">
  **The stress moves.** In the infinitive <Uk>писа́ти</Uk> and in the *I* form <Uk>пишу́</Uk>, the stress is near the end. In all the other forms it moves to the first syllable: <Uk>пи́шеш</Uk>, <Uk>пи́ше</Uk>, <Uk>пи́шуть</Uk>.
</Note>

Here the stem ends in a consonant (<Uk>пиш-</Uk>), so the endings are spelled with <Uk>у</Uk> and <Uk>е</Uk> instead of <Uk>ю</Uk> and <Uk>є</Uk>.

## Second group: "to speak"

<Uk>Говори́ти</Uk> is a second-group verb. Remove <Uk>-ити</Uk> to get the stem <Uk>говор-</Uk>, then add the second-group endings.

<GrammarTable
  caption="To speak: present tense (second group, stress moves)"
  headers={['Person', 'Ukrainian', 'English']}
  rows={[
    ['I', 'я говорю́', 'I speak, I am speaking'],
    ['you (informal)', 'ти гово́риш', 'you speak'],
    ['he / she / it', 'він / вона́ / воно́ гово́рить', 'he / she / it speaks'],
    ['we', 'ми гово́римо', 'we speak'],
    ['you (formal or plural)', 'ви гово́рите', 'you speak'],
    ['they', 'вони́ гово́рять', 'they speak'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[1]}
/>

<Note type="remember">
  **The stress moves here too.** It is on the ending in <Uk>говорю́</Uk>, and on <Uk>-во́-</Uk> in every other form: <Uk>гово́риш</Uk>, <Uk>гово́рить</Uk>, <Uk>гово́рять</Uk>.
</Note>

Notice the *he/she/it* form: second-group verbs end in <Uk>-ить</Uk> (<Uk>гово́рить</Uk>), with a <Uk>т</Uk>. First-group verbs have no <Uk>т</Uk> there (<Uk>чита́є</Uk>, <Uk>пи́ше</Uk>).

<Uk>Ба́чити</Uk> (to see) is a second-group verb with fixed stress: <Uk>ба́чу</Uk>, <Uk>ба́чиш</Uk>, <Uk>ба́чить</Uk>, <Uk>ба́чимо</Uk>, <Uk>ба́чите</Uk>, <Uk>ба́чать</Uk>.

## Where the stress goes

Stress in the present tense follows one of a few patterns. You can't tell which one from the infinitive, so learn the *I* and *you* (<Uk>ти</Uk>) forms of each new verb. Together they show both the group and the stress.

<Uk>Жи́ти</Uk> (to live) is a first-group verb with the stem <Uk>жив-</Uk>, and the stress falls on the ending in every form: <Uk>живу́</Uk>, <Uk>живе́ш</Uk>, <Uk>живе́</Uk>, <Uk>живемо́</Uk>, <Uk>живете́</Uk>, <Uk>живу́ть</Uk>.

## More patterns you will meet soon

### Verbs like "to work"

Many verbs end in <Uk>-ува́ти</Uk> or <Uk>-юва́ти</Uk>. In the present tense, <Uk>-ува-</Uk> becomes <Uk>-у-</Uk> (and <Uk>-юва-</Uk> becomes <Uk>-ю-</Uk>), and they take first-group endings.

<ExampleList title="Verbs like “to work” and “to phone”">
  <Example uk="працюва́ти → я працю́ю, ти працю́єш, вони́ працю́ють" en="to work → I work, you work, they work" note="Informal: ти." />
  <Example uk="телефонува́ти → я телефону́ю, ти телефону́єш, вони́ телефону́ють" en="to phone → I phone, you phone, they phone" note="Informal: ти." />
</ExampleList>

### A changed consonant in the I form

In some second-group verbs, the consonant before the ending changes in the *I* form. <Uk>Ходи́ти</Uk> (to walk, to go on foot) has <Uk>ходжу́</Uk> but <Uk>хо́диш</Uk>. <Uk>Роби́ти</Uk> (to do, to make) has <Uk>роблю́</Uk> but <Uk>ро́биш</Uk>; it adds <Uk>л</Uk> in the *they* form too: <Uk>ро́блять</Uk>. In both verbs the stress also moves, like <Uk>говори́ти</Uk>.

### Reflexive verbs

Some verbs end in <Uk>-ся</Uk>, which stays at the very end, after the person ending. <Uk>Навча́тися</Uk> (to study, to be a student) is a first-group verb: <Uk>я навча́юся</Uk>, <Uk>ти навча́єшся</Uk>, <Uk>він навча́ється</Uk>, <Uk>ми навча́ємося</Uk>, <Uk>ви навча́єтеся</Uk>, <Uk>вони́ навча́ються</Uk>. After a vowel, dictionaries also give a shorter <Uk>-сь</Uk>: <Uk>навча́юсь</Uk>.

Only imperfective verbs have a present tense. All the verbs on this page have a present tense. Perfective verbs, the other kind, take the same endings but give them a future meaning. The page on [the future tense](/grammar/future-tense/) explains this.

## Common mistakes and limits

<Note type="mistake">
  **Mixing the endings of the two groups.** Write <Uk>гово́рить</Uk> (second group, with <Uk>т</Uk>), but <Uk>чита́є</Uk> (first group, no <Uk>т</Uk>). Check the *you* (<Uk>ти</Uk>) form in a dictionary if you are not sure of the group.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Keeping the stress of the infinitive in every form.** <Uk>Говори́ти</Uk> is stressed on <Uk>-ри́-</Uk>, but the present forms are <Uk>гово́риш</Uk>, <Uk>гово́рить</Uk> and so on. Learn the stress of each form you use.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Adding a word for "am" or "is".** <Uk>Я чита́ю</Uk> already means "I am reading". There is no separate word for *am*.
</Note>

<Note>
  Dictionaries also list shorter *we* forms, such as <Uk>чита́єм</Uk> and <Uk>гово́рим</Uk>. This site uses the full forms <Uk>чита́ємо</Uk> and <Uk>гово́римо</Uk>. A few common verbs have irregular present-tense forms; see [Irregular verbs](/grammar/irregular-verbs/).
</Note>
