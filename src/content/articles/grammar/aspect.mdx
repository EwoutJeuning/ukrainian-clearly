After this page you will be able to explain the difference between imperfective and perfective verbs, recognise the main ways aspect pairs are formed, and know which forms each kind of verb has in the present, past and future. You will also have rules of thumb for choosing the right verb: for a single completed action, a repeated action, an action that lasts, a negative sentence, and after verbs such as *start* and *finish*.

[The past tense](/grammar/past-tense/) and [the future tense](/grammar/future-tense/) introduced **[aspect](/glossary/#aspect)**. This page brings it together.

## Two ways of seeing an action

Most Ukrainian verbs come in pairs that share one English translation [src: ukma-ukrainian-foreign-language]:

- the **imperfective** verb (<Uk>недоко́наний вид</Uk>) looks at the action as a process, or as something repeated. Whether it was finished is not the point;
- the **perfective** verb (<Uk>доко́наний вид</Uk>) looks at the action as one whole, completed, usually with a result.

<ExampleList title="Process or result">
  <Example uk="Я писа́в лист." en="I was writing a letter. / I wrote letters." note="Imperfective: the activity. We do not learn whether the letter was finished. Said by a man." />
  <Example uk="Я написа́в лист." en="I wrote the letter (and finished it)." note="Perfective: the letter is done. Said by a man." />
  <Example uk="Вона́ купува́ла квитки́." en="She was buying tickets." note="Imperfective: what she was busy doing." />
  <Example uk="Вона́ купи́ла квитки́." en="She bought the tickets." note="Perfective: she has them now." />
</ExampleList>

The English translation does not always show the difference. *I wrote a letter* can translate either sentence, depending on what the speaker wants to stress.

## How pairs are formed

There is no single rule that turns one aspect into the other. Learn each verb with its partner. Most pairs follow one of three patterns.

### A prefix makes the perfective

Many perfectives are the imperfective verb with a prefix. Which prefix is used has to be learned with the verb.

<GrammarTable
  caption="Perfective with a prefix"
  headers={['Imperfective', 'Perfective', 'English']}
  rows={[
    ['писа́ти', 'написа́ти', 'to write'],
    ['роби́ти', 'зроби́ти', 'to do, to make'],
    ['ба́чити', 'поба́чити', 'to see'],
    ['кла́сти', 'покла́сти', 'to put, to lay'],
    ['телефонува́ти', 'зателефонува́ти', 'to phone'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[0, 1]}
/>

A prefix can also change the meaning, so not every prefixed verb is the plain partner of the simple verb. <Uk>Записа́ти</Uk>, for example, means "to write down, to record", not just "to write".

### A change of suffix

In other pairs the two verbs share a root but have different suffixes. Often the imperfective is the longer verb, and the stress may move.

<GrammarTable
  caption="Pairs with different suffixes"
  headers={['Imperfective', 'Perfective', 'English']}
  rows={[
    ['вивча́ти', 'ви́вчити', 'to study, to learn (thoroughly)'],
    ['відкрива́ти', 'відкри́ти', 'to open'],
    ['закрива́ти', 'закри́ти', 'to close'],
    ['купува́ти', 'купи́ти', 'to buy'],
    ['дава́ти', 'да́ти', 'to give'],
    ['забува́ти', 'забу́ти', 'to forget'],
    ['допомага́ти', 'допомогти́', 'to help'],
    ['почина́ти', 'поча́ти', 'to begin, to start'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[0, 1]}
/>

### Different words

A few common pairs use entirely different stems. These are standard pairs in textbooks, but in the dictionary each verb has its own entry, so learn them as items.

<GrammarTable
  caption="Pairs with different stems"
  headers={['Imperfective', 'Perfective', 'English']}
  rows={[
    ['бра́ти', 'взя́ти', 'to take'],
    ['говори́ти', 'сказа́ти', 'to say, to tell'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[0, 1]}
/>

<Uk>Говори́ти</Uk> also means "to speak, to talk", while <Uk>сказа́ти</Uk> means only "to say, to tell".

## Aspect in each tense

Both kinds of verb have an [infinitive](/glossary/#infinitive) and a past tense. The difference is in the present and the future [src: ukma-ukrainian-foreign-language].

<GrammarTable
  caption="Which tenses each aspect has"
  headers={['Tense', 'Imperfective (писа́ти)', 'Perfective (написа́ти)']}
  rows={[
    ['Past', 'я писа́в, я писа́ла', 'я написа́в, я написа́ла'],
    ['Present', 'я пишу́', '(none)'],
    ['Future', 'я бу́ду писа́ти, я писа́тиму', 'я напишу́'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[1, 2]}
/>

- **No perfective present.** An action that is still going on cannot be seen as completed, so perfective verbs have no present tense [src: ukma-ukrainian-foreign-language]. Aspect also matters in commands; see [The imperative](/grammar/imperative/).
- **Perfective future.** The forms of a perfective verb that look like the present tense mean the future: <Uk>напишу́</Uk> is *I will write*, not *I write*. See [the future tense](/grammar/future-tense/).
- **Imperfective future.** Imperfective verbs make the future with <Uk>бу́ду</Uk> + infinitive, or with the one-word form <Uk>писа́тиму</Uk>. Perfective verbs never use <Uk>бу́ду</Uk>.

<ExampleList title="The same pair in three tenses">
  <Example uk="Я пишу́ лист." en="I am writing a letter." note="Imperfective present." />
  <Example uk="Уве́чері я бу́ду писа́ти лист." en="In the evening I will be writing a letter." note="Imperfective future: the activity." />
  <Example uk="Сього́дні я напишу́ лист, а за́втра відпра́влю." en="Today I will write the letter, and tomorrow I will send it." note="Perfective future: two completed actions, one after the other." />
  <Example uk="Я вже написа́ла лист." en="I have already written the letter." note="Perfective past. Said by a woman." />
</ExampleList>

## Choosing the aspect

These are tendencies, not mechanical rules. Native speakers choose by what they want to say, and sometimes both verbs are possible with a small change of meaning.

### One completed action: perfective

Use a perfective verb for a single action that is (or will be) completed, especially when the result matters, and for a sequence of actions where each one finishes before the next begins.

<ExampleList title="Single completed actions">
  <Example uk="Я ви́вчив ці слова́." en="I have learnt these words." note="Perfective: I know them now. Said by a man." />
  <Example uk="Він відкри́в вікно́ і сів." en="He opened the window and sat down." note="Perfective: one action after another." />
  <Example uk="За́втра я тобі́ допоможу́." en="I will help you tomorrow." note="Informal: ти. Perfective future: one occasion." />
</ExampleList>

### Repeated or habitual actions: imperfective

Use an imperfective verb for actions that happen regularly or many times. Words such as <Uk>завжди</Uk> (always), <Uk>ча́сто</Uk> (often), <Uk>щодня́</Uk> (every day) and <Uk>щоро́ку</Uk> (every year) usually go with an imperfective verb.

<ExampleList title="Repeated actions">
  <Example uk="Я щодня́ купу́ю хліб." en="I buy bread every day." />
  <Example uk="Ми ча́сто телефонува́ли бабу́сі." en="We often phoned Grandma." />
  <Example uk="Вона́ завжди забува́є ключі́." en="She always forgets her keys." />
</ExampleList>

Compare a single occasion with the perfective: <Uk>Вчо́ра вона́ забу́ла ключі́.</Uk> (Yesterday she forgot her keys.)

### Duration: imperfective

When you say how long an action went on, or describe what was happening at a moment, use an imperfective verb.

<ExampleList title="How long, or in progress">
  <Example uk="Я дві годи́ни писа́в лист." en="I spent two hours writing the letter." note="Literally “I was writing the letter for two hours.” Said by a man." />
  <Example uk="Коли́ ти подзвони́в, я чита́ла." en="When you phoned, I was reading." note="Informal: ти, said to a man. Said by a woman. The phone call is a single event (perfective); the reading was in progress (imperfective)." />
</ExampleList>

### Questions about the action itself

When you ask whether an action has ever happened, or just name the activity without caring about the result, the imperfective is common. This is a tendency, and a perfective question is also possible when you ask about a result.

<ExampleList title="The activity or the result">
  <Example uk="Ти чита́в цю кни́гу?" en="Have you read this book (ever)?" note="Informal: ти, said to a man. Imperfective: asking about the experience." />
  <Example uk="Ти вже прочита́в цю кни́гу?" en="Have you finished this book yet?" note="Informal: ти, said to a man. Perfective: asking about the result." />
</ExampleList>

### Negation

In negative sentences the imperfective often means that the action did not happen at all, while the perfective often means that an expected result was not reached. Again, treat this as a tendency. See also [negation](/grammar/negation/).

<ExampleList title="Negative sentences">
  <Example uk="Я не писа́в лист." en="I didn't write a letter. / I wasn't writing a letter." note="Imperfective: no writing took place. Said by a man." />
  <Example uk="Я не написа́в лист." en="I didn't get the letter written." note="Perfective: the expected result is missing. Said by a man." />
  <Example uk="Не забу́дь ключі́!" en="Don't forget the keys!" note="Informal: ти. A warning about one occasion uses the perfective. The Imperative page explains commands." />
</ExampleList>

### After “start”, “finish” and “stop”: imperfective infinitive

Verbs meaning *start*, *finish* and *stop* are followed by an **imperfective** infinitive, because they describe a stage of a process. The verbs themselves have both aspects: <Uk>почина́ти</Uk> / <Uk>поча́ти</Uk> (to start), <Uk>закі́нчувати</Uk> / <Uk>закінчити</Uk> (to finish) and <Uk>перестава́ти</Uk> / <Uk>переста́ти</Uk> (to stop).

<ExampleList title="Start, finish, stop + imperfective">
  <Example uk="Я почала́ вивча́ти украї́нську." en="I have started learning Ukrainian." note="Said by a woman. Not *почала́ ви́вчити." />
  <Example uk="Він закінчив писа́ти лист." en="He finished writing the letter." />
  <Example uk="Ми переста́ли купува́ти газе́ти." en="We stopped buying newspapers." />
</ExampleList>

<Uk>Закінчити</Uk> is left without a stress mark on this page: the dictionary accepts two stresses, <Uk>закі́нчити</Uk> and <Uk>закінчи́ти</Uk>, and the same goes for its past forms.

## How dictionaries mark aspect

Dictionaries label every verb. <Uk>Горох</Uk> shows the label on the word-forms page: <Uk>недоко́наний вид</Uk> (imperfective) or <Uk>доко́наний вид</Uk> (perfective). The definitions page (from the academic dictionary <Uk>СУМ</Uk>) uses the abbreviations <Uk>недок.</Uk> and <Uk>док.</Uk> and often points from one verb to its partner: <Uk>написа́ти</Uk> is defined as "<Uk>Док. до писа́ти</Uk>", the perfective of <Uk>писа́ти</Uk> [src: goroh-dictionary].

The dictionary sometimes pairs a perfective with a longer imperfective rather than the one you learnt first. For <Uk>прочита́ти</Uk> it gives <Uk>прочи́тувати</Uk>, and for <Uk>ви́пити</Uk> it gives <Uk>випива́ти</Uk> [src: goroh-dictionary]. In everyday use, <Uk>чита́ти</Uk> / <Uk>прочита́ти</Uk> works as a pair, as on [the future tense](/grammar/future-tense/) page.

## Summary

<GrammarTable caption="Imperfective or perfective?">

| Situation | Aspect | Example |
| --------- | ------ | ------- |
| Action in progress, now | Imperfective (no perfective present) | <Uk>Я пишу́.</Uk> I am writing. |
| One completed action, a result | Perfective | <Uk>Я написа́в лист.</Uk> I wrote the letter. |
| Repeated or habitual action | Imperfective | <Uk>Я щодня́ купу́ю хліб.</Uk> I buy bread every day. |
| How long something lasted | Imperfective | <Uk>Я дві годи́ни писа́в.</Uk> I was writing for two hours. |
| After *start*, *finish*, *stop* | Imperfective infinitive | <Uk>Я почала́ писа́ти.</Uk> I started writing. |

</GrammarTable>

## Common mistakes and limits

<Note type="mistake">
  **A perfective verb for something happening now.** <Uk>Я напишу́</Uk> means *I will write*. For *I am writing*, use the imperfective: <Uk>я пишу́</Uk>.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **<Uk>бу́ду</Uk> with a perfective verb.** Say <Uk>я бу́ду писа́ти</Uk> or <Uk>я напишу́</Uk>, not *<Uk>я бу́ду написа́ти</Uk>*.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **A perfective infinitive after *start* or *stop*.** Say <Uk>я почала́ писа́ти</Uk>, not *<Uk>я почала́ написа́ти</Uk>*.
</Note>

<Note type="remember">
  Learn every verb with its partner and check the label in a dictionary: <Uk>недоко́наний вид</Uk> or <Uk>доко́наний вид</Uk>. The rules on this page are tendencies. Context, and what the speaker wants to stress, decide the final choice.
</Note>
