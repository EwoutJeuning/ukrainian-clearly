After this page you will be able to turn an adjective into an adverb (<Uk>швидки́й</Uk> → <Uk>шви́дко</Uk>), use the <Uk>по-</Uk> forms such as <Uk>по-сво́єму</Uk>, and choose the right word for *here*, *there* and *where* when you talk about a place, a direction or a starting point. You will also know the common adverbs of time and manner, and how the spelling standard decides whether an adverb is one word, two words or hyphenated.

## What an adverb does

An adverb tells you **how**, **where**, **when** or **how much** something happens. Unlike an adjective, it does not agree with anything: it has one form, whatever the gender, number or case around it. The dictionary marks such words as not declined (<Uk>невідмі́нювана</Uk>) [src: goroh-dictionary].

<ExampleList title="Adverbs in a sentence">
  <Example uk="Він шви́дко гово́рить." en="He speaks fast." note="How?" />
  <Example uk="Вона́ живе́ тут." en="She lives here." note="Where?" />
  <Example uk="За́втра ми ї́демо до Льво́ва." en="Tomorrow we're going to Lviv." note="When?" />
  <Example uk="Я ду́же втоми́вся." en="I'm very tired." note="How much? Said by a man." />
</ExampleList>

## Adverbs from adjectives: -о and -е

Many adverbs are made from adjectives. Take off the ending <Uk>-ий</Uk> and add <Uk>-о</Uk>. The dictionary links each such adverb to its adjective: <Uk>шви́дко</Uk> is defined as "<Uk>Присл. до швидки́й</Uk>", the adverb of <Uk>швидки́й</Uk> [src: goroh-dictionary].

  caption="Adverbs in -о"
  headers={['Adjective', 'Adverb', 'English']}
  rows={[
    ['швидки́й', 'шви́дко', 'fast, quickly'],
    ['пові́льний', 'пові́льно', 'slowly'],
    ['га́рний', 'га́рно', 'nicely, beautifully'],
    ['ти́хий', 'ти́хо', 'quietly'],
    ['пога́ний', 'пога́но', 'badly'],
    ['ціка́вий', 'ціка́во', 'interestingly'],
    ['висо́кий', 'високо', 'high'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[0, 1]}
/>

A few adverbs end in <Uk>-е</Uk> instead. The most common is <Uk>до́бре</Uk> (well), from <Uk>до́брий</Uk>. After <Uk>ж</Uk>, <Uk>ч</Uk> and <Uk>ш</Uk> the spelling standard writes <Uk>-е</Uk> in some adverbs, such as <Uk>гаряче</Uk> (hotly) and <Uk>воро́же</Uk> (in a hostile way), but <Uk>-о</Uk> in others, such as <Uk>тво́рчо</Uk> (creatively) [src: pravopys-2026, §10, note]. Learn these one by one.

**Watch the stress.** It often stays where it was in the adjective, but not always: <Uk>швидки́й</Uk> but <Uk>шви́дко</Uk> [src: goroh-dictionary]. Check the adverb in the dictionary rather than guessing.

**Adjective or adverb?** Use the adjective with a noun, and the adverb with a verb. The same <Uk>-о</Uk> form is also used on its own in sentences such as <Uk>Тут ти́хо.</Uk> (It's quiet here.); see [Impersonal sentences](/grammar/impersonal-sentences/).

<ExampleList title="Adjective or adverb">
  <Example uk="У ньо́го га́рний го́лос." en="He has a beautiful voice." note="Adjective: describes the noun го́лос." />
  <Example uk="Він га́рно співа́є." en="He sings beautifully." note="Adverb: describes the verb." />
  <Example uk="Ти до́бре розмовля́єш украї́нською." en="You speak Ukrainian well." note="Informal: ти." />
</ExampleList>

## The по- forms: по-новому, по-українськи

Another group is made with <Uk>по-</Uk> and the ending <Uk>-ому</Uk> or <Uk>-и</Uk>. They mean "in the … way" and are always written with a hyphen: <Uk>по-украї́нському</Uk> and <Uk>по-украї́нськи</Uk>, <Uk>по-на́шому</Uk>, <Uk>по-сво́єму</Uk>, <Uk>по-і́ншому</Uk>, <Uk>по-лю́дськи</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §41]. The words for *firstly*, *secondly* and so on also take a hyphen: <Uk>по-пе́рше</Uk>, <Uk>по-дру́ге</Uk>, <Uk>по-тре́тє</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §41].

<ExampleList title="по- forms">
  <Example uk="Зроби́ це по-сво́єму." en="Do it your own way." note="Informal: зроби́ is the ти form of the command." />
  <Example uk="Тепе́р усе́ бу́де по-і́ншому." en="Now everything will be different." note="Literally: in another way." />
  <Example uk="По-пе́рше, у ме́не нема́є ча́су." en="Firstly, I don't have time." />
</ExampleList>

<Uk>Не</Uk> before a <Uk>по-</Uk> form is written separately: <Uk>розмовля́ють не по-на́шому</Uk> (they speak differently from us) [src: pravopys-2026, §44].

For **languages**, usage guides recommend the instrumental <Uk>украї́нською (мо́вою)</Uk> rather than <Uk>по-украї́нськи</Uk>: <Uk>я розмовля́ю украї́нською мо́вою</Uk>, not *<Uk>я говорю́ по-украї́нськи</Uk>*, and <Uk>як це сказа́ти украї́нською?</Uk> for *how do you say this in Ukrainian?* [src: goroh-usage].

## Place and direction: here, there, where

English *where* can mean three things, and Ukrainian has a separate word for each: <Uk>де</Uk> for a place, <Uk>куди́</Uk> for a direction [src: ukma-ukrainian-foreign-language], and <Uk>зві́дки</Uk> for where something comes from. [Questions](/grammar/questions/) introduced them. The words for *here* and *there* follow the same three-way split.

    ['at home, home', 'удо́ма', 'додо́му', '—'],
    ['up, above', 'угорі́, наверху́', 'уго́ру, наве́рх', 'зве́рху, згори́'],
    ['down, below', 'унизу́', 'уни́з', 'зни́зу'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[1, 2, 3]}
/>

<ExampleList title="Here, there and home">
  <Example uk="Ми тут живемо́." en="We live here." note="Place." />
  <Example uk="Іди́ сюди́!" en="Come here!" note="Informal: іди́ is the ти form of the command. Direction." />
  <Example uk="Зві́дси до це́нтру дале́ко?" en="Is it far from here to the centre?" note="Starting point." />
  <Example uk="Я вже йду додо́му." en="I'm already going home." note="Direction: not удо́ма." />
  <Example uk="Ма́ма вдо́ма?" en="Is Mum at home?" note="Place: в, not у, after a vowel." />
</ExampleList>

Many of these words begin with <Uk>у-</Uk> or <Uk>в-</Uk>: <Uk>угорі́</Uk> or <Uk>вгорі́</Uk>, <Uk>унизу́</Uk> or <Uk>внизу́</Uk>, <Uk>удо́ма</Uk> or <Uk>вдо́ма</Uk>. The spelling standard lists both forms [src: pravopys-2026, §41], and the choice follows the general <Uk>у</Uk>/<Uk>в</Uk> rule: <Uk>у-</Uk> between consonants, <Uk>в-</Uk> after a vowel [src: pravopys-2026, §23].

Two more pairs are useful for directions: <Uk>ліво́руч</Uk> (on the left, to the left) and <Uk>право́руч</Uk> (on the right, to the right), each written as one word [src: pravopys-2026, §41].

The parts of the day and the seasons are single words, made historically from a preposition and a noun. The standard writes them together, with <Uk>у-</Uk> or <Uk>в-</Uk> as above [src: pravopys-2026, §41].

Words for *every day*, *every evening* and *every year* are written as one word with <Uk>що-</Uk>: <Uk>щодня́</Uk>, <Uk>щове́чора</Uk>, <Uk>щоро́ку</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §41].

<ExampleList title="When?">
  <Example uk="Уве́чері я чита́ю." en="I read in the evenings." />
  <Example uk="Ми ча́сто гуля́ємо в па́рку." en="We often go for walks in the park." />
  <Example uk="Улі́тку тут ду́же га́рно." en="It's very beautiful here in summer." />
  <Example uk="Вона́ щодня́ хо́дить на робо́ту пі́шки." en="She walks to work every day." />
  <Example uk="Я ніко́ли не був у Ха́ркові." en="I've never been to Kharkiv." note="Said by a man. ніко́ли needs не with the verb; see Negation." />
</ExampleList>

**<Uk>Ніко́ли</Uk> or <Uk>ні́коли</Uk>?** The spelling is the same, but the stress changes the meaning. <Uk>Ніко́ли</Uk> means *never*. <Uk>Ні́коли</Uk> means *there is no time*: <Uk>Мені́ ні́коли.</Uk> (I have no time.) [src: goroh-dictionary; pravopys-2026, §44]. [Negation](/grammar/negation/) has more words with <Uk>ні-</Uk>.

## One word, a hyphen or two words?

Many adverbs grew out of a preposition and another word, so their spelling is a common question. The spelling standard has a long list [src: pravopys-2026, §41]. The main patterns:

<GrammarTable
  caption="How adverbs are written"
  headers={['Written', 'Pattern', 'Examples']}
  rows={[
    ['as one word', 'a preposition and a noun that has merged into one word', 'вра́нці, наре́шті, напа́м’ять, спе́реду, наза́д, напри́клад'],
    ['as one word', 'a preposition and a numeral', 'упе́рше, удво́х'],
    ['as one word', 'with що-, де-, аби-', 'щодня́, де́коли, аби́як'],
    ['as two words', 'a preposition and a noun that keeps its own meaning and case', 'на жаль, до ре́чі, без кінця́, на ща́стя'],
    ['as two words', 'a preposition and a word used like a noun', 'до за́втра, від сього́дні'],
    ['with a hyphen', 'по- with -ому or -и, and по- with an ordinal number', 'по-ново́му, по-украї́нськи, по-пе́рше'],
    ['with a hyphen', 'with будь-, -не́будь', 'будь-де, будь-коли, де-не́будь, коли́-не́будь'],
    ['with a hyphen', 'a repeated word', 'ось-о́сь, ти́хо-ти́хо'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[2]}
/>

The same letters can be an adverb or a preposition with a noun. Ask whether the noun still has its own meaning, or whether you could put an adjective between the two words. The spelling standard gives pairs such as these [src: pravopys-2026, §41, note]:

  <Example uk="Ми чу́ємо це впе́рше." en="We're hearing this for the first time." note="Adverb: one word." />
  <Example uk="за́йдемо в пе́рше село́." en="Let's stop at the first village." note="Preposition and a numeral with its noun: two words." />
  <Example uk="Вона́ ви́вчила вірш напа́м’ять." en="She learnt the poem by heart." note="Adverb: one word." />
  <Example uk="На па́м’ять він подарува́в мені́ кни́жку." en="He gave me a book as a keepsake." note="Preposition and noun: two words." />

## Comparing with adverbs

Adverbs made from adjectives can be compared: <Uk>шви́дше</Uk> (faster), <Uk>кра́ще</Uk> (better), <Uk>ви́ще</Uk> (higher), <Uk>до́вше</Uk> (longer). The consonant changes are the same as in the comparative adjective: <Uk>висо́кий</Uk> → <Uk>ви́щий</Uk>, <Uk>ви́ще</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §20]. Comparison of adjectives and adverbs has its own page.

For speed, usage guides recommend <Uk>шви́дше</Uk> rather than <Uk>скорі́ше</Uk> [src: goroh-usage].

## Common mistakes and limits

<Note type="mistake">
  **An adjective instead of an adverb.** With a verb, use the adverb: <Uk>Він шви́дко гово́рить</Uk>, not *<Uk>Він швидки́й гово́рить</Uk>*.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **<Uk>Де</Uk> or <Uk>тут</Uk> for a direction.** With verbs of going, use the direction word: <Uk>Іди́ сюди́!</Uk>, <Uk>Я йду додо́му</Uk>, not *<Uk>Іди́ тут</Uk>* or *<Uk>Я йду вдо́ма</Uk>*.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Writing a merged adverb as two words.** <Uk>Вра́нці</Uk>, <Uk>уве́чері</Uk>, <Uk>взи́мку</Uk> and <Uk>наре́шті</Uk> are single words [src: pravopys-2026, §41]. But <Uk>на жаль</Uk> and <Uk>до ре́чі</Uk> are two.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **<Uk>По-украї́нськи</Uk> for the language you speak.** Say <Uk>я розмовля́ю украї́нською</Uk> [src: goroh-usage].
</Note>

<Note type="remember">
  Learn place words in sets of three: <Uk>тут</Uk> / <Uk>сюди́</Uk> / <Uk>зві́дси</Uk>, <Uk>там</Uk> / <Uk>туди́</Uk> / <Uk>зві́дти</Uk>, <Uk>де</Uk> / <Uk>куди́</Uk> / <Uk>зві́дки</Uk>.
</Note>

<Note>
  This page leaves out most adverbs with <Uk>-сь</Uk> and <Uk>-не́будь</Uk>, such as <Uk>десь</Uk> (somewhere) and <Uk>коли́-не́будь</Uk> (ever, at some time). They work like the indefinite pronouns, which have their own page.
</Note>
