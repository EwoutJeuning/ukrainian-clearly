After this page you will be able to put any noun into the accusative, in the singular and the plural. You will know the one rule that trips up most learners: masculine nouns for people and animals behave differently from nouns for things. You will also use the accusative after common verbs, after the prepositions <Uk>в</Uk> (<Uk>у</Uk>), <Uk>на</Uk>, <Uk>за</Uk>, <Uk>про</Uk> and <Uk>че́рез</Uk>, and to say *on Monday*.

[Cases, an overview](/grammar/case-overview/) introduced the **[accusative](/glossary/#accusative)** as the case of the direct object. This page gives the full set of endings.

## What the accusative does

The accusative has three main jobs:

- **The direct object:** the person or thing the action is done to. <Uk>Я чита́ю кни́гу.</Uk> (I'm reading a book.)
- **After some prepositions**, most often for a direction: <Uk>Я йду в шко́лу.</Uk> (I'm going to school.)
- **Some time expressions:** <Uk>у понеді́лок</Uk> (on Monday).

## Singular endings

Only some nouns change in the accusative singular. Feminine nouns in <Uk>-а</Uk> and <Uk>-я</Uk> always change. Masculine nouns change only when they name people or animals. Neuter nouns never change.

/>

The endings are set out in the official orthography [src: pravopys-2026, §71, §84, §95]. Some notes:

- **Masculine nouns in <Uk>-а</Uk> follow the feminine pattern.** Names such as <Uk>Мико́ла</Uk> become <Uk>Мико́лу</Uk>: <Uk>Я ба́чу Мико́лу.</Uk>
- **Masculine nouns in <Uk>-о</Uk>** name people, so they take the genitive form: <Uk>ба́тько</Uk> → <Uk>ба́тька</Uk> (father).
- **Stress can move.** <Uk>Середа́</Uk> (Wednesday) becomes <Uk>се́реду</Uk>, with the stress on the first syllable. Stress shifts like this are shown in the dictionary paradigm.

  <Example uk="Ма́ма лю́бить цю пі́сню." en="Mum loves this song." note="пі́сня → пі́сню" />
  <Example uk="Ми ку́пимо стіл." en="We'll buy a table." note="A thing: стіл does not change." />
  <Example uk="Я ба́чу бра́та." en="I can see my brother." note="A person: брат → бра́та." />
  <Example uk="Він шука́є кварти́ру в це́нтрі." en="He's looking for a flat in the centre." note="кварти́ра → кварти́ру" />
  <Example uk="Я ма́ю кота́." en="I have a cat." note="An animal: кіт → кота́." />
</ExampleList>

## People and animals: the masculine rule

Grammars sort nouns into **animate** nouns, which name people and animals, and **inanimate** nouns, which name everything else. In the accusative singular, the difference matters only for masculine nouns:

- A masculine noun for a **thing** keeps its dictionary form: <Uk>Я ба́чу стіл.</Uk> (I see the table.)
- A masculine noun for a **person or animal** takes the genitive form: <Uk>Я ба́чу бра́та.</Uk> (I see my brother.) <Uk>Бра́та</Uk> is also the genitive, *of my brother*.

This is set out in the orthography: all masculine nouns for living beings have an accusative that is the same as the genitive [src: pravopys-2026, §84]. So once you know a noun's genitive, you also know its accusative.

<ExampleList title="Things and people side by side">
  <Example uk="Я купи́в квито́к." en="I bought a ticket." note="Said by a man. A thing: квито́к stays the same." />
  <Example uk="Я зна́ю цього́ лі́каря." en="I know this doctor." note="A person: лі́кар → лі́каря. The word for this changes too: цей → цього́." />
  <Example uk="Ми ба́чили хло́пця й соба́ку." en="We saw a boy and a dog." note="хло́пець → хло́пця (masculine, a person). соба́ка → соба́ку (the -а pattern)." />
</ExampleList>

## Plural endings

In the plural, the rule for people and animals covers **all three genders** [src: pravopys-2026, §78, §91, §100]:

- Nouns for **things** have an accusative plural that is the same as the nominative plural.
- Nouns for **people and animals** have an accusative plural that is the same as the [genitive plural](/grammar/genitive/#the-genitive-plural).

<ExampleList title="The accusative plural">
  <Example uk="Я люблю́ ці кни́ги." en="I love these books." note="Things: the same as the nominative." />
  <Example uk="Я ба́чу студе́нтів." en="I see the students." note="People: студе́нти → студе́нтів." />
  <Example uk="Ми запроси́ли дру́зів." en="We invited our friends." note="дру́зі → дру́зів" />

For some animals, especially domestic ones, the form like the nominative is also used: <Uk>ко́ней</Uk> and less often <Uk>ко́ні</Uk> (horses) [src: pravopys-2026, §91, note]. Young animals such as <Uk>кошеня́та</Uk> (kittens) have both forms too: <Uk>кошеня́т</Uk> or <Uk>кошеня́та</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §99].

## After verbs

Most verbs that have a direct object take the accusative. These are some of the most common ones:

<GrammarTable
  caption="Common verbs with a direct object in the accusative"
  headers={['Verb', 'Meaning', 'Example']}
  rows={[
    ['ба́чити', 'to see', 'Я ба́чу мо́ре.'],
    ['чита́ти', 'to read', 'Він чита́є газе́ту.'],
    ['люби́ти', 'to love, to like', 'Вона́ лю́бить му́зику.'],
    ['ма́ти', 'to have', 'Я ма́ю сестру́.'],
    ['купува́ти', 'to buy', 'Ми купу́ємо хліб.'],
    ['шука́ти', 'to look for', 'Я шука́ю робо́ту.'],
    ['слу́хати', 'to listen to', 'Ми слу́хаємо ра́діо.'],
    ['пи́ти', 'to drink', 'Ма́ма п’є ка́ву.'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[0, 2]}
/>

The examples mean: *I see the sea. He's reading a newspaper. She loves music. I have a sister. We're buying bread. I'm looking for a job. We're listening to the radio. Mum is drinking coffee.*

Notice that some English verbs need a preposition where Ukrainian has none: *listen **to** the radio* is <Uk>слу́хати ра́діо</Uk>, and *look **for** a job* is <Uk>шука́ти робо́ту</Uk>. <Uk>Ра́діо</Uk> never changes, so you can't see the accusative here, but it is still there.

Not every verb takes the accusative. <Uk>Телефонува́ти</Uk> (to phone) and <Uk>допомага́ти</Uk> (to help) take the [dative](/grammar/dative/), for example. When you learn a verb, learn the case that comes after it.

## Prepositions with the accusative

These prepositions take the accusative. Several of them can also take other cases, with a different meaning [src: goroh-dictionary].

<GrammarTable
  caption="Prepositions with the accusative"
  headers={['Preposition', 'Meaning with the accusative', 'Example']}
  rows={[
    ['в, у', 'into, to (direction)', 'Ми ї́демо в Украї́ну.'],
    ['на', 'onto, to (direction); for (a length of time)', 'Я йду на конце́рт.'],
    ['за', 'for (in thanks or exchange); in, within (a time)', 'Дя́кую за допомо́гу.'],
    ['про', 'about', 'Це фільм про Ки́їв.'],
    ['че́рез', 'across; in, after (a time); because of', 'Ми перехо́димо че́рез доро́гу.'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[0, 2]}
/>

The examples mean: *We're going to Ukraine. I'm going to a concert. Thank you for your help. It's a film about Kyiv. We're crossing the road.*

### Direction: в/у and на

With the accusative, <Uk>в</Uk> (<Uk>у</Uk>) and <Uk>на</Uk> show where someone or something is going. With the [locative](/grammar/locative/) they show where it is. [Common prepositions](/grammar/prepositions/) explains this in detail, and when to write <Uk>у</Uk> or <Uk>в</Uk>.

<ExampleList title="Direction with the accusative">
  <Example uk="Ді́ти йдуть у шко́лу." en="The children are going to school." />
  <Example uk="Ма́ма ї́де на робо́ту." en="Mum is going to work." note="By some kind of transport: ї́хати." />
  <Example uk="Покладі́ть кни́гу на стіл, будь ла́ска." en="Put the book on the table, please." note="Formal or plural: ви. The book moves onto the table, so на takes the accusative." />
</ExampleList>

### Time: за, через and на

These three prepositions are often used with a length of time [src: goroh-dictionary].

<ExampleList title="Lengths of time">
  <Example uk="Я зроблю́ це за годи́ну." en="I'll do it in an hour." note="за: the time something takes." />
  <Example uk="Авто́бус бу́де че́рез годи́ну." en="The bus will come in an hour." note="че́рез: how long until something happens." />
  <Example uk="Вона́ приї́хала на ти́ждень." en="She has come for a week." note="на: how long something is planned to last." />
</ExampleList>

<Uk>За</Uk> can also mean *in* in the sense of "after a time": both <Uk>за годи́ну</Uk> and <Uk>че́рез годи́ну</Uk> can mean *an hour from now*. For the time something takes, use <Uk>за</Uk>.

### Other meanings of за and через

<ExampleList title="За and через: more examples">
  <Example uk="Дя́кую за кни́гу!" en="Thank you for the book!" note="за: the reason for thanks." />
  <Example uk="Я купи́в цей светр за п’ятсо́т гри́вень." en="I bought this jumper for five hundred hryvnias." note="Said by a man. за: the price. гри́вень is the genitive plural of гри́вня." />
  <Example uk="Че́рез дощ ми сиді́ли вдо́ма." en="Because of the rain we stayed at home." note="че́рез: the cause." />

## Days of the week: у понеділок

To say *on Monday*, *on Tuesday* and so on, use <Uk>у</Uk> (<Uk>в</Uk>) with the accusative of the day [src: ukma-ukrainian-foreign-language; goroh-dictionary]. Masculine days look the same as the dictionary form. Feminine days take <Uk>-у</Uk> or <Uk>-ю</Uk>.

<GrammarTable
  caption="On Monday, on Tuesday…"
  headers={['Day', 'On that day', 'English']}
  rows={[
    ['понеді́лок', 'у понеді́лок', 'on Monday'],
    ['вівто́рок', 'у вівто́рок', 'on Tuesday'],
    ['середа́', 'у се́реду', 'on Wednesday'],
    ['четве́р', 'у четве́р', 'on Thursday'],
    ['п’я́тниця', 'у п’я́тницю', 'on Friday'],
    ['субо́та', 'у субо́ту', 'on Saturday'],
    ['неді́ля', 'у неді́лю', 'on Sunday'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[0, 1]}
/>

In a sentence, choose <Uk>у</Uk> or <Uk>в</Uk> by the sounds around it, as on the [prepositions page](/grammar/prepositions/): <Uk>Я працю́ю в понеді́лок.</Uk> (I work on Monday.) But <Uk>у</Uk> before <Uk>в</Uk>: <Uk>Ми зустрі́лися у вівто́рок.</Uk> (We met on Tuesday.)

Two related expressions:

- **Every day** is <Uk>щодня́</Uk>. This is an adverb, not a case form, so it never changes: <Uk>Я чита́ю щодня́.</Uk> (I read every day.)
- **The whole day, the whole year** use the accusative with no preposition: <Uk>Він працюва́в ці́лий день.</Uk> (He worked all day.) The word for *whole* changes too: <Uk>ці́лу ніч</Uk> (all night). Adjective endings are covered on the [adjective cases](/grammar/adjective-cases/) page.

## Common mistakes and limits

<Note type="mistake">
  **Leaving a masculine person in the dictionary form.** Say <Uk>Я ба́чу бра́та</Uk>, not *<Uk>Я ба́чу брат</Uk>*. For people and animals, the masculine accusative is the genitive form.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Changing a masculine thing.** Say <Uk>Я ба́чу стіл</Uk>, not *<Uk>Я ба́чу стола́</Uk>*. Masculine nouns for things keep the dictionary form.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Using the nominative plural for people.** Say <Uk>Я ба́чу студе́нтів</Uk>, not *<Uk>Я ба́чу студе́нти</Uk>*. In the plural, the rule for people and animals covers every gender: <Uk>жіно́к</Uk>, <Uk>діте́й</Uk>.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Mixing up direction and place.** <Uk>Я йду на робо́ту</Uk> (accusative: where to?) but <Uk>Я на робо́ті</Uk> (locative: where?).
</Note>

<Note type="remember">
  Masculine people and animals, and all plural people and animals, borrow the genitive form. Learn the [genitive](/grammar/genitive/) next: it gives you half of the accusative for free.
</Note>

<Note>
  Some masculine nouns for everyday things also have a second accusative form like the genitive, for example <Uk>узя́в ніж</Uk> or <Uk>узя́в ножа́</Uk> (took the knife) [src: pravopys-2026, §84, note]. Use the dictionary form and you will be correct. After a negative verb, the object is often in the genitive instead of the accusative; see [The genitive case](/grammar/genitive/#after-a-negative-verb).
</Note>
