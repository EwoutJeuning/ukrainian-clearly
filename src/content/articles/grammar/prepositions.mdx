After this page you will know the most common Ukrainian prepositions: words like *in*, *on*, *to*, *from*, *with* and *without*. You will know which case of the noun each one needs, how to tell "in the school" from "into the school", and when to write <Uk>у</Uk> or <Uk>в</Uk>, and <Uk>з</Uk>, <Uk>із</Uk> or <Uk>зі</Uk>.

This page uses the cases introduced in [Cases, an overview](/grammar/case-overview/). You don't need to know every ending yet: the examples show the forms.

## Every preposition takes a case

In Ukrainian, the noun after a preposition is always in a particular case. Each preposition needs its own case, and usually the noun's ending changes to match. Sometimes the form looks the same as the dictionary form, as in <Uk>в Ки́їв</Uk> below. Some prepositions take one case. Others can take two, with a difference in meaning.

The best way to learn them is in short phrases: <Uk>без цу́кру</Uk> (without sugar), <Uk>з молоко́м</Uk> (with milk), <Uk>у шко́лі</Uk> (at school). A phrase shows the preposition and the case together.

## Place or direction: в/у and на

The prepositions <Uk>в</Uk> (also written <Uk>у</Uk>) and <Uk>на</Uk> each take two cases.

- With the **locative**, they describe a place: where something is. This answers <Uk>де?</Uk> (where?).
- With the **accusative**, they describe a direction: where something is going. This answers <Uk>куди́?</Uk> (where to?).

<ExampleList title="Place and direction">
  <Example uk="Я в шко́лі." en="I'm at school." note="шко́лі: locative. A place." />
  <Example uk="Я йду в шко́лу." en="I'm going to school." note="шко́лу: accusative. A direction." />
  <Example uk="Ма́ма на робо́ті." en="Mum is at work." note="робо́ті: locative." />
  <Example uk="Ма́ма йде на робо́ту." en="Mum is going to work." note="робо́ту: accusative." />
</ExampleList>

For many masculine nouns that name things, the accusative looks like the dictionary form: <Uk>Ми ї́демо в Ки́їв.</Uk> (We're going to Kyiv.) Compare the place: <Uk>Ми живемо́ в Ки́єві.</Uk> (We live in Kyiv.)

### В or на?

Both can mean *in*, *at* or *to*. Some nouns go with <Uk>в</Uk> and some with <Uk>на</Uk>, and there is no simple rule. Learn the preposition with the noun.

<Uk>На</Uk> with the locative also means *on*: <Uk>на столі́</Uk> (on the table).

For the country, say <Uk>в Украї́ні</Uk> (in Ukraine) and <Uk>в Украї́ну</Uk> (to Ukraine), the same preposition as with most other countries. The form *<Uk>на Украї́ні</Uk>* is still heard, but usage guides treat it as incorrect in standard Ukrainian [src: goroh-usage].

### Two meanings of з

<Uk>З</Uk> has two common meanings, and the case shows which one is meant.

  <Example uk="Ка́ва з молоко́м, будь ла́ска." en="A coffee with milk, please." note="молоко́м: instrumental. With." />

### To: в/на or до

For *to*, Ukrainian has <Uk>в</Uk> or <Uk>на</Uk> with the accusative, and <Uk>до</Uk> with the genitive. With people, use <Uk>до</Uk>: <Uk>Я йду до лі́каря.</Uk> (I'm going to the doctor.) <Uk>Ми ї́демо до ма́ми.</Uk> (We're going to Mum's.) With places, both are common: <Uk>Ми ї́демо до Льво́ва.</Uk> (We're going to Lviv.)

### Under, behind: place or direction

<Uk>Під</Uk> (under) and <Uk>за</Uk> (behind) work like <Uk>в</Uk> and <Uk>на</Uk>. With the instrumental they describe a place: <Uk>Кіт під столо́м.</Uk> (The cat is under the table.) With the accusative they describe a direction: <Uk>Кіт пішо́в під стіл.</Uk> (The cat went under the table.)

## У or в?

<Uk>У</Uk> and <Uk>в</Uk> are the same preposition. Ukrainian chooses between them to avoid awkward runs of consonants or vowels [src: pravopys-2026, §23]. Simplified, the main rules are:

- **<Uk>у</Uk> between consonants:** <Uk>Брат у шко́лі.</Uk>
- **<Uk>у</Uk> before <Uk>в</Uk>, <Uk>ф</Uk> and clusters like <Uk>льв-</Uk>, <Uk>св-</Uk>, <Uk>тв-</Uk>, <Uk>хв-</Uk>:** <Uk>у Льво́ві</Uk>, <Uk>у Фра́нції</Uk>.
- **<Uk>в</Uk> between vowels:** <Uk>Вона́ в Оде́сі.</Uk>
- **<Uk>в</Uk> after a vowel, before most consonants:** <Uk>Ма́ма в шко́лі.</Uk>
- **<Uk>в</Uk> after a consonant, before a vowel:** <Uk>Брат в Оде́сі.</Uk>
- **At the start of a sentence:** <Uk>у</Uk> before a consonant (<Uk>У ме́не є час.</Uk>), <Uk>в</Uk> before a vowel. The current standard also allows <Uk>в</Uk> before a consonant at the start of a sentence [src: pravopys-2026, §23, note 1].

These rules are about sound, so they don't change the meaning. If you are not sure, choose the form that is easier to say.

## З, із or зі?

<Uk>З</Uk> is the basic form. The forms <Uk>із</Uk> and <Uk>зі</Uk> are used to avoid hard runs of consonants [src: pravopys-2026, §25].

- **<Uk>з</Uk>** is the usual form: <Uk>з Ки́єва</Uk>, <Uk>з молоко́м</Uk>, <Uk>з бра́том</Uk>.
- **<Uk>із</Uk>** mostly comes before words starting with <Uk>з</Uk>, <Uk>с</Uk>, <Uk>ц</Uk>, <Uk>ч</Uk>, <Uk>ш</Uk> or <Uk>щ</Uk>: <Uk>із сестро́ю</Uk> (with my sister).
- **<Uk>зі</Uk>** comes before words starting with a cluster with <Uk>з</Uk>, <Uk>с</Uk> or <Uk>ш</Uk>, such as <Uk>зн-</Uk>, <Uk>ст-</Uk>, <Uk>шк-</Uk>, and before some other clusters, such as <Uk>льв-</Uk>: <Uk>зі шко́ли</Uk>, <Uk>зі Льво́ва</Uk>. *With me* is <Uk>зі мно́ю</Uk>.

Before some clusters, <Uk>із</Uk> and <Uk>зі</Uk> are both correct.

## Common mistakes and limits

<Note type="mistake">
  **Leaving the noun in the dictionary form.** After a preposition, the noun takes the case the preposition needs: <Uk>у шко́лі</Uk>, not *<Uk>у шко́ла</Uk>*; <Uk>без цу́кру</Uk>, not *<Uk>без цу́кор</Uk>*.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Mixing up place and direction.** <Uk>Я в шко́лі</Uk> (I'm at school) uses the locative. <Uk>Я йду в шко́лу</Uk> (I'm going to school) uses the accusative. Ask yourself: <Uk>де?</Uk> or <Uk>куди́?</Uk>
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Saying *<Uk>на Украї́ні</Uk>*.** Usage guides treat it as incorrect; standard Ukrainian uses <Uk>в Украї́ні</Uk> and <Uk>в Украї́ну</Uk>.
</Note>

<Note type="remember">
  This page covers the main uses of each preposition. Many prepositions have more meanings and can take other cases. Prepositions such as <Uk>по</Uk>, <Uk>че́рез</Uk> and <Uk>між</Uk> come later.
</Note>
