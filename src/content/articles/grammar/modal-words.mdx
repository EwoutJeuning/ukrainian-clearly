After this page you will be able to say what you can, want, must and know how to do, using a verb in the infinitive. You will know the difference between <Uk>могти́</Uk> (can, be able to) and <Uk>умі́ти</Uk> (know how to). You will be able to say what someone needs to do, what is allowed and what is not, and what someone should do, and you will know how to make each of these negative.

[Everyday needs](/sentences/everyday-needs/) introduced <Uk>хо́чу</Uk> and <Uk>мені́ тре́ба</Uk>. This page puts them together with the other words of this kind.

## Two kinds of modal words

Ukrainian has two kinds of words for *can*, *want* and *must*.

- **Verbs** that change for the person: <Uk>я мо́жу</Uk>, <Uk>ти мо́жеш</Uk>. The person is the subject, in the nominative.
- **Words that never change**: <Uk>тре́ба</Uk>, <Uk>потрі́бно</Uk>, <Uk>мо́жна</Uk>, <Uk>слід</Uk>. The person, if there is one, is in the [dative](/grammar/dative/): <Uk>мені́ тре́ба</Uk>, "to me it is necessary".

Both kinds are followed by a verb in the infinitive.

The stem changes from <Uk>мог-</Uk> to <Uk>мож-</Uk>, and the stress is on <Uk>мо́-</Uk> in every present form. The past tense is <Uk>міг</Uk> (masculine), <Uk>могла́</Uk>, <Uk>могло́</Uk>, <Uk>могли́</Uk> [src: goroh-dictionary].

<ExampleList title="Мо́жу + infinitive">
  <Example uk="Я мо́жу прийти́ о шо́стій." en="I can come at six." />
  <Example uk="Ти мо́жеш мені́ допомогти́?" en="Can you help me?" note="Informal: ти. мені́: dative." />
  <Example uk="Ви мо́жете говори́ти пові́льніше?" en="Could you speak more slowly?" note="Formal or plural: ви." />
  <Example uk="Він не міг спа́ти." en="He couldn't sleep." />
</ExampleList>

## Хотіти: want

The present tense of <Uk>хоті́ти</Uk> is in [Everyday needs](/sentences/everyday-needs/): <Uk>хо́чу</Uk>, <Uk>хо́чеш</Uk>, <Uk>хо́че</Uk>, <Uk>хо́чемо</Uk>, <Uk>хо́чете</Uk>, <Uk>хо́чуть</Uk> [src: goroh-dictionary]. It takes an infinitive or a noun in the accusative. The past tense is <Uk>хоті́в</Uk>, <Uk>хоті́ла</Uk>, <Uk>хоті́ли</Uk>, and with <Uk>би</Uk> or <Uk>б</Uk> it makes a polite wish: <Uk>я хоті́в би</Uk>, <Uk>я хоті́ла б</Uk> (I would like).

<ExampleList title="Хо́чу + infinitive">
  <Example uk="Я хо́чу ви́вчити украї́нську мо́ву." en="I want to learn Ukrainian." />
  <Example uk="Що ви хо́чете роби́ти за́втра?" en="What do you want to do tomorrow?" note="Formal or plural: ви." />
  <Example uk="Я хоті́ла б подзвони́ти." en="I'd like to make a phone call." note="Said by a woman." />
</ExampleList>

## Мусити: must, have to

<Uk>Му́сити</Uk> means "must, have to". The *I* form changes <Uk>с</Uk> to <Uk>ш</Uk>: <Uk>му́шу</Uk>, <Uk>му́сиш</Uk>, <Uk>му́сить</Uk>, <Uk>му́симо</Uk>, <Uk>му́сите</Uk>, <Uk>му́сять</Uk> [src: goroh-dictionary]. Usage guides describe it as the word for a strong need to do something, sometimes against your wishes [src: goroh-usage].

<ExampleList title="Му́шу + infinitive">
  <Example uk="Я му́шу йти, бо запізню́ся." en="I have to go or I'll be late." note="йти after a vowel." />
  <Example uk="Ви му́сите заплати́ти за квито́к." en="You have to pay for the ticket." note="Formal or plural: ви." />
</ExampleList>

## Уміти: know how to

<Uk>Умі́ти</Uk> or <Uk>вмі́ти</Uk> means "to know how to, to have the skill" [src: goroh-dictionary]. It is a regular first-group verb: <Uk>умі́ю</Uk>, <Uk>умі́єш</Uk>, <Uk>умі́є</Uk>, <Uk>умі́ємо</Uk>, <Uk>умі́єте</Uk>, <Uk>умі́ють</Uk>. Choose <Uk>вмі́ти</Uk> after a vowel and <Uk>умі́ти</Uk> after a consonant [src: pravopys-2026, §23]: <Uk>Вона́ вмі́є</Uk>, but <Uk>Він умі́є</Uk>.

English uses *can* for both "I am able to" and "I know how to". Ukrainian separates them:

- <Uk>умі́ти</Uk> is a skill you have learned: swimming, cooking, driving;
- <Uk>могти́</Uk> is being able or free to do something now, or being allowed to.

<ExampleList title="вмі́ти or могти́?">
  <Example uk="Ти вмі́єш пла́вати?" en="Can you swim?" note="Informal: ти. A skill." />
  <Example uk="Він умі́є гра́ти на гіта́рі." en="He can play the guitar." />
  <Example uk="Я вмі́ю пла́вати, але́ сього́дні не мо́жу: вода́ холо́дна." en="I can swim, but I can't today: the water's cold." note="The skill, and then the situation." />
</ExampleList>

## Треба, потрібно: need to

<Uk>Тре́ба</Uk> and <Uk>потрі́бно</Uk> mean "it is necessary". The person is in the dative. The dictionary gives <Uk>потрі́бно</Uk>, <Uk>необхі́дно</Uk> and <Uk>слід</Uk> as the meaning of <Uk>тре́ба</Uk> [src: goroh-dictionary].

<ExampleList title="Мені́ тре́ба…">
  <Example uk="Мені́ тре́ба йти." en="I have to go." />
  <Example uk="Нам потрі́бно купи́ти хліб." en="We need to buy some bread." />
  <Example uk="Тобі́ тре́ба відпочи́ти." en="You need to rest." note="Informal: ти." />
</ExampleList>

To say that you need a *thing*, use <Uk>потрі́бен</Uk>, <Uk>потрі́бна</Uk>, <Uk>потрі́бне</Uk>, <Uk>потрі́бні</Uk>, which agree with the thing. [Everyday needs](/sentences/everyday-needs/) explains this.

## Можна and не можна: may, may not

<Uk>Мо́жна</Uk> means that something is possible or allowed [src: goroh-dictionary]. As a question, it asks for permission. <Uk>Не мо́жна</Uk>, written as two words, means that something is not allowed. Add a person in the dative if you need one.

<ExampleList title="Мо́жна, не мо́жна">
  <Example uk="Тут мо́жна сиді́ти?" en="Can I sit here?" />
  <Example uk="Мо́жна ввійти́?" en="May I come in?" note="ввійти́ after a vowel." />
  <Example uk="Тут не мо́жна пали́ти." en="Smoking isn't allowed here." />
  <Example uk="Вам не мо́жна ї́сти соло́дке." en="You're not allowed to eat sweet things." note="Formal or plural: ви. вам: dative." />
</ExampleList>

## Слід: should, ought to

<Uk>Слід</Uk> works like <Uk>тре́ба</Uk>: it does not change, and the person is in the dative. The dictionary defines it as "it is necessary, it is worth" [src: goroh-dictionary].

<ExampleList title="Слід + infinitive">
  <Example uk="Вам слід відпочи́ти." en="You ought to rest." note="Formal or plural: ви." />
  <Example uk="Про це слід пам’ята́ти." en="This should be remembered." note="No person: a general rule." />
</ExampleList>

## Повинен: obliged to

<Uk>Пови́нен</Uk> means "obliged to, having the duty to" [src: goroh-dictionary]. It is not a verb. Like an adjective, it agrees with the subject in gender and number:

<GrammarTable
  caption="пови́нен agrees with the subject"
  headers={['Subject', 'Form', 'Example']}
  rows={[
    ['Masculine', 'пови́нен', 'Він пови́нен прийти́.'],
    ['Feminine', 'пови́нна', 'Вона́ пови́нна прийти́.'],
    ['Neuter', 'пови́нне', 'Дитя́ пови́нне спа́ти.'],
    ['Plural', 'пови́нні', 'Ми пови́нні прийти́.'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[1, 2]}
/>

A man says <Uk>я пови́нен</Uk> and a woman says <Uk>я пови́нна</Uk>. With <Uk>ви</Uk> said politely to one person, use <Uk>пови́нні</Uk>.

Usage guides keep <Uk>пови́нен</Uk> for a duty or an obligation. They warn against using it everywhere, especially about things: an advertisement saying that furniture "must be placed correctly" is their example of overuse [src: goroh-usage]. For a strong need, they prefer <Uk>му́сити</Uk>. For a smaller need, they prefer <Uk>ма́ти</Uk>.

## Мати + infinitive: be supposed to

<Uk>Ма́ти</Uk> (to have) with an infinitive means that someone has to or is supposed to do something [src: goroh-dictionary]. Usage guides recommend it for a smaller need, or for something that is expected or likely to happen [src: goroh-usage]. It is conjugated as usual: <Uk>ма́ю</Uk>, <Uk>ма́єш</Uk>, <Uk>ма́є</Uk>.

<ExampleList title="Ма́ю + infinitive">
  <Example uk="Я ма́ю піти́ до лі́каря." en="I have to go to the doctor." />
  <Example uk="По́тяг ма́є прибу́ти о шо́стій." en="The train is due at six." note="Expected to happen." />
</ExampleList>

## Common mistakes and limits

<Note type="mistake">
  **The nominative with <Uk>тре́ба</Uk>, <Uk>мо́жна</Uk> or <Uk>слід</Uk>.** The person goes in the dative: <Uk>Мені́ тре́ба йти</Uk>, <Uk>Вам слід відпочи́ти</Uk>.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **<Uk>Могти́</Uk> for a skill.** "Can you swim?" is <Uk>Ти вмі́єш пла́вати?</Uk> (informal). <Uk>Ти мо́жеш пла́вати?</Uk> usually asks whether you are able to swim now, for example after an injury.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Forgetting agreement with <Uk>пови́нен</Uk>.** It matches the subject: <Uk>він пови́нен</Uk>, <Uk>вона́ пови́нна</Uk>, <Uk>вони́ пови́нні</Uk>.
</Note>

<Note type="remember">
  **<Uk>Не мо́жна</Uk> and <Uk>не тре́ба</Uk> are different.** <Uk>Тут не мо́жна пали́ти</Uk>: smoking is forbidden. <Uk>Не тре́ба поспіша́ти</Uk>: there is no need to hurry. The same is true of <Uk>не му́шу</Uk>: it means "I don't have to", not "I must not".
</Note>
