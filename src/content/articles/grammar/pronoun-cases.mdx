After this page you will know every case form of the Ukrainian personal pronouns: *me*, *to you*, *with him*, *about her*, *for them* and so on. You will know when <Uk>його́</Uk> becomes <Uk>ньо́го</Uk> and <Uk>її́</Uk> becomes <Uk>не́ї</Uk>, and why <Uk>мене́</Uk> is stressed differently in <Uk>до ме́не</Uk>. You will also be able to use <Uk>себе́</Uk> (oneself) and the case forms of <Uk>хто</Uk> and <Uk>що</Uk>.

[Pronouns](/grammar/pronouns/) introduced the nominative forms and a few others, such as <Uk>тебе́</Uk> in <Uk>Як тебе́ зва́ти?</Uk> This page gives the full set. The [case overview](/grammar/case-overview/) explains what each case is for.

Three things make these tables easier than they look:

- **The genitive and the accusative are the same** for every personal pronoun: <Uk>мене́</Uk>, <Uk>тебе́</Uk>, <Uk>його́</Uk>, <Uk>її́</Uk>, <Uk>нас</Uk>, <Uk>вас</Uk>, <Uk>їх</Uk>.
- **<Uk>Він</Uk> and <Uk>воно́</Uk> share all their other forms.** Only the nominative is different.
- **<Uk>Ми</Uk> and <Uk>ви</Uk> are very regular:** <Uk>нас</Uk>, <Uk>нам</Uk>, <Uk>на́ми</Uk> and <Uk>вас</Uk>, <Uk>вам</Uk>, <Uk>ва́ми</Uk>.

<Uk>Ви</Uk> keeps the same forms whether it means one person politely or several people: <Uk>вас</Uk>, <Uk>вам</Uk>, <Uk>ва́ми</Uk>.

## The cases in use

### Genitive

The genitive follows prepositions such as <Uk>у</Uk> (at), <Uk>до</Uk> (to), <Uk>для</Uk> (for) and <Uk>без</Uk> (without). [Common prepositions](/grammar/prepositions/) lists more. The phrase <Uk>у ме́не є</Uk> (I have) uses it too.

<ExampleList title="Genitive">
  <Example uk="У ме́не є пита́ння." en="I have a question." note="Literally: at me there is a question." />
  <Example uk="У ньо́го нема́є ча́су." en="He has no time." />
  <Example uk="Це подару́нок для те́бе." en="This present is for you." note="Informal: те́бе is a form of ти." />
  <Example uk="За́втра я пої́ду до не́ї." en="Tomorrow I'll go and see her." note="Literally: I'll go to her." />
</ExampleList>

### Dative

The dative is the person something is given or said to. It is also the person who feels or experiences something, in sentences where English uses *I*: <Uk>Мені́ хо́лодно</Uk> is "I'm cold", literally "to me it is cold".

<ExampleList title="Dative">
  <Example uk="Мені́ хо́лодно." en="I'm cold." />
  <Example uk="Я тобі́ зателефону́ю за́втра." en="I'll call you tomorrow." note="Informal: тобі́ is a form of ти." />
  <Example uk="Їй подо́бається Ки́їв." en="She likes Kyiv." note="Literally: Kyiv is pleasing to her." />
  <Example uk="Скажі́ть нам, будь ла́ска." en="Tell us, please." note="Formal or plural: скажі́ть is the ви form of the command." />
</ExampleList>

### Accusative

The accusative is the direct object. It also follows some prepositions, such as <Uk>про</Uk> (about) and <Uk>на</Uk> in <Uk>чека́ти на</Uk> (to wait for).

<ExampleList title="Accusative">
  <Example uk="Ти мене́ чу́єш?" en="Can you hear me?" note="Informal: ти." />
  <Example uk="Ми його́ до́бре зна́ємо." en="We know him well." />
  <Example uk="Я чека́ю на не́ї." en="I'm waiting for her." />
  <Example uk="Ми про вас говори́ли." en="We were talking about you." note="Formal or plural: вас is a form of ви." />
</ExampleList>

### Instrumental

The instrumental is used with <Uk>з</Uk> (with, together with), and after some verbs. The form <Uk>мно́ю</Uk> usually takes <Uk>зі</Uk>: <Uk>зі мно́ю</Uk> (with me).

<ExampleList title="Instrumental">
  <Example uk="Ходи́ зі мно́ю!" en="Come with me!" note="Informal: ходи́ is the ти form of the command." />
  <Example uk="Я ча́сто розмовля́ю з ним." en="I often talk to him." />
  <Example uk="Що з тобо́ю?" en="What's wrong with you?" note="Informal: ти. Literally: what is with you?" />
  <Example uk="Ми з ва́ми вже знайо́мі?" en="Have we met before?" note="Formal or plural: ва́ми is a form of ви. Literally: are we and you already acquainted?" />
</ExampleList>

### Locative

The locative is used only after a preposition, such as <Uk>на</Uk> (on) or <Uk>у</Uk>, <Uk>в</Uk> (in). With <Uk>на</Uk> it is a common way to describe what someone is wearing.

<ExampleList title="Locative">
  <Example uk="На ньо́му си́ня ку́ртка." en="He's wearing a blue jacket." note="Literally: on him is a blue jacket." />
  <Example uk="На ній черво́на су́кня." en="She's wearing a red dress." />
</ExampleList>

## The н- forms after a preposition

After a preposition, the forms of <Uk>він</Uk>, <Uk>вона́</Uk>, <Uk>воно́</Uk> and <Uk>вони́</Uk> begin with <Uk>н-</Uk>. The spelling standard calls it an "added <Uk>н</Uk>" (<Uk>приставне́ н</Uk>).

<GrammarTable
  caption="Third-person forms without and with a preposition"
  headers={['Without a preposition', 'After a preposition', 'English']}
  rows={[
    ['його́', 'до ньо́го, для ньо́го', 'him, it'],
    ['її́', 'до не́ї, на не́ї', 'her'],
    ['їх', 'до них, для них', 'them'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[0, 1]}
/>

<ExampleList title="Without and with a preposition">
  <Example uk="Я його́ ба́чу." en="I can see him." />
  <Example uk="Я йду до ньо́го." en="I'm going to his place." note="Literally: I'm going to him." />
  <Example uk="Я їх зна́ю." en="I know them." />
  <Example uk="Це для них." en="This is for them." />
</ExampleList>

The instrumental always has <Uk>н-</Uk>, with or without a preposition: <Uk>ним</Uk>, <Uk>не́ю</Uk>, <Uk>ни́ми</Uk>. The locative always comes after a preposition, so it always has <Uk>н-</Uk> too: <Uk>на ньо́му</Uk>, <Uk>на ній</Uk>, <Uk>на них</Uk>.

The dative has no <Uk>н-</Uk> form: <Uk>йому́</Uk>, <Uk>їй</Uk>, <Uk>їм</Uk>.

### His, her and their do not take н-

<Uk>Його́</Uk>, <Uk>її́</Uk> and <Uk>їх</Uk> are also the words for *his*, *her* and *their* (see [Pronouns](/grammar/pronouns/)). The spelling rule about <Uk>н-</Uk> is for the personal pronouns only, so the possessives never take it, even after a preposition.

<ExampleList title="Him or his?">
  <Example uk="Я йду до ньо́го." en="I'm going to his place." note="ньо́го means him." />
  <Example uk="Я йду до його́ бра́та." en="I'm going to his brother's." note="його́ means his: no н-." />
</ExampleList>

## Stress after a preposition: до мене

<Uk>Мене́</Uk>, <Uk>тебе́</Uk> and <Uk>себе́</Uk> are stressed on the ending. After a preposition, the stress moves to the first syllable: <Uk>до ме́не</Uk>, <Uk>у ме́не</Uk>, <Uk>для те́бе</Uk>, <Uk>на те́бе</Uk>, <Uk>до се́бе</Uk>. The spelling is the same. The spelling standard shows both stresses in its tables.

<ExampleList title="Stress with and without a preposition">
  <Example uk="Ти мене́ чу́єш?" en="Can you hear me?" note="Informal: ти. No preposition: мене́." />
  <Example uk="Іди́ до ме́не!" en="Come to me!" note="Informal: іди́ is the ти form of the command. After до: ме́не." />
</ExampleList>

## The reflexive pronoun себе

<Uk>Себе́</Uk> means *myself*, *yourself*, *himself*, *herself*, *ourselves*, *themselves*: it always refers back to the subject of the sentence, whoever that is. It has no nominative, because it is never the subject.

<GrammarTable
  caption="The reflexive pronoun себе́"
  headers={['Case', 'Form']}
  rows={[
    ['Nominative', '—'],
    ['Genitive', 'себе́ (до се́бе)'],
    ['Dative', 'собі́'],
    ['Accusative', 'себе́ (на се́бе)'],
    ['Instrumental', 'собо́ю'],
    ['Locative', 'на собі́'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[1]}
/>

The forms are like those of <Uk>ти</Uk>: <Uk>тебе́</Uk>, <Uk>тобі́</Uk>, <Uk>тобо́ю</Uk> and <Uk>себе́</Uk>, <Uk>собі́</Uk>, <Uk>собо́ю</Uk>.

<ExampleList title="себе refers back to the subject">
  <Example uk="Він купи́в собі́ нову́ ку́ртку." en="He bought himself a new jacket." />
  <Example uk="Я купи́ла собі́ нову́ ку́ртку." en="I bought myself a new jacket." note="Said by a woman." />
  <Example uk="Візьми́ з собо́ю парасо́льку." en="Take an umbrella with you." note="Informal: візьми́ is the ти form of the command." />
  <Example uk="Розкажі́ть про се́бе." en="Tell us about yourself." note="Formal or plural: розкажі́ть is the ви form of the command." />
</ExampleList>

## хто and що in all cases

[Questions](/grammar/questions/) showed four cases of <Uk>хто</Uk> (who) and <Uk>що</Uk> (what). Here are all of them.

  caption="хто and що in all cases"
  headers={['Case', 'хто', 'що']}
  rows={[
    ['Nominative', 'хто', 'що'],
    ['Genitive', 'кого́', 'чого́'],

As with the personal pronouns, the genitive and accusative of <Uk>хто</Uk> are the same. For <Uk>що</Uk> the accusative is the same as the nominative.

<ExampleList title="хто and що">
  <Example uk="Для кого́ ці кві́ти?" en="Who are these flowers for?" />
  <Example uk="Про що ви гово́рите?" en="What are you talking about?" note="Formal or plural: ви." />
  <Example uk="З ким ти був?" en="Who were you with?" note="Informal: ти. Said to a man or boy." />
</ExampleList>

## Common mistakes and limits

<Note type="mistake">
  **Forgetting <Uk>н-</Uk> after a preposition.** Say <Uk>для ньо́го</Uk>, <Uk>до не́ї</Uk>, <Uk>з ни́ми</Uk>, not *<Uk>для його́</Uk>* or *<Uk>до її́</Uk>*. Only *his*, *her* and *their* stay without <Uk>н-</Uk>: <Uk>до його́ бра́та</Uk>.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Using <Uk>йому́</Uk> or <Uk>його́</Uk> instead of <Uk>себе́</Uk>.** <Uk>Він купи́в собі́ ку́ртку</Uk> means he bought it for himself. <Uk>Він купи́в йому́ ку́ртку</Uk> means he bought it for someone else.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Using the nominative after a preposition.** After a preposition, a pronoun is never in the nominative: <Uk>зі мно́ю</Uk>, not *<Uk>з я</Uk>*; <Uk>для нас</Uk>, not *<Uk>для ми</Uk>*.
</Note>

<Note type="remember">
  Learn pronouns in short phrases: <Uk>у ме́не є</Uk>, <Uk>мені́ хо́лодно</Uk>, <Uk>зі мно́ю</Uk>, <Uk>до ньо́го</Uk>, <Uk>для них</Uk>. The <Uk>н-</Uk> forms and the stress shift come naturally that way.
</Note>

<Note>
  Some pronouns have a second, less common form that this page leaves out. For example, the locative of <Uk>він</Uk> can also be <Uk>нім</Uk>, and the locative of <Uk>хто</Uk> can also be <Uk>кім</Uk>. For the locative of <Uk>що</Uk>, the table shows <Uk>чім</Uk>, the form that the spelling standard and <Uk>Горох</Uk> both give; they mark the stress of its other form differently.
</Note>
