After this page you will be able to form the **[locative](/glossary/#locative)** of most nouns in the singular and plural, and say where someone or something is: <Uk>у Ки́єві</Uk>, <Uk>на столі́</Uk>, <Uk>у па́рку</Uk>. You will know when a masculine noun ends in <Uk>-і</Uk>, <Uk>-у</Uk> or <Uk>-ові</Uk>, how <Uk>г</Uk>, <Uk>к</Uk> and <Uk>х</Uk> change before <Uk>-і</Uk>, and which prepositions take the locative.

This page builds on [The dative case](/grammar/dative/): for many nouns, the two cases share their endings.

## What the locative does

The locative is the only case that never appears without a preposition. That is why it is also called the prepositional case. Its main job is to say where something is, after <Uk>в</Uk> (<Uk>у</Uk>) or <Uk>на</Uk>. It answers the question <Uk>де?</Uk> (where?).

<ExampleList title="The locative for place">
  <Example uk="Я живу́ в Оде́сі." en="I live in Odesa." note="Оде́сі: locative of Оде́са." />
  <Example uk="Кни́га на столі́." en="The book is on the table." note="столі́: locative of стіл." />
  <Example uk="Ді́ти гуля́ють у па́рку." en="The children are playing in the park." note="па́рку: locative of парк." />
</ExampleList>

## Singular endings

The forms below come from the paradigms in the <Uk>Горох</Uk> dictionary [src: goroh-dictionary], and the rules follow the official orthography [src: pravopys-2026, §73, §86, §95].

### Feminine nouns: -і

Feminine nouns take <Uk>-і</Uk>, spelled <Uk>-ї</Uk> after a vowel or an apostrophe [src: pravopys-2026, §73]. This is the same form as the dative. Feminine nouns that end in a consonant, like <Uk>ніч</Uk>, also take <Uk>-і</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §95].

<GrammarTable
  caption="Locative singular of feminine nouns"
  headers={['Dictionary form', 'Locative', 'Meaning']}
  rows={[
    ['шко́ла', 'у шко́лі', 'at school'],
    ['Украї́на', 'в Украї́ні', 'in Ukraine'],
    ['ку́хня', 'на ку́хні', 'in the kitchen'],
    ['пло́ща', 'на пло́щі', 'in the square'],
    ['сім’я́', 'у сім’ї́', 'in the family'],
    ['тінь', 'у ті́ні', 'in the shade'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[0, 1]}
/>

### Consonant changes: на нозі, у руці

Before <Uk>-і</Uk>, the consonants <Uk>г</Uk>, <Uk>к</Uk> and <Uk>х</Uk> change to <Uk>з</Uk>, <Uk>ц</Uk> and <Uk>с</Uk>. The orthography gives <Uk>нога́</Uk> → <Uk>нозі́</Uk>, <Uk>рука́</Uk> → <Uk>руці́</Uk>, <Uk>му́ха</Uk> → <Uk>му́сі</Uk> as examples [src: pravopys-2026, §73, note]. The same change happens in masculine and neuter nouns that take <Uk>-і</Uk>.

<GrammarTable
  caption="Consonant changes before the locative ending"
  headers={['Change', 'Dictionary form', 'Locative', 'Meaning']}
  rows={[
    ['г → з', 'нога́', 'на нозі́', 'on the leg, on the foot'],
    ['г → з', 'доро́га', 'на доро́зі', 'on the road'],
    ['г → з', 'кни́га', 'у кни́зі', 'in the book'],
    ['г → з', 'бе́рег', 'на бе́резі', 'on the bank, on the shore'],
    ['к → ц', 'рука́', 'у руці́', 'in the hand'],
    ['к → ц', 'Аме́рика', 'в Аме́риці', 'in America'],
    ['х → с', 'по́верх', 'на по́версі', 'on the floor (storey)'],
    ['х → с', 'ву́хо', 'у ву́сі', 'in the ear'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[0, 1, 2]}
/>

The dictionary also gives <Uk>у ву́ху</Uk> for "in the ear" [src: goroh-dictionary]. The change happens only before <Uk>-і</Uk>. When a noun takes <Uk>-у</Uk>, the consonant stays: <Uk>у па́рку</Uk>.

### Masculine and neuter nouns: -і, -у or -ові

This is the tricky part. Masculine and neuter nouns can take three different endings. The orthography describes which nouns usually take which [src: pravopys-2026, §86]. Treat these as tendencies and check new words in a dictionary.

**<Uk>-і</Uk> (<Uk>-ї</Uk>)** is usual for most nouns that name things, especially short ones without a suffix, and for most neuter nouns:

<GrammarTable
  caption="Masculine and neuter nouns with -і"
  headers={['Dictionary form', 'Locative', 'Meaning']}
  rows={[
    ['стіл', 'на столі́', 'on the table'],
    ['телефо́н', 'у телефо́ні', 'in the phone, on the phone'],
    ['магази́н', 'у магази́ні', 'in the shop'],
    ['Ки́їв', 'у Ки́єві', 'in Kyiv'],
    ['Львів', 'у Льво́ві', 'in Lviv'],
    ['мі́сто', 'у мі́сті', 'in the town, in the city'],
    ['село́', 'у селі́', 'in the village'],
    ['мо́ре', 'на мо́рі', 'at the seaside, on the sea'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[0, 1]}
/>

**<Uk>-у</Uk> (<Uk>-ю</Uk>)** is usual for nouns with the suffix <Uk>-к</Uk> or <Uk>-ок</Uk>, such as <Uk>буди́нок</Uk> (building) and <Uk>лі́жко</Uk> (bed), and for some one-syllable nouns whose stress moves to the ending, such as <Uk>сніг</Uk> (snow) [src: pravopys-2026, §86]. Many other nouns ending in <Uk>-к</Uk>, <Uk>-г</Uk> or <Uk>-х</Uk> also take it. For <Uk>парк</Uk>, for example, the dictionary gives <Uk>па́рку</Uk> as the first locative form, not a form in <Uk>-і</Uk> [src: goroh-dictionary].

<GrammarTable
  caption="Masculine and neuter nouns with -у"
  headers={['Dictionary form', 'Locative', 'Meaning']}
  rows={[
    ['буди́нок', 'у буди́нку', 'in the building'],
    ['лі́жко', 'у лі́жку', 'in bed'],
    ['парк', 'у па́рку', 'in the park'],
    ['сніг', 'на снігу́', 'on the snow'],
    ['сад', 'у саду́', 'in the garden'],
    ['музе́й', 'у музе́ї, у музе́ю', 'in the museum'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[0, 1]}
/>

**<Uk>-ові</Uk> (<Uk>-еві</Uk>, <Uk>-єві</Uk>)** is usual for masculine nouns that name people and animals [src: pravopys-2026, §86]. You will meet it less often in practice, because people are seldom the place where something is. With nouns for people, the dictionary often gives several correct forms.

<GrammarTable
  caption="Masculine nouns for people"
  headers={['Dictionary form', 'Locative', 'Meaning']}
  rows={[
    ['ба́тько', 'на ба́тькові, на ба́тьку', 'on father'],
    ['вчи́тель', 'на вчи́телеві, на вчи́телю, на вчи́телі', 'on the teacher'],
    ['студе́нт', 'на студе́нтові, на студе́нті', 'on the student'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[0, 1]}
/>

<ExampleList title="The locative of people">
  <Example uk="На ба́тькові була́ нова́ ку́ртка." en="Father was wearing a new jacket." note="Literally: on father was a new jacket." />
  <Example uk="При вчи́телеві ді́ти мовча́ли." en="The children kept quiet in front of the teacher." note="при: in the presence of." />
</ExampleList>

## Plural endings

The plural is regular. All nouns take <Uk>-ах</Uk>, or <Uk>-ях</Uk> after a soft consonant or a vowel [src: pravopys-2026, §80, §93, §96].

<GrammarTable
  caption="Locative plural"
  headers={['Dictionary form', 'Nominative plural', 'Locative plural']}
  rows={[
    ['мі́сто', 'міста́', 'у міста́х'],
    ['гора́', 'го́ри', 'у го́рах'],
    ['кімна́та', 'кімна́ти', 'у кімна́тах'],
    ['ву́лиця', 'ву́лиці', 'на ву́лицях'],
    ['ді́ти', 'ді́ти', 'на ді́тях'],
    ['Карпа́ти', 'Карпа́ти', 'у Карпа́тах'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[0, 1, 2]}
/>

<Uk>Карпа́ти</Uk> (the Carpathians) exists only in the plural, like *the Alps* in English.

## Pronouns in the locative

These forms come from the orthography's table [src: pravopys-2026, §108]. Because the locative always follows a preposition, the forms of <Uk>він</Uk>, <Uk>вона́</Uk> and <Uk>вони́</Uk> begin with <Uk>н-</Uk>.

<GrammarTable
  caption="Personal pronouns in the locative, after на"
  headers={['Pronoun', 'Locative', 'Meaning']}
  rows={[
    ['я', 'на мені́', 'on me'],
    ['ти', 'на тобі́', 'on you (informal)'],
    ['він, воно́', 'на ньо́му, на нім', 'on him, on it'],
    ['вона́', 'на ній', 'on her'],
    ['ми', 'на нас', 'on us'],
    ['ви', 'на вас', 'on you (formal or plural)'],
    ['вони́', 'на них', 'on them'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[0, 1]}
/>

A natural use is for clothes. Ukrainian says that a dress is "on" someone, where English says that someone is wearing it. The NaUKMA course uses this pattern to introduce the locative [src: ukma-ukrainian-foreign-language, Lesson 5].

<Example uk="На ній краси́ва су́кня." en="She's wearing a beautiful dress." note="Literally: on her is a beautiful dress." />

## Prepositions with the locative

Five prepositions take the locative. Each is listed with the locative in the dictionary [src: goroh-dictionary].

<GrammarTable
  caption="Prepositions that take the locative"
  headers={['Preposition', 'Meaning with the locative', 'Example']}
  rows={[
    ['в, у', 'in, at', 'у шко́лі (at school)'],
    ['на', 'on, at', 'на робо́ті (at work)'],
    ['по', 'around, across, along', 'по мі́сті (around the town)'],
    ['при', 'by, near; in the presence of', 'при вхо́ді (by the entrance)'],
    ['о, об', 'at (a time of day)', 'о дру́гій (at two)'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[0, 2]}
/>

### В and на: place

With the locative, <Uk>в</Uk> (<Uk>у</Uk>) and <Uk>на</Uk> describe where something is. Which noun takes which preposition has to be learned word by word: <Uk>у шко́лі</Uk>, but <Uk>на робо́ті</Uk>. [Common prepositions](/grammar/prepositions/) has a list, and the rules for choosing <Uk>у</Uk> or <Uk>в</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §23].

### По: around, across, along

<Uk>По</Uk> with the locative describes movement over a surface or along something, or being spread around a place [src: goroh-dictionary].

<ExampleList title="По with the locative">
  <Example uk="Ми до́вго гуля́ли по мі́сті." en="We walked around the town for a long time." />
  <Example uk="Вона́ ї́здить по міста́х Украї́ни." en="She travels around the towns of Ukraine." note="міста́х: locative plural." />
  <Example uk="Він ходи́в по кімна́ті." en="He was walking about the room." />
</ExampleList>

With <Uk>по</Uk>, some nouns have two correct singular forms: <Uk>по мі́сті</Uk> and <Uk>по мі́сту</Uk>, <Uk>по селі́</Uk> and <Uk>по селу́</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §86, note 1]. In the plural, though, the ending must be the locative <Uk>-ах</Uk> or <Uk>-ях</Uk>: <Uk>по кімна́тах</Uk>, not the dative *<Uk>по кімна́там</Uk>*. Usage guides call the dative after <Uk>по</Uk> a mistake under Russian influence [src: goroh-usage].

<Uk>По</Uk> can also take the accusative, with other meanings. One you will hear is "to fetch": <Uk>піти́ по хліб</Uk> (to go and get bread) [src: goroh-usage].

### При: by, near, in the presence of

<Uk>При</Uk> means *by* or *near* something, and also *in someone's presence* [src: goroh-dictionary]. It is less common than the other four in everyday speech.

<ExampleList title="При with the locative">
  <Example uk="Зустрі́немося при вхо́ді." en="Let's meet by the entrance." />
  <Example uk="Не говори́ так при ді́тях." en="Don't talk like that in front of the children." note="Informal: ти (говори́)." />
</ExampleList>

### О, об: at a time

<Uk>О</Uk> with the locative tells the time of day: <Uk>о дру́гій</Uk> (at two). Before a vowel it becomes <Uk>об</Uk>: <Uk>об одина́дцятій</Uk> (at eleven) [src: goroh-dictionary]. [Time](/sentences/time/) explains these forms.

## Place or direction: a recap

The locative answers <Uk>де?</Uk> (where?). For a direction, <Uk>куди́?</Uk> (where to?), <Uk>в</Uk> and <Uk>на</Uk> take the accusative instead. <Uk>Під</Uk> and <Uk>за</Uk> work the same way with the instrumental for place: see [The instrumental case](/grammar/instrumental/).

<GrammarTable
  caption="Where versus where to"
  headers={['Place: where?', 'Direction: where to?']}

    ['на робо́ті (locative)', 'на робо́ту (accusative)'],
    ['у Ки́єві (locative)', 'у Ки́їв (accusative)'],
    ['під столо́м (instrumental)', 'під стіл (accusative)'],
  ]}

/>

<ExampleList title="Place and direction">
  <Example uk="Ми живемо́ у Льво́ві." en="We live in Lviv." note="у before льв-." />
  <Example uk="Ми ї́демо у Львів." en="We're going to Lviv." note="Львів: accusative, the same as the dictionary form." />
</ExampleList>

## Common mistakes and limits

<Note type="mistake">
  **Forgetting the consonant change.** <Uk>У кни́зі</Uk>, not *<Uk>у кни́гі</Uk>*; <Uk>у руці́</Uk>, not *<Uk>у рукі́</Uk>*. Before <Uk>-і</Uk>, <Uk>г</Uk>, <Uk>к</Uk> and <Uk>х</Uk> become <Uk>з</Uk>, <Uk>ц</Uk> and <Uk>с</Uk>.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Using the dative plural after <Uk>по</Uk>.** Say <Uk>по кімна́тах</Uk>, <Uk>по міста́х</Uk>, not *<Uk>по кімна́там</Uk>*, *<Uk>по міста́м</Uk>* [src: goroh-usage].
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Using the locative for a direction.** <Uk>Я в шко́лі</Uk> means "I'm at school". "I'm going to school" needs the accusative: <Uk>Я йду в шко́лу</Uk>.
</Note>

<Note type="remember">
  Feminine nouns take <Uk>-і</Uk> in the locative singular, with <Uk>г</Uk>, <Uk>к</Uk>, <Uk>х</Uk> → <Uk>з</Uk>, <Uk>ц</Uk>, <Uk>с</Uk>. Masculine and neuter nouns take <Uk>-і</Uk> or <Uk>-у</Uk>, and nouns for people often take <Uk>-ові</Uk>: when in doubt, check the dictionary. Every plural ends in <Uk>-ах</Uk> or <Uk>-ях</Uk>.
</Note>

<Note>
  This page covers nouns and personal pronouns. Adjectives and words like *my* and *this* also have locative forms that agree with the noun. They are covered on [a separate page](/grammar/adjective-cases/).
</Note>
