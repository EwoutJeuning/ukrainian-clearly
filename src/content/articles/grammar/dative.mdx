After this page you will be able to form the **[dative](/glossary/#dative)** of most nouns in the singular and plural, and use it for the person you give something to, write to, phone or help. You will be able to say what you like with <Uk>мені́ подо́бається</Uk>, say how old you are, and say how you feel with phrases like <Uk>мені́ хо́лодно</Uk>. You will also know the few prepositions that take the dative.

[Cases, an overview](/grammar/case-overview/) introduced the dative as the case for "to" or "for" someone. This page gives the endings and the main uses.

## What the dative does

The dative usually names the person who receives something or who is affected by an action. In English this is often the word after *to* or *for*: *give the book **to my brother***. Ukrainian uses no preposition here. The ending alone does the job.

<ExampleList title="The dative for the receiver">
  <Example uk="Я даю́ кни́гу бра́тові." en="I'm giving the book to my brother." note="бра́тові: dative of брат. кни́гу is the direct object (accusative)." />
  <Example uk="Я пишу́ листа́ сестрі́." en="I'm writing a letter to my sister." note="сестрі́: dative of сестра́." />
</ExampleList>

The question for the dative is <Uk>кому́?</Uk> (to whom?) for people and <Uk>чому́?</Uk> (to what?) for things.

## Singular endings

The ending depends on the kind of noun. The tables group nouns the way Ukrainian grammars do, by **declension** (<Uk>відмі́на</Uk>): roughly, feminine nouns in <Uk>-а</Uk> or <Uk>-я</Uk>; masculine and neuter nouns; and feminine nouns that end in a consonant. All the forms below come from the paradigms in the <Uk>Горох</Uk> dictionary [src: goroh-dictionary], and the endings follow the official orthography [src: pravopys-2026, §70, §83, §95].

### Feminine nouns in -а and -я

These nouns take <Uk>-і</Uk>. After a vowel or an apostrophe, the ending is spelled <Uk>-ї</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §70].

<GrammarTable
  caption="Dative singular of feminine nouns in -а and -я"
  headers={['Dictionary form', 'Dative', 'Meaning']}
  rows={[
    ['сестра́', 'сестрі́', 'sister'],
    ['ма́ма', 'ма́мі', 'Mum'],
    ['по́друга', 'по́друзі', 'friend (female)'],
    ['ку́хня', 'ку́хні', 'kitchen'],
    ['пло́ща', 'пло́щі', 'square'],
    ['наді́я', 'наді́ї', 'hope'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[0, 1]}
/>

Notice <Uk>по́друга</Uk> → <Uk>по́друзі</Uk>: the <Uk>г</Uk> changed to <Uk>з</Uk>. This change is explained near the end of this page.

The dative of these nouns is the same as their locative: <Uk>сестрі́</Uk> can be "to my sister" or, after a preposition, "on my sister". See [The locative case](/grammar/locative/).

### Masculine nouns: -ові or -у

Most masculine nouns have **two correct dative endings**: <Uk>-ові</Uk> or <Uk>-у</Uk>. Soft-stem nouns, such as <Uk>лі́кар</Uk>, take <Uk>-еві</Uk> or <Uk>-ю</Uk>. After a vowel the long ending is spelled <Uk>-єві</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §83].

<GrammarTable
  caption="Dative singular of masculine nouns"
  headers={['Dictionary form', 'Dative', 'Meaning']}
  rows={[
    ['брат', 'бра́тові, бра́ту', 'brother'],
    ['ба́тько', 'ба́тькові, ба́тьку', 'father'],
    ['студе́нт', 'студе́нтові, студе́нту', 'student'],
    ['друг', 'дру́гові, дру́гу', 'friend'],
    ['лі́кар', 'лі́кареві, лі́карю', 'doctor'],
    ['вчи́тель', 'вчи́телеві, вчи́телю', 'teacher'],
    ['музе́й', 'музе́ю, музе́єві', 'museum'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[0, 1]}
/>

Both forms in each row are correct, and both are listed by the dictionary [src: goroh-dictionary]. A few things help you choose:

- **Both endings can look like other cases.** The short ending <Uk>-у</Uk> can also be the genitive or the locative: <Uk>па́рку</Uk> is all three for <Uk>парк</Uk>. After a preposition, <Uk>-ові</Uk> and <Uk>-у</Uk> can also be locative: <Uk>на ба́тькові</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §86, note 1]. Without a preposition, a noun for a person in <Uk>-ові</Uk> is dative.
- **Vary them in a list.** When several masculine nouns in the dative come together, the orthography recommends putting <Uk>-ові</Uk> or <Uk>-еві</Uk> first and <Uk>-у</Uk> or <Uk>-ю</Uk> after it, so that the endings are not all the same [src: pravopys-2026, §83, note].
- **Town names in <Uk>-ів</Uk> take only <Uk>-у</Uk>.** Names like <Uk>Львів</Uk> and <Uk>Ки́їв</Uk> have only the short ending: <Uk>Льво́ву</Uk>, <Uk>Ки́єву</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §83].

### Neuter nouns: -у or -ю

Neuter nouns take <Uk>-у</Uk>, or <Uk>-ю</Uk> after a soft consonant [src: pravopys-2026, §83]. A few, such as <Uk>мі́сто</Uk>, can also take <Uk>-ові</Uk>.

<GrammarTable
  caption="Dative singular of neuter nouns"
  headers={['Dictionary form', 'Dative', 'Meaning']}
  rows={[
    ['село́', 'селу́', 'village'],
    ['мі́сто', 'мі́сту, мі́стові', 'town, city'],
    ['мо́ре', 'мо́рю', 'sea'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[0, 1]}
/>

### Feminine nouns ending in a consonant

Feminine nouns like <Uk>ніч</Uk> (night) and <Uk>ра́дість</Uk> (joy) take <Uk>-і</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §95]. In some of them the vowel of the stem changes too: <Uk>ніч</Uk> → <Uk>но́чі</Uk>, <Uk>сіль</Uk> (salt) → <Uk>со́лі</Uk> [src: goroh-dictionary].

<Uk>Ма́ти</Uk> (mother) is irregular: its dative is <Uk>ма́тері</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §97]. In everyday speech most people say <Uk>ма́ма</Uk>, which is regular: <Uk>ма́мі</Uk>.

## Plural endings

The plural is simple. All nouns take <Uk>-ам</Uk>, or <Uk>-ям</Uk> after a soft consonant or a vowel [src: pravopys-2026, §77, §90, §96].

    ['друг', 'дру́зі', 'дру́зям'],

<Uk>Ді́ти</Uk> (children) and <Uk>лю́ди</Uk> (people) are the plurals of <Uk>дити́на</Uk> and <Uk>люди́на</Uk>. They are worth learning as they are: <Uk>ді́тям</Uk>, <Uk>лю́дям</Uk>.

## Giving, writing, phoning and helping

Verbs of giving and telling take the receiver in the dative: <Uk>дава́ти</Uk> (to give), <Uk>писа́ти</Uk> (to write), <Uk>каза́ти</Uk> (to say, to tell). The thing given or written is in the [accusative](/grammar/accusative/).

<ExampleList title="The receiver in the dative">
  <Example uk="Ма́ма дає́ ді́тям я́блука." en="Mum is giving the children apples." note="ді́тям: dative plural." />
  <Example uk="Я пишу́ вам із Ки́єва." en="I'm writing to you from Kyiv." note="Formal or plural: ви. із, not з, between two consonants (вам із)." />
  <Example uk="Скажі́ть йому́, будь ла́ска." en="Please tell him." note="Formal or plural: ви (скажі́ть)." />
</ExampleList>

Some verbs take the dative where English has a direct object. These are worth learning together with the case:

- <Uk>телефонува́ти</Uk> (to phone): <Uk>Я телефону́ю ма́мі.</Uk> (I'm phoning Mum.) Usage guides prefer <Uk>телефонува́ти</Uk> to <Uk>дзвони́ти</Uk> for phone calls [src: goroh-usage].
- <Uk>допомага́ти</Uk> (to help): <Uk>Він допомага́є бра́тові.</Uk> (He helps his brother.) The dictionary defines it as giving help to someone, in the dative [src: goroh-dictionary].
- <Uk>дя́кувати</Uk> (to thank): <Uk>Дя́кую вам!</Uk> (Thank you!) In standard Ukrainian it takes only the dative [src: goroh-usage].

<ExampleList title="Verbs that take the dative">
  <Example uk="Зателефону́й мені́ за́втра." en="Phone me tomorrow." note="Informal: ти (зателефону́й)." />
  <Example uk="Ми допомага́ємо сусі́дам." en="We help our neighbours." note="сусі́дам: dative plural of сусі́д." />
  <Example uk="Дя́кую тобі́ за допомо́гу." en="Thank you for your help." note="Informal: ти (тобі́)." />
</ExampleList>

## Мені подобається: what you like

The usual way to say "I like" turns the English sentence round. The thing you like is the subject, in the nominative. The person who likes it is in the dative. Literally, *the city pleases me*.

<ExampleList title="Подо́батися: to be liked">
  <Example uk="Мені́ подо́бається Ки́їв." en="I like Kyiv." note="Literally: Kyiv pleases me." />
  <Example uk="Мені́ подо́баються ці кни́ги." en="I like these books." note="Plural subject, so the verb is plural: подо́баються." />
  <Example uk="Тобі́ подо́бається ка́ва?" en="Do you like coffee?" note="Informal: ти (тобі́)." />
  <Example uk="Йому́ подо́бається ця пі́сня." en="He likes this song." />
</ExampleList>

The verb <Uk>подо́батися</Uk> takes the dative of the person [src: goroh-dictionary]. Because the liked thing is the subject, the verb agrees with it, not with the person: <Uk>подо́бається</Uk> for one thing, <Uk>подо́баються</Uk> for several.

## Saying your age

Age also uses the dative. Literally, Ukrainian says "to me are twenty years".

<ExampleList title="Age">
  <Example uk="Мені́ два́дцять років." en="I'm twenty." />
  <Example uk="Скі́льки вам років?" en="How old are you?" note="Formal or plural: ви (вам)." />
  <Example uk="Си́нові три роки." en="My son is three." note="си́нові: dative of син." />
  <Example uk="Бабу́сі вісімдеся́т оди́н рік." en="Grandma is eighty-one." />
</ExampleList>

The word for "year" changes with the number, as <Uk>гри́вня</Uk> does on the [shopping page](/sentences/shopping/): <Uk>оди́н рік</Uk> (1), <Uk>два роки</Uk> (2 to 4), <Uk>п’ять років</Uk> (5 and up, including 11 to 14). In a longer number only the last word counts: <Uk>два́дцять оди́н рік</Uk>, <Uk>два́дцять два роки</Uk> [src: ukma-ukrainian-foreign-language, Lesson 8]. The dictionary accepts two stresses in <Uk>роки</Uk> and <Uk>років</Uk>, so they are shown without a mark [src: goroh-dictionary].

## Мені холодно, мені треба: how you feel and what you need

Many words that describe a state are used with the dative of the person. There is no subject and no verb in the present tense. Literally, *to me [it is] cold*.

<ExampleList title="State words with the dative">
  <Example uk="Мені́ хо́лодно." en="I'm cold." />
  <Example uk="Ді́тям ціка́во." en="The children are interested." note="Literally: to the children it is interesting." />
  <Example uk="Тобі́ не су́мно?" en="Aren't you sad?" note="Informal: ти (тобі́)." />
  <Example uk="Нам тре́ба йти." en="We have to go." />
</ExampleList>

The dictionary gives <Uk>хо́лодно</Uk> with the dative of the person who feels cold, and its examples include <Uk>мені́ ціка́во</Uk> [src: goroh-dictionary]. [Everyday needs](/sentences/everyday-needs/) has more on <Uk>мені́ тре́ба</Uk> and <Uk>мені́ потрі́бен</Uk>.

In the past and the future, add <Uk>було́</Uk> or <Uk>бу́де</Uk>, always in this neuter form: <Uk>Мені́ було́ хо́лодно.</Uk> (I was cold.) <Uk>Тобі́ бу́де ціка́во.</Uk> (You'll find it interesting. Informal: <Uk>ти</Uk>.)

## Prepositions with the dative

Most prepositions take other cases. The dictionary marks only a few common ones as taking the dative [src: goroh-dictionary]:

    ['завдяки́', 'thanks to', 'завдяки́ дру́гові (thanks to a friend)'],
    ['всу́переч, усу́переч', 'despite, contrary to', 'всу́переч пого́ді (despite the weather)'],

/>

<Uk>Всу́переч</Uk> and <Uk>усу́переч</Uk> are the same word, and the choice follows the <Uk>у</Uk>/<Uk>в</Uk> rule [src: pravopys-2026, §23]. <Uk>Назу́стріч</Uk> is first of all an adverb (*towards each other*); the dictionary lists its use as a preposition with the dative separately [src: goroh-dictionary].

<ExampleList title="Prepositions with the dative">
  <Example uk="Завдяки́ вам я все зрозумі́в." en="Thanks to you, I understood everything." note="Formal or plural: ви (вам). A man speaking: зрозумі́в." />
  <Example uk="Ми пішли́ гуля́ти всу́переч пого́ді." en="We went for a walk despite the weather." />
</ExampleList>

## Consonant changes before -і

Before the ending <Uk>-і</Uk>, the consonants <Uk>г</Uk>, <Uk>к</Uk> and <Uk>х</Uk> change to <Uk>з</Uk>, <Uk>ц</Uk> and <Uk>с</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §73, note]. This happens in the dative and in the locative of feminine nouns in <Uk>-а</Uk>.

<GrammarTable
  caption="Consonant changes before the dative ending"
  headers={['Change', 'Dictionary form', 'Dative', 'Meaning']}
  rows={[
    ['г → з', 'нога́', 'нозі́', 'leg, foot'],
    ['г → з', 'кни́га', 'кни́зі', 'book'],
    ['г → з', 'по́друга', 'по́друзі', 'friend (female)'],
    ['к → ц', 'рука́', 'руці́', 'hand, arm'],
    ['к → ц', 'ру́чка', 'ру́чці', 'pen'],
    ['х → с', 'му́ха', 'му́сі', 'fly'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[0, 1, 2]}
/>

Only the last consonant of the stem changes, so check what comes just before the ending: <Uk>кни́га</Uk> ends in <Uk>-га</Uk> and becomes <Uk>кни́зі</Uk>; <Uk>ру́чка</Uk> ends in <Uk>-ка</Uk> and becomes <Uk>ру́чці</Uk>.

## Common mistakes and limits

<Note type="mistake">
  **Adding a preposition for "to".** The dative does the work of English *to* on its own: <Uk>Я даю́ кни́гу бра́тові</Uk>, not *<Uk>до бра́та</Uk>* when you mean giving. <Uk>До</Uk> with the genitive means movement towards someone: <Uk>Я йду до бра́та</Uk> (I'm going to my brother's).
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Translating "I like" word for word.** *<Uk>Я подо́баюся Ки́їв</Uk>* is wrong. Say <Uk>Мені́ подо́бається Ки́їв</Uk>, with the person in the dative and the liked thing as the subject. (<Uk>Я подо́баюся</Uk> exists, but it means that someone likes *me*: <Uk>Я тобі́ подо́баюся?</Uk>, "Do you like me?", informal.)
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Using <Uk>к</Uk> for "to".** The dictionary marks the preposition <Uk>к</Uk> as old-fashioned [src: goroh-dictionary]. In modern Ukrainian, use <Uk>до</Uk> with the genitive: <Uk>до ма́ми</Uk>, not *<Uk>к ма́мі</Uk>*.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Thanking with the accusative.** *<Uk>Дя́кую вас</Uk>* is a calque; usage guides call it an error. Say <Uk>Дя́кую вам</Uk> [src: goroh-usage].
</Note>

<Note type="remember">
  For masculine nouns, both <Uk>-ові</Uk> and <Uk>-у</Uk> are correct in the dative: <Uk>бра́тові</Uk> and <Uk>бра́ту</Uk>. Feminine nouns in <Uk>-а</Uk> or <Uk>-я</Uk> take <Uk>-і</Uk>, often with a consonant change: <Uk>кни́зі</Uk>, <Uk>руці́</Uk>. In the plural, everything ends in <Uk>-ам</Uk> or <Uk>-ям</Uk>.
</Note>

<Note>
  This page covers nouns and personal pronouns. Adjectives and words like *my* and *this* also have dative forms, which agree with the noun. They are covered on [a separate page](/grammar/adjective-cases/).
</Note>
