After this page you will be able to conjugate a **[reflexive verb](/glossary/#reflexive-verb)**, a verb ending in <Uk>-ся</Uk>, in the present and past tenses. You will know when the short form <Uk>-сь</Uk> appears, and you will recognise the main meanings of <Uk>-ся</Uk>: doing something to yourself, doing something to each other, something happening by itself, and verbs that simply always have <Uk>-ся</Uk>. You will also know why Ukrainians do not apologise with <Uk>я вибача́юся</Uk>.

## What -ся is

<Uk>-ся</Uk> is a small ending that goes at the very end of a verb, after every other ending. The orthography calls it a **postfix** (<Uk>постфі́кс</Uk>) and says it is written together with the verb [src: pravopys-2026, §115, part 4, note 2]. Apart from that, a reflexive verb is conjugated like any other verb [src: ukma-ukrainian-foreign-language].

Compare <Uk>ми́ти</Uk> (to wash something) with <Uk>ми́тися</Uk> (to wash, to wash yourself). The dictionary defines <Uk>ми́тися</Uk> as "to wash oneself" [src: goroh-dictionary].

<ExampleList title="With and without -ся">
  <Example uk="Я ми́ю по́суд." en="I'm washing the dishes." note="ми́ти + accusative: you wash something." />
  <Example uk="Я ми́юся." en="I'm having a wash." note="ми́тися: you wash yourself. There is no object." />
</ExampleList>

## Conjugation

Take the ordinary present-tense form and add <Uk>-ся</Uk>. There is one change to learn: in the *he/she/it* form, <Uk>-ть-</Uk> appears before <Uk>-ся</Uk>, even in first-group verbs that have no <Uk>т</Uk> without it [src: pravopys-2026, §115, part 4, note 2]. So <Uk>вмива́є</Uk> becomes <Uk>вмива́ється</Uk>.

All the forms come from the <Uk>Горох</Uk> dictionary [src: goroh-dictionary]. The [present tense](/grammar/present-tense/) page explains the endings themselves.

The dictionary lists this verb as both <Uk>вмива́тися</Uk> and <Uk>умива́тися</Uk>. Choose the way you choose between <Uk>в</Uk> and <Uk>у</Uk> anywhere else: <Uk>в-</Uk> after a vowel, <Uk>у-</Uk> after a consonant [src: pravopys-2026, §23]. So: <Uk>Я вмива́юся</Uk>, but <Uk>Він умива́ється</Uk>.

### The past tense

### Two verbs with moving stress

<Uk>Смія́тися</Uk> (to laugh) and <Uk>боя́тися</Uk> (to be afraid) are very common. <Uk>Смія́тися</Uk> is in the first group and <Uk>боя́тися</Uk> in the second [src: pravopys-2026, §115]. In both, the stress is on the ending in the *we* and *you* (<Uk>ви</Uk>) forms.

## -ся or -сь?

You will also see a short form, <Uk>-сь</Uk>: <Uk>вмива́юсь</Uk>, <Uk>смія́лась</Uk>. The orthography says that <Uk>-ся</Uk> can change to <Uk>-сь</Uk> "in spoken language and in literary style". It can do this after the infinitive, after past-tense endings, and after the *I*, *we* and *you* (<Uk>ви</Uk>) endings of the future, as in <Uk>візьму́сь</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §115, part 7, note 3]. The dictionary also gives present forms such as <Uk>вмива́юсь</Uk> and <Uk>вмива́ємось</Uk> [src: goroh-dictionary].

- **Feminine, neuter and plural past forms:** <Uk>смія́лась</Uk>, <Uk>смія́лось</Uk>, <Uk>смія́лись</Uk>.
- **The masculine past form** only when it ends in <Uk>в</Uk>: <Uk>вмива́всь</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §115, part 7, note 3].
- **The *you* (<Uk>ти</Uk>), *he/she/it* and *they* forms** do not shorten: always <Uk>вмива́єшся</Uk>, <Uk>вмива́ється</Uk>, <Uk>вмива́ються</Uk>. The dictionary gives no <Uk>-сь</Uk> form for them [src: goroh-dictionary].

This site writes <Uk>-ся</Uk> everywhere. Learn <Uk>-сь</Uk> so that you recognise it, especially in songs, poems and speech.

## What -ся means

The name "reflexive" suggests "oneself", but that is only one of the meanings. Here are the main ones you will meet.

### Doing something to yourself

The action goes back to the person who does it: washing, dressing.

<ExampleList title="To yourself">
  <Example uk="Я вмива́юся холо́дною водо́ю." en="I wash my face with cold water." note="холо́дною водо́ю: instrumental, “with cold water”." />
  <Example uk="Ти до́вго одяга́єшся." en="You take a long time to get dressed." note="Informal: ти." />
  <Example uk="Ді́ти ми́ються." en="The children are having a wash." />
</ExampleList>

### Doing something to each other

With a plural subject, or with <Uk>з</Uk> + the [instrumental](/grammar/instrumental/), some verbs mean "each other". The dictionary defines <Uk>цілува́тися</Uk> as "to kiss one another" [src: goroh-dictionary].

<ExampleList title="Each other">
  <Example uk="Ми зустріча́ємося о шо́стій." en="We're meeting at six." />
  <Example uk="Я зустріча́юся з дру́гом." en="I'm meeting a friend." note="з дру́гом: instrumental of друг." />
  <Example uk="Вони́ цілу́ються." en="They're kissing." />
</ExampleList>

### Something happening by itself

Compare <Uk>почина́ти</Uk> (to start something) and <Uk>почина́тися</Uk> (to start, to begin). With <Uk>-ся</Uk>, the thing that starts is the subject, and nobody is named as starting it [src: goroh-dictionary].

<ExampleList title="It starts, it ends">
  <Example uk="Учи́тель почина́є уро́к." en="The teacher starts the lesson." note="почина́ти + accusative." />
  <Example uk="Уро́к почина́ється о дев’я́тій." en="The lesson starts at nine." />
  <Example uk="Фільм закі́нчується пі́зно." en="The film finishes late." />
</ExampleList>

### Verbs that always have -ся

Some verbs have no form without <Uk>-ся</Uk>: the dictionary has no entry for them without it [src: goroh-dictionary]. Here <Uk>-ся</Uk> has no meaning of its own. Just learn the verb as it is.

<Uk>Боя́тися</Uk> takes the [genitive](/grammar/genitive/): you are afraid *of* someone or something [src: goroh-dictionary].

<ExampleList title="Always with -ся">
  <Example uk="Чому́ ти сміє́шся?" en="Why are you laughing?" note="Informal: ти." />
  <Example uk="Я бою́ся соба́к." en="I'm afraid of dogs." note="соба́к: genitive plural of соба́ка." />
</ExampleList>

### "I like": подобатися

<Uk>Подо́батися</Uk> means "to be pleasing". The thing you like is the subject, and the person who likes it is in the [dative](/grammar/dative/). The verb agrees with the thing you like [src: goroh-dictionary].

<ExampleList title="Мені́ подо́бається…">
  <Example uk="Мені́ подо́бається це мі́сто." en="I like this city." note="мі́сто is the subject; мені́ is dative." />
  <Example uk="Мені́ подо́баються ці пі́сні." en="I like these songs." note="Plural subject, so the verb is plural: подо́баються." />
</ExampleList>

### Something being done

With a thing as the subject, <Uk>-ся</Uk> can also mean that something *is being done*, without saying by whom. The dictionary marks this as a passive use of <Uk>будува́тися</Uk>, with examples about a castle and a new metro being built [src: goroh-dictionary].

<ExampleList title="Being done">
  <Example uk="Тут буду́ється шко́ла." en="A school is being built here." />
  <Example uk="Буди́нок будува́вся до́вго." en="The house took a long time to build." note="Literally “the house was being built for a long time”." />
</ExampleList>

Treat this as a pattern to recognise. When you know who does the action, an ordinary active sentence is usually the natural choice: <Uk>Ми буду́ємо буди́нок</Uk> (We are building a house).

## Common mistakes and limits

<Note type="mistake">
  **Apologising with <Uk>я вибача́юся</Uk>.** Usage guides reject it: <Uk>-ся</Uk> means "oneself", so it sounds as if you are excusing yourself. Say <Uk>ви́бачте</Uk>, <Uk>проба́чте</Uk> or <Uk>перепро́шую</Uk> instead [src: goroh-usage]. <Uk>Ви́бачте</Uk> is the <Uk>ви</Uk> form; to someone you call <Uk>ти</Uk>, say <Uk>ви́бач</Uk>.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Leaving out <Uk>-ть-</Uk>.** The *he/she/it* form needs <Uk>-ть-</Uk> before <Uk>-ся</Uk>: <Uk>вмива́ється</Uk>, <Uk>сміє́ться</Uk>. The <Uk>т</Uk> appears even in first-group verbs, which have none without <Uk>-ся</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §115, part 4, note 2].
</Note>

<Note type="remember">
  **Not every <Uk>-ся</Uk> means "oneself".** <Uk>Уро́к почина́ється</Uk> does not mean that the lesson starts itself, and <Uk>смія́тися</Uk> has no "self" in it at all. Learn each reflexive verb with its meaning, and learn which case it takes: <Uk>боя́тися</Uk> + genitive, <Uk>подо́батися</Uk> + dative, <Uk>зустріча́тися з</Uk> + instrumental.
</Note>

<Note>
  The same verbs also have future forms: <Uk>вмива́тимуся</Uk> (I will be washing). The [future tense](/grammar/future-tense/) page explains them. A reflexive verb can be perfective too: <Uk>зустрі́тися</Uk> (to meet, once) is the partner of <Uk>зустріча́тися</Uk>. See [aspect](/glossary/#aspect) in the glossary.
</Note>
