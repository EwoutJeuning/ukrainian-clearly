After this page you will be able to form the command forms of most verbs for <Uk>ти</Uk> and <Uk>ви</Uk>, say *let's* with forms such as <Uk>чита́ймо</Uk>, and say *let him* or *let them* with <Uk>хай</Uk> and <Uk>неха́й</Uk>. You will know which aspect to use in a command, and how to soften a request.

The command form is called the **imperative** (<Uk>наказо́вий спо́сіб</Uk>). Ukrainian has three one-word imperative forms: for <Uk>ти</Uk>, for <Uk>ви</Uk>, and for *we* (*let's*) [src: pravopys-2026, §116].

## The ти form

The imperative is built on the stem of the present tense (for a perfective verb, the stem of its future). A practical way to find it: take the <Uk>ти</Uk> form of the [present tense](/grammar/present-tense/) and drop the ending <Uk>-еш</Uk>, <Uk>-єш</Uk> or <Uk>-иш</Uk>. Then there are three patterns [src: pravopys-2026, §116].

<GrammarTable
  caption="Three ways to form the ти imperative"
  headers={['Stem', 'Ending', 'Examples']}
  rows={[
    ['ends in a vowel', '-й', 'чита́ти (чита́єш) → чита́й; купува́ти (купу́єш) → купу́й'],
    ['ends in a consonant; the я form is stressed on the ending', 'stressed -и́', 'писа́ти (пишу́) → пиши́; роби́ти (роблю́) → роби́; сказа́ти (скажу́) → скажи́'],
    ['ends in a consonant; the я form is stressed on the stem', 'no ending (ь after д, т, з, с, л, н)', 'сі́сти (ся́ду) → сядь; забу́ти (забу́ду) → забу́дь; ви́бачити (ви́бачу) → ви́бач'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[2]}
/>

So the stress of the <Uk>я</Uk> form is a useful guide. If it falls on the ending, as in <Uk>пишу́</Uk>, the imperative ends in a stressed <Uk>-и́</Uk>. If it falls on the stem, as in <Uk>ся́ду</Uk>, there is usually no ending. Notice that the consonant change of the present stays in the imperative: <Uk>писа́ти</Uk> → <Uk>пишу́</Uk> → <Uk>пиши́</Uk>, <Uk>сказа́ти</Uk> → <Uk>скажу́</Uk> → <Uk>скажи́</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §116].

There are exceptions. Verbs with the stressed prefix <Uk>ви́-</Uk> (<Uk>ви́бери</Uk>), and verbs whose stem ends in a group of consonants, such as <Uk>кри́кни</Uk> and <Uk>підкре́сли</Uk>, keep an unstressed <Uk>-и</Uk>, and a few verbs are irregular [src: pravopys-2026, §116]. Some common ones:

  rows={[
    ['да́ти', 'дай', 'да́йте', 'give'],
    ['дава́ти', 'дава́й', 'дава́йте', 'give (imperfective)'],

    ['взя́ти', 'візьми́', 'візьмі́ть', 'take'],

Imperfective verbs in <Uk>-ава́ти</Uk> keep <Uk>-ава-</Uk> in the imperative: <Uk>дава́й</Uk>, <Uk>встава́й</Uk> (get up). Check new verbs in a dictionary: the <Uk>Горох</Uk> word-forms page lists the imperative under <Uk>наказо́вий спо́сіб</Uk>.

## The ви form

The <Uk>ви</Uk> form is used for several people, or politely for one person. It is built from the <Uk>ти</Uk> form [src: pravopys-2026, §116]:

- if the <Uk>ти</Uk> form ends in <Uk>-и</Uk>, replace it with <Uk>-іть</Uk>: <Uk>пиши́</Uk> → <Uk>пиші́ть</Uk>, <Uk>роби́</Uk> → <Uk>робі́ть</Uk>;
- otherwise add <Uk>-те</Uk>: <Uk>чита́й</Uk> → <Uk>чита́йте</Uk>, <Uk>сядь</Uk> → <Uk>ся́дьте</Uk>.

The stress stays on the same syllable as in the <Uk>ти</Uk> form.

## Let's: the we form

To suggest doing something together, Ukrainian has a one-word *we* form. It is built like the <Uk>ви</Uk> form, with <Uk>-імо</Uk> instead of <Uk>-іть</Uk>, or <Uk>-мо</Uk> instead of <Uk>-те</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §116].

    ['ходи́ти', 'ходи́', 'ході́мо', 'let’s go'],
    ['сі́сти', 'сядь', 'ся́дьмо', 'let’s sit down'],
    ['зроби́ти', 'зроби́', 'зробі́мо', 'let’s do (it)'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[0, 1, 2]}
/>

The orthography also gives a shorter form in <Uk>-ім</Uk> next to <Uk>-імо</Uk>, for example <Uk>пиші́м</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §116]. This page uses the full form.

  <Example uk="Ході́мо додо́му." en="Let's go home." />
  <Example uk="Чита́ймо да́лі." en="Let's read on." />
  <Example uk="Зробі́мо пере́рву." en="Let's take a break." />
</ExampleList>

### Давай, давайте

In conversation you will also hear <Uk>дава́й</Uk> or <Uk>дава́йте</Uk> + the *we* form of the future: <Uk>Дава́й погра́ємо.</Uk> (Let's play.) The academic dictionary records this as colloquial [src: goroh-dictionary]. Usage guides recommend the one-word imperative instead: <Uk>ході́мо</Uk> rather than <Uk>дава́йте пі́демо</Uk>, <Uk>чита́ймо</Uk> rather than <Uk>дава́йте чита́ти</Uk> [src: goroh-usage]. Learn to understand <Uk>дава́й</Uk>, but use the one-word form yourself. <Uk>Дава́йте</Uk> in its own meaning, "give", is fine: <Uk>Дава́йте ва́ші зо́шити.</Uk> (Hand in your exercise books.) [src: goroh-usage]

## Let him, let them: хай and нехай

For *he*, *she*, *it* and *they* there is no one-word imperative. Use <Uk>хай</Uk> or <Uk>неха́й</Uk> (they mean the same) with the present tense of an imperfective verb, or the future of a perfective verb [src: goroh-dictionary].

<ExampleList title="Хай, неха́й">
  <Example uk="Хай він зателефону́є мені́ за́втра." en="Let him phone me tomorrow. / Tell him to phone me tomorrow." note="Perfective future: one call." />
  <Example uk="Неха́й вони́ захо́дять." en="Let them come in." note="Imperfective present." />
  <Example uk="Хай пи́шуть." en="Let them write." />
</ExampleList>

## Aspect in commands

Both imperfective and perfective verbs have an imperative. The choice follows the ideas on the [aspect](/grammar/aspect/) page. As tendencies:

- **One specific request:** usually perfective. You want one action done, with a result.
- **Invitations, offers and general instructions:** often imperfective. <Uk>Захо́дьте!</Uk> (Come in!) and <Uk>Сіда́йте!</Uk> (Have a seat!) are the usual friendly invitations.
- **Repeated actions:** imperfective, as in other tenses.

<ExampleList title="Requests and invitations">
  <Example uk="Відчині́ть, будь ла́ска, вікно́." en="Open the window, please." note="Formal or plural: ви. Perfective: one action." />
  <Example uk="Купи́ хліб." en="Buy some bread." note="Informal: ти. Perfective: one errand." />
  <Example uk="Захо́дьте, сіда́йте!" en="Come in, have a seat!" note="Formal or plural: ви. Imperfective: a friendly invitation." />
  <Example uk="Пиші́ть мені́ ча́сто." en="Write to me often." note="Formal or plural: ви. Imperfective: repeated action." />
</ExampleList>

### Negative commands

With <Uk>не</Uk>, the difference is clearer (see also [negation](/grammar/negation/)):

- <Uk>не</Uk> + **imperfective** tells someone not to do something: a prohibition or advice.
- <Uk>не</Uk> + **perfective** is mostly a warning against something that might happen by accident: *don't forget*, *don't fall*.

<ExampleList title="Don't…">
  <Example uk="Не відчиня́йте вікно́." en="Don't open the window." note="Formal or plural: ви. Imperfective: a prohibition." />
  <Example uk="Не говорі́ть так го́лосно." en="Don't speak so loudly." note="Formal or plural: ви." />
  <Example uk="Не забу́дь па́спорт!" en="Don't forget your passport!" note="Informal: ти. Perfective: a warning." />
  <Example uk="Не впади́!" en="Don't fall!" note="Informal: ти. Perfective: a warning." />
</ExampleList>

When you are unsure, use the imperfective after <Uk>не</Uk>.

## Making a request polite

A bare imperative can sound abrupt. With <Uk>будь ла́ска</Uk> (please) it is an ordinary polite request. <Uk>Будь ла́ска</Uk> does not change for <Uk>ти</Uk> or <Uk>ви</Uk>. [Politeness](/sentences/politeness/) has many everyday examples.

<ExampleList title="Будь ла́ска with an imperative">
  <Example uk="Повторі́ть, будь ла́ска." en="Say that again, please." note="Formal or plural: ви." />
  <Example uk="Зачека́йте хвили́нку, будь ла́ска." en="Wait a moment, please." note="Formal or plural: ви." />
  <Example uk="Допоможи́, будь ла́ска." en="Help me, please." note="Informal: ти." />
</ExampleList>

For a softer request, ask a question instead of giving a command. <Uk>Чи не могли́ б ви</Uk> + infinitive (Could you…?) uses the [conditional](/grammar/conditional/).

<ExampleList title="Softer requests">
  <Example uk="Чи не могли́ б ви відчини́ти вікно́?" en="Could you open the window?" note="Formal or plural: ви." />
  <Example uk="Чи не могли́ б ви допомогти́?" en="Could you help?" note="Formal or plural: ви." />
</ExampleList>

## Common mistakes and limits

<Note type="mistake">
  **Adding <Uk>-те</Uk> to an <Uk>-и</Uk> form.** The <Uk>ви</Uk> form of <Uk>пиши́</Uk> is <Uk>пиші́ть</Uk>, not *<Uk>пиши́те</Uk>*. Only forms without <Uk>-и</Uk> add <Uk>-те</Uk>: <Uk>чита́йте</Uk>, <Uk>ся́дьте</Uk>.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Using <Uk>дава́йте</Uk> for every *let's*.** Usage guides recommend <Uk>ході́мо</Uk>, <Uk>чита́ймо</Uk>, <Uk>зробі́мо</Uk> [src: goroh-usage].
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **The <Uk>ти</Uk> form to a stranger.** Use the <Uk>ви</Uk> form with people you do not know well, even when you speak to one person.
</Note>

<Note type="remember">
  The stress of the <Uk>я</Uk> form helps: <Uk>пишу́</Uk> → <Uk>пиши́</Uk>, but <Uk>ся́ду</Uk> → <Uk>сядь</Uk>. The aspect rules in commands are tendencies; with <Uk>не</Uk>, prefer the imperfective unless you are warning someone.
</Note>
