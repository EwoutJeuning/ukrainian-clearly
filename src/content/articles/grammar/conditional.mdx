After this page you will be able to say *would* in Ukrainian: to express a wish (*I would like…*), to make a polite request, and to talk about things that are not real with <Uk>якби́</Uk> (*if I had time, I would go*). You will know when to write <Uk>би</Uk> and when <Uk>б</Uk>, where the word goes in the sentence, and how a real condition with <Uk>якщо́</Uk> differs.

## How to form it

The conditional (<Uk>умо́вний спо́сіб</Uk>) is easy to build. Take the **past form** of the verb, which you know from [the past tense](/grammar/past-tense/), and add the little word <Uk>би</Uk> or <Uk>б</Uk> [src: goroh-dictionary]. The verb agrees in gender and number, exactly as in the past tense. <Uk>Би</Uk> itself never changes.

The conditional has no tense of its own. The same form can refer to the present, the future or the past; the context decides.

### Би or б?

The orthography gives a simple rule [src: pravopys-2026, §117]:

- <Uk>б</Uk> after a word that ends in a vowel: <Uk>я хоті́ла б</Uk>, <Uk>вона́ б сказа́ла</Uk>;
- <Uk>би</Uk> after a word that ends in a consonant: <Uk>я хоті́в би</Uk>, <Uk>він би сказа́в</Uk>.

The rule looks at the word just before the particle, whatever that word is. The particle <Uk>би</Uk> / <Uk>б</Uk> that forms the conditional is always written as a separate word [src: pravopys-2026, §44, §117]. Inside conjunctions such as <Uk>якби́</Uk> and <Uk>щоб</Uk> it is part of the word (see below) [src: pravopys-2026, §43, §44].

### Where би goes

<Uk>Би</Uk> usually comes straight after the verb, or after an earlier word in the sentence, often the subject. The orthography's own examples include both <Uk>я хоті́в би</Uk> and <Uk>я б про це розпові́в</Uk> (I would talk about it) [src: pravopys-2026, §117].

<ExampleList title="Two positions, one meaning">
  <Example uk="Я хоті́в би ка́ви." en="I would like some coffee." note="Said by a man. After хоті́ти, ка́ви (genitive) means “some coffee”." />
  <Example uk="Я б хоті́в ка́ви." en="I would like some coffee." note="Said by a man. The same meaning; б follows я, which ends in a vowel." />
  <Example uk="Я хоті́ла б ка́ви." en="I would like some coffee." note="Said by a woman." />
</ExampleList>

## Wishes

The conditional of <Uk>хоті́ти</Uk> with an infinitive is the usual way to say *I would like to…*. It is softer than <Uk>я хо́чу</Uk> (I want).

<ExampleList title="Wishes">
  <Example uk="Я хоті́в би пої́хати до Льво́ва." en="I would like to go to Lviv." note="Said by a man." />
  <Example uk="Ми хоті́ли б жи́ти бі́ля мо́ря." en="We would like to live by the sea." />
  <Example uk="Було́ б до́бре відпочи́ти." en="It would be good to have a rest." note="було́ is the neuter past of бу́ти: “it would be”." />
</ExampleList>

## Polite requests

The conditional makes a request less direct. Two common patterns are <Uk>я хоті́в би</Uk> / <Uk>я хоті́ла б</Uk> + infinitive (I would like to…) and <Uk>Чи не могли́ б ви</Uk> + infinitive (Could you…?). <Uk>Могли́</Uk> is the past form of <Uk>могти́</Uk> (can, to be able). [Politeness](/sentences/politeness/) has more examples.

<ExampleList title="Polite requests">
  <Example uk="Я хоті́ла б замо́вити ка́ву." en="I would like to order a coffee." note="Said by a woman." />
  <Example uk="Чи не могли́ б ви повтори́ти?" en="Could you say that again?" note="Formal or plural: ви." />
  <Example uk="Ви не могли́ б мені́ допомогти́?" en="Could you help me?" note="Formal or plural: ви." />
</ExampleList>

## Unreal conditions with якби

To talk about something that is not true, or did not happen, use <Uk>якби́</Uk> (if) in the *if* part and the conditional in the main part [src: goroh-dictionary]. The *if* part also uses the past form; <Uk>якби́</Uk> already contains <Uk>би</Uk>, so it is not repeated there.

<ExampleList title="If… would…">
  <Example uk="Якби́ я мав час, я пої́хав би до Льво́ва." en="If I had time, I would go to Lviv." note="Said by a man. I have no time." />
  <Example uk="Якби́ я ма́ла час, я пої́хала б до Льво́ва." en="If I had time, I would go to Lviv." note="Said by a woman." />
  <Example uk="Якби́ ти сказа́в мені́, я допомі́г би." en="If you had told me, I would have helped." note="Informal: ти, said to a man. Said by a man." />
  <Example uk="Якби́ не було́ дощу́, ми пої́хали б на мо́ре." en="If it weren't raining, we would go to the seaside. / If it hadn't rained, we would have gone to the seaside." note="The same form for the present and the past; context decides." />
</ExampleList>

<Uk>Якби́</Uk> is one word. Written apart, <Uk>як би</Uk> is the question word <Uk>як</Uk> (how) with the particle <Uk>би</Uk>: <Uk>Як би кра́ще ви́конати це завда́ння!</Uk> (How could this task best be done!) [src: pravopys-2026, §43].

## Real conditions with якщо

When the condition is possible or likely, use <Uk>якщо́</Uk> (if) and an ordinary tense, not the conditional. Where English uses the present after *if* for a future event, Ukrainian usually uses the **future** [src: goroh-dictionary].

<ExampleList title="If… will…">
  <Example uk="Якщо́ бу́де час, я пої́ду до Льво́ва." en="If I have time, I will go to Lviv." note="Literally “If there will be time…”." />
  <Example uk="Якщо́ за́втра бу́де дощ, ми бу́демо вдо́ма." en="If it rains tomorrow, we will stay at home." />
  <Example uk="Якщо́ ти хо́чеш, я допоможу́." en="If you want, I will help." note="Informal: ти. A condition about the present uses the present." />
</ExampleList>

## Щоб + past form

The conjunction <Uk>щоб</Uk> (so that, in order to) introduces a purpose [src: goroh-dictionary]. When the subject of both parts is the same person, it is usually followed by an infinitive. When the subject is different, the verb after <Uk>щоб</Uk> takes the **past form**, as in the conditional, but without a separate <Uk>би</Uk>: <Uk>щоб</Uk> already contains it. The same pattern reports a request or wish: <Uk>Він сказа́в, щоб усі́ прийшли́.</Uk> (He told everyone to come.) [src: pravopys-2026, §43].

<ExampleList title="Щоб">
  <Example uk="Я прийшо́в, щоб допомогти́." en="I came to help." note="Said by a man. The same person comes and helps: infinitive." />
  <Example uk="Я хо́чу, щоб ти прийшла́." en="I want you to come." note="Informal: ти, said to a woman. Different subjects: past form." />
  <Example uk="Вона́ сказа́ла, щоб ми зателефонува́ли." en="She told us to phone." />
</ExampleList>

<Uk>Щоб</Uk> is written as one word. Apart, <Uk>що б</Uk> is <Uk>що</Uk> (what) with the particle: <Uk>Що б ви сказа́ли?</Uk> (What would you say?) [src: pravopys-2026, §43].

## Common mistakes and limits

<Note type="mistake">
  **The wrong form of the particle.** After a vowel write <Uk>б</Uk>, after a consonant <Uk>би</Uk>: <Uk>я хоті́ла б</Uk>, <Uk>я хоті́в би</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §117].
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Present or future after <Uk>би</Uk>.** A verb with <Uk>би</Uk> is in the past form: <Uk>я пої́хав би</Uk>, not *<Uk>я пої́ду би</Uk>*.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **<Uk>Якби́</Uk> for a real condition.** For something that may well happen, use <Uk>якщо́</Uk> + future: <Uk>Якщо́ бу́де час, я пої́ду.</Uk>
</Note>

<Note type="remember">
  The verb in the conditional agrees like a past-tense verb. A man says <Uk>я хоті́в би</Uk>, a woman says <Uk>я хоті́ла б</Uk>, and with <Uk>ви</Uk> the form is always plural: <Uk>ви хоті́ли б</Uk>.
</Note>
