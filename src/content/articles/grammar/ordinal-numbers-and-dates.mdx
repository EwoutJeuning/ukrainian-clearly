After this page you will be able to use ordinal numbers (*first*, *second*, *third* …) with the right ending, say and understand dates such as <Uk>два́дцять четве́ртого се́рпня</Uk> (on the 24th of August), say a year, and tell the time in full, including *half past*, *a quarter past* and *five to*.

[Time](/sentences/time/) showed how to ask the time and say *at two*, *at seven in the evening*. [Numbers](/grammar/numbers/) covered the counting numbers. This page adds the ordinal numbers and what they are used for. The names of the months and days are on the [time vocabulary](/vocabulary/time/) page.

## The ordinal numbers

**Ordinal numbers** (<Uk>порядко́ві числі́вники</Uk>) give a position in a row: *first*, *second*, *tenth*. In Ukrainian they are adjectives. They have the same endings as <Uk>нови́й</Uk>: <Uk>-ий</Uk>, <Uk>-а</Uk>, <Uk>-е</Uk>, plural <Uk>-і</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §106; ukma-ukrainian-foreign-language, Lesson 9].

## Agreement and case

An ordinal agrees with its noun in gender, number and case, like any adjective. <Uk>Тре́тій</Uk> is the one exception to the pattern: it has the soft endings of <Uk>си́ній</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §106]. [Adjective cases](/grammar/adjective-cases/) has the full tables.

<ExampleList title="Ordinals agree with the noun">
  <Example uk="Це мій пе́рший уро́к украї́нської." en="This is my first Ukrainian lesson." note="уро́к is masculine: пе́рший." />
  <Example uk="Ми живемо́ на п’я́тому по́версі." en="We live on the fifth floor." note="на + locative: п’я́тому по́версі." />
  <Example uk="Ва́ша кімна́та — тре́тя право́руч." en="Your room is the third on the right." note="Formal or plural: ва́ша. Soft ending: тре́тя." />
  <Example uk="Тре́тє мі́сце також до́бре." en="Third place is good too." note="мі́сце is neuter: тре́тє." />
</ExampleList>

### Long ordinals: only the last word changes

In a long ordinal, the earlier parts are ordinary counting numbers. Only the **last** word is an ordinal, and only that word changes its ending [src: pravopys-2026, §106; ukma-ukrainian-foreign-language, Lesson 9]:

- <Uk>два́дцять пе́рший</Uk> (21st), <Uk>сто п’я́тий</Uk> (105th);
- <Uk>вісімдеся́т во́сьмий</Uk>, genitive <Uk>вісімдеся́т во́сьмого</Uk>.

### Ordinals written with figures

Ordinals are usually written in words. When an ordinal is written with a figure, its ending follows a hyphen: <Uk>5-й по́верх</Uk> (5th floor), <Uk>на 36-му кіломе́трі</Uk> (at the 36th kilometre). Dates and page numbers are an exception: they are written with the figure alone, without an ending: <Uk>1 сі́чня 2000 ро́ку</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §106].

## Dates

### What is the date today?

To ask the date, say <Uk>Яке́ сього́дні число́?</Uk>, literally "Which number is today?" [src: goroh-dictionary]. The answer is a **neuter** ordinal (it goes with <Uk>число́</Uk>, which is neuter), followed by the month in the **genitive** [src: ukma-ukrainian-foreign-language, Lesson 9]:

<ExampleList title="Saying today's date">
  <Example uk="Яке́ сього́дні число́?" en="What's the date today?" />
  <Example uk="Сього́дні вісімна́дцяте се́рпня." en="Today is the 18th of August." note="Neuter ordinal; се́рпня is the genitive of се́рпень." />
  <Example uk="За́втра пе́рше бе́резня." en="Tomorrow is the 1st of March." />
</ExampleList>

### The months in the genitive

Most month names end in <Uk>-ень</Uk>. In the genitive, <Uk>-ень</Uk> becomes <Uk>-ня</Uk>. Two months are different: <Uk>лю́тий</Uk> is an adjective in form and becomes <Uk>лю́того</Uk>, and <Uk>листопа́д</Uk> becomes <Uk>листопа́да</Uk> [src: ukma-ukrainian-foreign-language, Lesson 9]. The names of the months are written with a small letter.

### On a date: the genitive

To say *on* a date, put the ordinal in the **genitive** as well, with no preposition: <Uk>тре́тього лю́того</Uk> (on the 3rd of February) [src: ukma-ukrainian-foreign-language, Lesson 9].

    ['24 August', 'два́дцять четве́рте се́рпня', 'два́дцять четве́ртого се́рпня'],

<ExampleList title="On a date">
  <Example uk="Заня́ття закі́нчуються два́дцять п’я́того тра́вня." en="Classes end on the 25th of May." />
  <Example uk="Ми зустрі́немося в неді́лю, тре́тього лю́того." en="We'll meet on Sunday, the 3rd of February." note="The day of the week takes в + accusative; the date takes the genitive." />
  <Example uk="Па́спорт ді́йсний до три́дцять пе́ршого ли́пня." en="The passport is valid until the 31st of July." note="до + genitive." />
</ExampleList>

## Years

A year is an ordinal with the word <Uk>рік</Uk> (year), which is masculine. Only the last word is an ordinal: <Uk>дві ти́сячі два́дцять шо́стий рік</Uk> (2026, literally "two thousand twenty-sixth year") [src: pravopys-2026, §106].

- **In a full date**, the year is in the **genitive**, like the day: <Uk>ти́сяча дев’ятсо́т дев’яно́сто пе́ршого ро́ку</Uk> (of 1991) [src: ukma-ukrainian-foreign-language, Lesson 9].
- **For "in" a year**, the orthography's own examples use <Uk>у</Uk> with the **locative**: <Uk>у ти́сяча сімсо́т вісімдеся́т дев’я́тому (ро́ці)</Uk> (in 1789), <Uk>у дві ти́сячі вісімна́дцятому (ро́ці)</Uk> (in 2018). The word <Uk>ро́ці</Uk> can be left out [src: pravopys-2026, §106].

<ExampleList title="Years and full dates">
  <Example uk="Украї́на проголоси́ла незале́жність два́дцять четве́ртого се́рпня ти́сяча дев’ятсо́т дев’яно́сто пе́ршого ро́ку." en="Ukraine declared independence on the 24th of August 1991." />
  <Example uk="Я народи́лася шістна́дцятого бе́резня ти́сяча дев’ятсо́т вісімдеся́т четве́ртого ро́ку." en="I was born on the 16th of March 1984." note="Said by a woman." />
  <Example uk="Він народи́вся у дві ти́сячі вісімна́дцятому ро́ці." en="He was born in 2018." note="у before дв-." />
</ExampleList>

For *this year*, *last year* and *next year*, usage guides recommend the genitive: <Uk>цього́ ро́ку</Uk> (this year), <Uk>мину́лого ро́ку</Uk> or <Uk>торі́к</Uk> (last year), <Uk>насту́пного ро́ку</Uk> (next year), rather than *<Uk>у цьо́му ро́ці</Uk>* [src: goroh-usage].

## Telling the time in full

### The simple way: the hour, then the minutes

The simplest way to tell the time is the hour as a feminine ordinal (it agrees with <Uk>годи́на</Uk>, hour), then the minutes as a counting number. To say *at* a time, use <Uk>о</Uk> (<Uk>об</Uk> before a vowel) with the locative, ending in <Uk>-ій</Uk>. [Time](/sentences/time/) explains this in detail.

<ExampleList title="The simple way">
  <Example uk="Котра́ годи́на? — Четве́рта со́рок три." en="What time is it? — 4:43." />
  <Example uk="О котрі́й поча́ток?" en="What time does it start?" note="Literally “At which (hour) is the start?”" />
  <Example uk="Ми зустрі́немося о тре́тій два́дцять п’ять." en="We'll meet at 3:25." />
</ExampleList>

The minutes are an ordinary number: <Uk>о тре́тій со́рок п’ять</Uk> (at 3:45). Usage guides recommend this, not *<Uk>в три годи́ни со́рок п’ять хвили́н</Uk>* [src: goroh-usage; ukma-ukrainian-foreign-language, Lesson 9]. The 24-hour clock works the same way: <Uk>о вісімна́дцятій</Uk> (at 18:00).

### Counting towards the next hour

Ukrainian also tells the time the traditional way. Here the ordinal names the hour that is **running**, the one you are moving towards. Between 2:00 and 3:00 you are in the *third* hour, <Uk>тре́тя годи́на</Uk>. So *ten past two* is "ten minutes **on** the third": <Uk>де́сять хвили́н на тре́тю</Uk> [src: ukma-ukrainian-foreign-language, Lesson 9].

<GrammarTable
  caption="Telling the time in full"
  headers={['Time', 'Ukrainian', 'Literally']}
  rows={[
    ['2:10', 'де́сять хвили́н на тре́тю', 'ten minutes on the third'],
    ['7:15', 'чверть на во́сьму', 'a quarter on the eighth'],
    ['5:30', 'пів на шо́сту', 'half on the sixth'],
    ['9:45', 'за чверть деся́та', 'in a quarter, the tenth'],
    ['2:55', 'за п’ять хвили́н тре́тя', 'in five minutes, the third'],
  ]}

/>

- **Minutes past:** <Uk>на</Uk> with the accusative of the hour that is running: <Uk>чверть на дру́гу</Uk> (1:15) [src: goroh-usage]. You may also hear <Uk>де́сять хвили́н по дру́гій</Uk> (ten past two) [src: ukma-ukrainian-foreign-language, Lesson 9].
- **Half past:** <Uk>пів на</Uk> with the accusative: <Uk>пів на шо́сту</Uk> is 5:30, the middle of the sixth hour. The dictionary defines this <Uk>пів</Uk> as the moment in the middle of the hour [src: goroh-dictionary]. The usage guides recommend <Uk>пів на п’я́ту</Uk> rather than *<Uk>пів п’я́тої</Uk>*, and <Uk>пів на тре́тю</Uk>; <Uk>пів до тре́тьої</Uk> is dated [src: goroh-usage; goroh-dictionary].
- **Minutes to:** <Uk>за</Uk> with the minutes, and the coming hour in the nominative: <Uk>за чверть во́сьма</Uk> (7:45), <Uk>за п’ять хвили́н тре́тя</Uk> (2:55) [src: goroh-usage].

<Note type="remember">
  <Uk>Пів на шо́сту</Uk> is **5:30**, not 6:30. It is halfway to six. British English *half six* means 6:30, so take care.
</Note>

### At what time?

To say *at* one of these times, put <Uk>о</Uk> in front: <Uk>о пів на шо́сту</Uk> (at 5:30) [src: goroh-dictionary].

<ExampleList title="The time in full">
  <Example uk="Конце́рт почина́ється о пів на шо́сту." en="The concert starts at half past five." />
  <Example uk="Уже́ чверть на во́сьму." en="It's already a quarter past seven." />
  <Example uk="За́раз за п’ять хвили́н тре́тя." en="It's five to three now." />
  <Example uk="Ми домо́вилися зустрі́тися о пів на п’я́ту." en="We arranged to meet at half past four." />
</ExampleList>

## Common mistakes and limits

<Note type="mistake">
  **A counting number for the hour.** The hour is an ordinal: <Uk>о дев’я́тій</Uk>, not *<Uk>у де́в’ять годи́н</Uk>* [src: goroh-usage].
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **<Uk>Без</Uk> for minutes to the hour.** The usage guides give <Uk>за п’ять хвили́н тре́тя</Uk>, not *<Uk>без п’яти́ хвили́н три</Uk>*, and <Uk>за чверть во́сьма</Uk>, not *<Uk>без че́тверті во́сьма</Uk>* [src: goroh-usage]. The NaUKMA course also warns against the <Uk>без</Uk> pattern [src: ukma-ukrainian-foreign-language, Lesson 9].
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **The nominative for *on* a date.** *On the 1st of March* is <Uk>пе́ршого бе́резня</Uk>, in the genitive. <Uk>Пе́рше бе́резня</Uk> answers *what is the date?*
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Changing every part of a long ordinal.** Only the last word changes: <Uk>два́дцять пе́ршого</Uk>, not *<Uk>двадця́того пе́ршого</Uk>*.
</Note>

<Note>
  Ways of telling the time vary across Ukraine. The NaUKMA course notes that there are regional variations [src: ukma-ukrainian-foreign-language, Lesson 9]. The simple pattern, hour then minutes (<Uk>о тре́тій со́рок п’ять</Uk>), is the easiest one to use yourself.
</Note>
