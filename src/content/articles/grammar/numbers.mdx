After this page you will be able to count in Ukrainian and put the noun after a number in the right form: <Uk>оди́н до́лар</Uk>, <Uk>два до́лари</Uk>, <Uk>п’ять до́ларів</Uk>. You will know which numbers change for gender, how <Uk>оди́н</Uk>, <Uk>два</Uk>, <Uk>три</Uk>, <Uk>чоти́ри</Uk> and <Uk>п’ять</Uk> change for case, and how to say prices and ages.

You have met the basic rule on the [shopping](/sentences/shopping/) page and on the [genitive](/grammar/genitive/) page. This page brings it together and goes a step further. Numbers that say *how many* are **cardinal numbers** (<Uk>кі́лькісні числі́вники</Uk>). Numbers that give a position, such as *first* and *second*, are ordinal numbers; they have [their own page](/grammar/ordinal-numbers-and-dates/).

Larger numbers are built as in English, one word after another: <Uk>два́дцять оди́н</Uk> (21), <Uk>сто п’ятдеся́т чоти́ри</Uk> (154) [src: ukma-ukrainian-foreign-language, Lesson 8]. The parts are written as separate words: <Uk>ти́сяча п’ятсо́т</Uk>, <Uk>три́дцять ві́сім</Uk>. Numbers such as <Uk>одина́дцять</Uk>, <Uk>п’ятдеся́т</Uk> and <Uk>три́ста</Uk> are one word [src: pravopys-2026, §38].

<Uk>Ти́сяча</Uk> (thousand), <Uk>мільйо́н</Uk> (million) and <Uk>мілья́рд</Uk> (billion) behave like nouns and change their endings like other nouns [src: pravopys-2026, §105; ukma-ukrainian-foreign-language, Lesson 8]. So they follow the rule for nouns after numbers, below: <Uk>дві ти́сячі</Uk>, <Uk>п’ять ти́сяч</Uk>.

## One and two change for gender

Only *one* and *two* (and <Uk>півтора́</Uk> / <Uk>півтори́</Uk>, below) have different forms for the gender of the noun [src: ukma-ukrainian-foreign-language, Lesson 8; pravopys-2026, §105]:

<GrammarTable
  caption="One and two with each gender"
  headers={['', 'Masculine', 'Feminine', 'Neuter']}
  rows={[
    ['one', 'оди́н до́лар', 'одна́ гри́вня', 'одне́ я́блуко'],
    ['two', 'два до́лари', 'дві гри́вні', 'два я́блука'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[1, 2, 3]}
/>

- <Uk>Оди́н</Uk>, <Uk>одна́</Uk>, <Uk>одне́</Uk> agree like an adjective. The neuter also has the less common form <Uk>одно́</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §105].
- <Uk>Два</Uk> goes with masculine and neuter nouns, <Uk>дві</Uk> with feminine nouns.
- <Uk>Три</Uk>, <Uk>чоти́ри</Uk> and all the other numbers have one form for every gender.

## The noun after a number

The form of the noun depends on the **last word** of the number [src: ukma-ukrainian-foreign-language, Lesson 8]:

- After **1**: the nominative singular.
- After **2, 3, 4**: the nominative plural.
- After **5 and above**, including **11 to 14**: the genitive plural.

So the deciding word is the last one you say. <Uk>Со́рок три гри́вні</Uk> (43) ends in *three*, so it takes the nominative plural. <Uk>П’ятна́дцять гри́вень</Uk> (15) is one word, and it is not 2, 3 or 4, so it takes the genitive plural [src: ukma-ukrainian-foreign-language, Lesson 8].

Masculine nouns after <Uk>два</Uk>, <Uk>три</Uk> and <Uk>чоти́ри</Uk> take the plural endings <Uk>-и</Uk> or <Uk>-і</Uk>: <Uk>два хло́пці</Uk>, <Uk>три робітники́</Uk>, <Uk>чоти́ри слухачі́</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §88, note 1]. The [noun plurals](/grammar/noun-plurals/) page shows how these plurals are formed, and the [genitive](/grammar/genitive/) page shows the genitive plural.

### Thousands and millions

<Uk>Ти́сяча</Uk> is a feminine noun in <Uk>-а</Uk>, so it takes <Uk>дві</Uk> and changes like other feminine nouns in <Uk>-а</Uk>. <Uk>Мільйо́н</Uk> is masculine. The thing you count then goes into the genitive plural, after the word for *thousand* or *million*.

## Numbers in other cases

The rule above is for the nominative, and for the accusative of things. A number can also stand in another case, for example after a preposition. Then the number changes its form too. Learners mostly need the forms below; the orthography gives the full tables [src: pravopys-2026, §105].

### Один

<Uk>Оди́н</Uk> changes like an adjective and agrees with its noun in gender and case.

<GrammarTable
  caption="Оди́н, одна́, одне́"
  headers={['Case', 'Masculine', 'Feminine', 'Neuter']}
  rows={[
    ['Nominative', 'оди́н', 'одна́', 'одне́'],
    ['Genitive', 'одного́', 'одніє́ї', 'одного́'],
    ['Dative', 'одному́', 'одні́й', 'одному́'],
    ['Accusative', 'оди́н or одного́', 'одну́', 'одне́'],
    ['Instrumental', 'одни́м', 'одніє́ю', 'одни́м'],
    ['Locative', '(в) одному́, одні́м', '(в) одні́й', '(в) одному́, одні́м'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[1, 2, 3]}
/>

These are the forms and stresses of the orthography's table [src: pravopys-2026, §105]. The accusative masculine is <Uk>одного́</Uk> with people and animals, and <Uk>оди́н</Uk> with things, as for adjectives. The orthography also lists less common feminine forms such as <Uk>одно́ї</Uk>. In phrases like *one another* (<Uk>оди́н до одного</Uk>, <Uk>оди́н одному</Uk>), the orthography puts the stress on the first syllable [src: pravopys-2026, §105, note]. The dictionary gives other stresses for these forms, so they are shown here without a mark [src: goroh-dictionary].

### Два, три, чотири

In the other cases, <Uk>два</Uk> and <Uk>дві</Uk> have the same forms. For the accusative, the orthography says "the same as the nominative or the genitive" [src: pravopys-2026, §105]. As with masculine nouns on the [accusative](/grammar/accusative/) page, the nominative form goes with things (<Uk>ба́чу два буди́нки</Uk>, I see two houses) and the genitive form with people and animals (<Uk>ба́чу двох студе́нтів</Uk>, I see two students).

### П’ять and above

From five up, the numbers follow one pattern. Most cases have two forms, and both are correct [src: pravopys-2026, §105]:

<GrammarTable
  caption="П’ять (the same pattern for 6 to 20 and 30)"
  headers={['Case', 'Form']}
  rows={[
    ['Nominative', 'п’ять'],
    ['Genitive', 'п’яти́, п’ятьо́х'],
    ['Dative', 'п’яти́, п’ятьо́м'],
    ['Accusative', 'п’ять or п’ятьо́х'],
    ['Instrumental', 'п’ятьма́, п’ятьома́'],
    ['Locative', '(на) п’яти́, п’ятьо́х'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[1]}
/>

The numbers from <Uk>шість</Uk> to <Uk>два́дцять</Uk>, and <Uk>три́дцять</Uk>, change in the same way. <Uk>Шість</Uk>, <Uk>сім</Uk> and <Uk>ві́сім</Uk> change their stem: <Uk>шести́</Uk>, <Uk>семи́</Uk>, <Uk>восьми́</Uk>; the instrumental of <Uk>сім</Uk> and <Uk>ві́сім</Uk> can also be <Uk>сьома́</Uk>, <Uk>вісьма́</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §105].

Three numbers are simpler. <Uk>Со́рок</Uk>, <Uk>дев’яно́сто</Uk> and <Uk>сто</Uk> have a single ending <Uk>-а</Uk> in every case except the nominative and accusative: <Uk>сорока́</Uk>, <Uk>дев’яно́ста</Uk>, <Uk>ста</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §105].

In a long number, every part changes [src: pravopys-2026, §105].

  <Example uk="Прийшло́ бли́зько тридцяти́ студе́нтів." en="About thirty students came." note="бли́зько + genitive: тридцяти́." />
</ExampleList>

## Half: пів and півтора

- <Uk>Пів</Uk> means *half*. The noun after it is in the genitive singular, and <Uk>пів</Uk> is written as a separate word: <Uk>пів годи́ни</Uk> (half an hour), <Uk>пів я́блука</Uk> (half an apple) [src: pravopys-2026, §36, note].
- <Uk>Півтора́</Uk> (one and a half) goes with masculine and neuter nouns, <Uk>півтори́</Uk> with feminine nouns. They never change their form, and the noun after them is in the genitive singular: <Uk>півтора́ кілогра́ма</Uk>, <Uk>півтори́ гри́вні</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §107].

The usage guides mark *<Uk>півтора́ роки</Uk>* as an error: the correct form is <Uk>півтора́ ро́ку</Uk> (a year and a half) [src: goroh-usage].

## Prices

A price is a number and <Uk>гри́вня</Uk> (hryvnia), the Ukrainian currency, with <Uk>копі́йка</Uk> (kopiyka, a hundredth of a hryvnia) for the change [src: ukma-ukrainian-foreign-language, Lesson 8]. Both are feminine, so they take <Uk>одна́</Uk> and <Uk>дві</Uk>.

<GrammarTable
  caption="Гри́вня and копі́йка after numbers"
  headers={['', 'гри́вня', 'копі́йка']}
  rows={[
    ['1, 21, 31 …', 'гри́вня', 'копі́йка'],
    ['2–4, 22–24 …', 'гри́вні', 'копі́йки'],
    ['5–20, 25–30 …', 'гри́вень', 'копійо́к'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[1, 2]}
/>

After the verb <Uk>коштувати</Uk> (to cost) and after <Uk>за</Uk> (for), the price is in the accusative. *One* with a feminine noun changes: <Uk>одна́ гри́вня</Uk> becomes <Uk>одну́ гри́вню</Uk>. <Uk>Ти́сяча</Uk> changes like a noun: <Uk>ко́штує ти́сячу гри́вень</Uk>. The other numbers stay as they are [src: ukma-ukrainian-foreign-language, Lesson 8; pravopys-2026, §105].

<ExampleList title="Prices">
  <Example uk="Ка́ва коштує со́рок п’ять гри́вень." en="A coffee costs forty-five hryvnias." note="коштує is shown without a stress mark: the dictionary accepts two stresses." />
  <Example uk="Квито́к коштує одну́ гри́вню." en="A ticket costs one hryvnia." note="Accusative: одну́ гри́вню." />
  <Example uk="Я купи́в ці кві́ти за сто три́дцять дві гри́вні." en="I bought these flowers for a hundred and thirty-two hryvnias." note="Said by a man. Ends in дві: nominative plural." />
  <Example uk="Це коштує дві ти́сячі гри́вень." en="It costs two thousand hryvnias." />
</ExampleList>

In everyday speech, the words for hryvnias and kopiykas are often left out, especially when the price has both. <Uk>Одина́дцять три́дцять п’ять</Uk> means 11 hryvnias 35 kopiykas [src: ukma-ukrainian-foreign-language, Lesson 8]. [Shopping](/sentences/shopping/) has more phrases for paying.

## Ages

Ukrainian gives a person's age with the **dative** and the word <Uk>рік</Uk> (year): literally, "to me are twenty years". [The dative](/grammar/dative/) page explains the pattern. <Uk>Рік</Uk> follows the same 1 / 2–4 / 5+ rule as every other noun [src: ukma-ukrainian-foreign-language, Lesson 8]:

## Common mistakes and limits

<Note type="mistake">
  **Letting the whole number decide.** Only the last word counts. Say <Uk>два́дцять дві гри́вні</Uk> (22), not *<Uk>два́дцять дві гри́вень</Uk>*, and <Uk>два́дцять оди́н рік</Uk> (21), not *<Uk>два́дцять оди́н років</Uk>*.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Treating 12 to 14 like 2 to 4.** <Uk>Двана́дцять</Uk>, <Uk>трина́дцять</Uk> and <Uk>чотирна́дцять</Uk> are single words, so the noun is in the genitive plural: <Uk>двана́дцять гри́вень</Uk>, not *<Uk>двана́дцять гри́вні</Uk>*.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **The masculine form with a feminine noun.** Say <Uk>дві гри́вні</Uk>, <Uk>дві ти́сячі</Uk>, not *<Uk>два гри́вні</Uk>*, *<Uk>два ти́сячі</Uk>*.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **A plural after <Uk>півтора́</Uk>.** Say <Uk>півтора́ ро́ку</Uk>, not *<Uk>півтора́ роки</Uk>* [src: goroh-usage].
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **<Uk>Бі́льше</Uk> with the genitive of a number.** Usage guides recommend <Uk>пона́д сто</Uk> or <Uk>більш ніж сто</Uk> (more than a hundred), not *<Uk>бі́льше ста</Uk>* [src: goroh-usage].
</Note>

<Note type="remember">
  Learn three forms of every noun you will count: <Uk>оди́н до́лар</Uk>, <Uk>два до́лари</Uk>, <Uk>п’ять до́ларів</Uk>. Then any number works: look at its last word.
</Note>

<Note>
  This page does not cover the collective numbers <Uk>дво́є</Uk>, <Uk>тро́є</Uk>, <Uk>че́тверо</Uk> and so on, or fractions such as <Uk>одна́ тре́тя</Uk> (one third). The orthography gives their forms [src: pravopys-2026, §105, §107]. Adjectives between a number and its noun (*two new houses*) also have their own rules, which are not covered here.
</Note>
