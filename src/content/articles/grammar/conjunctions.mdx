After this page you will be able to join words and clauses with *and*, *but* and *or*, and build longer sentences with *that*, *so that*, *because*, *if*, *when* and *which*. You will also know the main comma rules: Ukrainian puts a comma before most words that begin a new clause, including <Uk>що</Uk> (that), where English usually has none.

## And: і, й, та

<Uk>І</Uk> and <Uk>й</Uk> are the same word, *and*. Which one you use depends on the sounds around it [src: pravopys-2026, §24]:

- <Uk>і</Uk> between consonants, at the start of a sentence, and after a pause marked by punctuation when a consonant follows;
- <Uk>й</Uk> between vowels, and between a vowel and a consonant;
- always <Uk>і</Uk> when two ideas are set against each other (<Uk>батьки́ і ді́ти</Uk>) and before a word that starts with <Uk>й</Uk>, <Uk>є</Uk>, <Uk>ї</Uk>, <Uk>ю</Uk> or <Uk>я</Uk>.

After a word ending in <Uk>й</Uk>, <Uk>я</Uk>, <Uk>ю</Uk>, <Uk>є</Uk> or <Uk>ї</Uk>, use <Uk>і</Uk> or <Uk>та</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §24]. <Uk>Та</Uk> is a third word for *and*. The rules exist for smooth sound, and the orthography allows departures from them for the sake of rhythm [src: pravopys-2026, §24].

<ExampleList title="And">
  <Example uk="Брат і сестра́ живу́ть у Льво́ві." en="My brother and sister live in Lviv." note="і between consonants." />
  <Example uk="Ми їмо́ й розмовля́ємо." en="We are eating and talking." note="й after a vowel, before a consonant." />
  <Example uk="Я п’ю чай та ка́ву." en="I drink tea and coffee." note="та after й." />
</ExampleList>

## But: а and але

Both join parts that contrast with each other [src: goroh-dictionary]. <Uk>А</Uk> is used when two things are compared, so it often translates English *and* or *whereas*. In <Uk>не…, а…</Uk> it means *not this but that*: one thing instead of another. <Uk>Але́</Uk> is the usual *but*; the dictionary gives <Uk>проте́</Uk> and <Uk>одна́к</Uk> (however) as near synonyms [src: goroh-dictionary].

<ExampleList title="A and але">
  <Example uk="Я вчи́телька, а він лі́кар." en="I'm a teacher, and he's a doctor." note="Said by a woman. A contrast: а." />
  <Example uk="Не ка́ва, а чай." en="Not coffee but tea." note="не…, а…: one thing instead of another." />
  <Example uk="Він хворі́в, але́ прийшо́в на робо́ту." en="He was ill, but he came to work." note="but: але́." />
</ExampleList>

<Uk>А</Uk> also starts a follow-up question: <Uk>А ти?</Uk> (And you?)

## Or: або and чи

<Uk>Або́</Uk> and <Uk>чи</Uk> both mean *or*: only one of the options is meant [src: goroh-dictionary]. <Uk>Чи</Uk> has two more jobs:

- at the start of a yes/no question (see [Questions](/grammar/questions/)): <Uk>Чи ти вдо́ма?</Uk> (Are you at home?);
- after verbs such as *know* and *ask*, it means *whether* or *if*: <Uk>Я не зна́ю, чи він при́йде.</Uk> (I don't know if he'll come.)

Repeated, <Uk>або́… або́…</Uk> means *either… or…*.

<ExampleList title="Or">
  <Example uk="Ви хо́чете ча́ю чи ка́ви?" en="Would you like tea or coffee?" note="Formal or plural: ви." />
  <Example uk="Я прийду́ в субо́ту або́ в неді́лю." en="I'll come on Saturday or Sunday." />
  <Example uk="Запита́й, чи є квитки́." en="Ask whether there are tickets." note="Informal: ти." />
</ExampleList>

## Words that start a dependent clause

The words below begin a clause that depends on the main one. In writing, that clause is separated by a comma, or by two commas if it sits in the middle of the sentence [src: pravopys-2026, §158].

### Що: that

<Uk>Що</Uk> introduces what someone says, thinks or knows. English can leave out *that*; Ukrainian keeps <Uk>що</Uk> and puts a comma before it.

<ExampleList title="Що">
  <Example uk="Я ду́маю, що він ма́є ра́цію." en="I think (that) he's right." />
  <Example uk="Вона́ сказа́ла, що за́втра бу́де дощ." en="She said (that) it would rain tomorrow." note="Literally “tomorrow there will be rain”." />
</ExampleList>

### Щоб: so that, in order to

<Uk>Щоб</Uk> introduces a purpose or a wish. With the same subject it takes an infinitive; with a different subject it takes the past form. [The conditional](/grammar/conditional/) explains this in detail. Compare <Uk>що</Uk> and <Uk>щоб</Uk> after *said*. The orthography uses this example: <Uk>Він сказа́в, щоб усі́ прийшли́</Uk> (He told everyone to come) [src: pravopys-2026, §43].

<ExampleList title="Що or щоб">
  <Example uk="Він сказа́в, що всі прийшли́." en="He said that everyone had come." note="A report of a fact." />
  <Example uk="Він сказа́в, щоб усі́ прийшли́." en="He told everyone to come." note="A request." />
  <Example uk="Я вчу украї́нську, щоб чита́ти книжки́." en="I'm learning Ukrainian so that I can read books." note="Same subject: infinitive." />
</ExampleList>

<Uk>Щоб</Uk> is one word. <Uk>Що б</Uk>, written apart, is "what" with the particle <Uk>би</Uk>: <Uk>Що б ви сказа́ли?</Uk> (What would you say?) [src: pravopys-2026, §43].

### Бо and тому що: because

Both mean *because*. <Uk>Бо</Uk> usually stands at the start of its clause [src: goroh-dictionary]. <Uk>Тому́ що</Uk> is written as two words, usually with no comma between them; a comma goes before <Uk>що</Uk> only when <Uk>тому́</Uk> is stressed [src: pravopys-2026, §43, §158]. To ask *why?*, use <Uk>чому́?</Uk>; the answer often starts with <Uk>Тому́ що…</Uk>.

<ExampleList title="Because">
  <Example uk="Я не прийшо́в, бо був хво́рий." en="I didn't come because I was ill." note="Said by a man." />
  <Example uk="Ми вдо́ма, тому́ що па́дає дощ." en="We're at home because it's raining." />
  <Example uk="— Чому́ ти не спиш? — Тому́ що за́втра важли́вий день." en="“Why aren't you asleep?” “Because tomorrow is an important day.”" note="Informal: ти." />
</ExampleList>

### Якщо and коли: if, when

<Uk>Якщо́</Uk> means *if* for a real or possible condition. For something that will happen, Ukrainian uses the future after <Uk>якщо́</Uk>, where English uses the present. For an unreal condition (*if I had…, I would…*) use <Uk>якби́</Uk> and the conditional; see [The conditional](/grammar/conditional/). <Uk>Коли́</Uk> means *when*, and also introduces a time clause.

<ExampleList title="If and when">
  <Example uk="Якщо́ хо́чеш, допоможу́ тобі́." en="If you want, I'll help you." note="Informal: ти. An example from the orthography." />
  <Example uk="Якщо́ бу́де хоро́ша пого́да, ми пі́демо в парк." en="If the weather is good, we'll go to the park." note="Future after якщо́." />
  <Example uk="Коли́ я прийшла́ додо́му, усі́ вже спа́ли." en="When I got home, everyone was already asleep." note="Said by a woman." />
</ExampleList>

The first example above is from the orthography [src: pravopys-2026, §43]. <Uk>Якщо́</Uk> and <Uk>якби́</Uk> are one word. Apart, <Uk>як</Uk> is the question word *how* [src: pravopys-2026, §43].

### Question words inside a sentence

The question words from [Questions](/grammar/questions/) also join clauses: <Uk>хто</Uk>, <Uk>що</Uk>, <Uk>де</Uk>, <Uk>куди́</Uk>, <Uk>коли́</Uk>, <Uk>чому́</Uk>, <Uk>скі́льки</Uk>. A comma goes before the question word [src: pravopys-2026, §158].

<ExampleList title="Hidden questions">
  <Example uk="Я не зна́ю, де він живе́." en="I don't know where he lives." />
  <Example uk="Скажі́ть, будь ла́ска, котра́ годи́на." en="Could you tell me what time it is, please?" note="Formal or plural: ви." />
</ExampleList>

## Який: who, which, that

To say *the man who…*, *the book that…*, use <Uk>яки́й</Uk>. It agrees with the noun it refers to in **gender and number**, like an adjective. Its **case** depends on its role inside its own clause.

/>

Two differences from English:

- The preposition goes **before** <Uk>яки́й</Uk>, not at the end of the clause: <Uk>у яко́му ми живемо́</Uk>, not *which we live in*.
- <Uk>Яки́й</Uk> is not dropped the way English drops *that*: *the book I'm reading* is <Uk>кни́га, яку́ я чита́ю</Uk>.

When the <Uk>яки́й</Uk> clause sits in the middle of the sentence, it has a comma on both sides [src: pravopys-2026, §158]:

<ExampleList title="Commas on both sides">
  <Example uk="Кни́га, яку́ ти мені́ дав, ду́же ціка́ва." en="The book you gave me is very interesting." note="Informal: ти, said to a man." />
</ExampleList>

See [Adjectives and agreement](/grammar/adjectives-and-agreement/) for the endings of <Uk>яки́й</Uk>.

## Where the comma goes

The orthography's comma rules are long. These are the main points for the words on this page [src: pravopys-2026, §158]:

There are exceptions. For example, no comma goes between two clauses joined by a single <Uk>і</Uk> when something at the start of the sentence belongs to both of them [src: pravopys-2026, §158]. Some fixed phrases have no comma at all, such as <Uk>роби́ що хо́чеш</Uk> (do as you like) [src: pravopys-2026, §158]. Treat the table as a guide for everyday writing.

## Common mistakes and limits

<Note type="mistake">
  **No comma before <Uk>що</Uk>.** English writes *I think he's right* without a comma. Ukrainian needs both the word and the comma: <Uk>Я ду́маю, що він ма́є ра́цію.</Uk>
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **<Uk>Якщо́</Uk> for *whether*.** *I don't know if he'll come* is <Uk>Я не зна́ю, чи він при́йде</Uk>. <Uk>Якщо́</Uk> means *if* only in the sense of a condition.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Spelling.** <Uk>Щоб</Uk>, <Uk>якщо́</Uk> and <Uk>якби́</Uk> are one word; <Uk>тому́ що</Uk> is two words [src: pravopys-2026, §43].
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Leaving out <Uk>яки́й</Uk>.** *The book I bought* is <Uk>кни́га, яку́ я купи́в</Uk> (said by a man), not just *<Uk>кни́га я купи́в</Uk>*.
</Note>

<Note type="remember">
  If a clause begins with a joining word such as <Uk>що</Uk>, <Uk>щоб</Uk>, <Uk>бо</Uk>, <Uk>якщо́</Uk>, <Uk>коли́</Uk> or <Uk>яки́й</Uk>, it usually needs a comma before it.
</Note>
