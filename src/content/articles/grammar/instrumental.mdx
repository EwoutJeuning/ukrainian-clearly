After this page you will be able to form the **[instrumental](/glossary/#instrumental)** of most nouns in the singular and plural. You will be able to say what you do something with (<Uk>пишу́ ру́чкою</Uk>, I write with a pen), how you travel, who you are with, and what job someone does. You will also know how the instrumental is used for some expressions of time and place.

This page follows on from [The dative case](/grammar/dative/), which explains how the endings are grouped by kind of noun.

## What the instrumental does

The instrumental answers the questions <Uk>ким?</Uk> (by whom? with whom?) and <Uk>чим?</Uk> (with what?). Its basic job is the tool or means: the thing you do something *with*. English needs a preposition for that. Ukrainian uses the ending alone.

<ExampleList title="The tool or means">
  <Example uk="Я пишу́ ру́чкою." en="I write with a pen." note="ру́чкою: instrumental of ру́чка. No preposition." />
  <Example uk="Я рі́жу хліб ноже́м." en="I cut the bread with a knife." note="ноже́м: instrumental of ніж." />
</ExampleList>

## Singular endings

The forms below come from the paradigms in the <Uk>Горох</Uk> dictionary [src: goroh-dictionary], and the endings follow the official orthography [src: pravopys-2026, §72, §85, §95].

### Feminine nouns in -а and -я

These take <Uk>-ою</Uk> after a hard consonant and <Uk>-ею</Uk> after a soft consonant or after <Uk>ж</Uk>, <Uk>ч</Uk>, <Uk>ш</Uk>, <Uk>щ</Uk>. After a vowel or an apostrophe, the ending is <Uk>-єю</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §72].

<GrammarTable
  caption="Instrumental singular of feminine nouns in -а and -я"
  headers={['Dictionary form', 'Instrumental', 'Meaning']}
  rows={[
    ['сестра́', 'сестро́ю', 'sister'],
    ['кни́га', 'кни́гою', 'book'],
    ['ру́чка', 'ру́чкою', 'pen'],
    ['ву́лиця', 'ву́лицею', 'street'],
    ['ку́хня', 'ку́хнею', 'kitchen'],
    ['пло́ща', 'пло́щею', 'square'],
    ['наді́я', 'наді́єю', 'hope'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[0, 1]}
/>

Unlike the dative, there is no consonant change here: <Uk>кни́га</Uk> → <Uk>кни́гою</Uk>, <Uk>рука́</Uk> → <Uk>руко́ю</Uk>.

### Masculine and neuter nouns

These take <Uk>-ом</Uk> after a hard consonant and <Uk>-ем</Uk> after a soft consonant or after <Uk>ж</Uk>, <Uk>ч</Uk>, <Uk>ш</Uk>, <Uk>щ</Uk>. After a vowel the ending is <Uk>-єм</Uk>. Neuter nouns in <Uk>-я</Uk> take <Uk>-ям</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §85].

<GrammarTable
  caption="Instrumental singular of masculine and neuter nouns"
  headers={['Dictionary form', 'Instrumental', 'Meaning']}
  rows={[
    ['брат', 'бра́том', 'brother'],
    ['авто́бус', 'авто́бусом', 'bus'],
    ['мі́сто', 'мі́стом', 'town, city'],
    ['ніж', 'ноже́м', 'knife'],
    ['лі́кар', 'лі́карем', 'doctor'],
    ['мо́ре', 'мо́рем', 'sea'],
    ['музе́й', 'музе́єм', 'museum'],
    ['життя́', 'життя́м', 'life'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[0, 1]}
/>

Town names in <Uk>-ів</Uk> follow the pattern: <Uk>Ки́їв</Uk> → <Uk>Ки́євом</Uk>, <Uk>Ха́рків</Uk> → <Uk>Ха́рковом</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §85]. Many surnames in <Uk>-ов</Uk>, <Uk>-ев</Uk>, <Uk>-ин</Uk> or <Uk>-ін</Uk> are different again: they take <Uk>-им</Uk>, like an adjective [src: pravopys-2026, §85].

### Feminine nouns ending in a consonant: ніччю, сіллю

Feminine nouns like <Uk>ніч</Uk> take <Uk>-ю</Uk>. The rule for the consonant before it is the part to learn [src: pravopys-2026, §95]:

- **One consonant at the end, after a vowel: it doubles.** <Uk>ніч</Uk> → <Uk>ні́ччю</Uk>, <Uk>сіль</Uk> → <Uk>сі́ллю</Uk>, <Uk>тінь</Uk> → <Uk>ті́нню</Uk>. The soft sign drops out.
- **Two consonants at the end: no doubling.** <Uk>ра́дість</Uk> → <Uk>ра́дістю</Uk>.
- **A final <Uk>б</Uk>, <Uk>п</Uk>, <Uk>в</Uk>, <Uk>м</Uk>, <Uk>ф</Uk> or <Uk>р</Uk>: no doubling, and an apostrophe comes before <Uk>-ю</Uk>.** <Uk>любо́в</Uk> → <Uk>любо́в’ю</Uk>, <Uk>ма́ти</Uk> → <Uk>ма́тір’ю</Uk> (mother, which is irregular).

<GrammarTable
  caption="Instrumental singular of feminine nouns ending in a consonant"
  headers={['Dictionary form', 'Instrumental', 'Meaning', 'Why']}
  rows={[
    ['ніч', 'ні́ччю', 'night', 'one consonant: doubled'],
    ['сіль', 'сі́ллю', 'salt', 'one consonant: doubled'],
    ['тінь', 'ті́нню', 'shadow, shade', 'one consonant: doubled'],
    ['ра́дість', 'ра́дістю', 'joy', 'two consonants: not doubled'],

Notice that the vowel of the dictionary form stays in the instrumental: <Uk>ніч</Uk>, <Uk>ні́ччю</Uk>, but <Uk>но́чі</Uk> in the dative.

## Plural endings

In the plural, nouns take <Uk>-ами</Uk>, or <Uk>-ями</Uk> after a soft consonant or a vowel [src: pravopys-2026, §79, §92, §96].

<GrammarTable
  caption="Instrumental plural"
  headers={['Dictionary form', 'Nominative plural', 'Instrumental plural']}
  rows={[
    ['кни́га', 'кни́ги', 'кни́гами'],
    ['студе́нт', 'студе́нти', 'студе́нтами'],
    ['мі́сто', 'міста́', 'міста́ми'],
    ['лі́кар', 'лікарі́', 'лікаря́ми'],
    ['ву́лиця', 'ву́лиці', 'ву́лицями'],
    ['друг', 'дру́зі', 'дру́зями'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[0, 1, 2]}
/>

A few common nouns end in <Uk>-ми</Uk> instead. Learn these two: <Uk>ді́ти</Uk> → <Uk>ді́тьми</Uk> (with children) and <Uk>лю́ди</Uk> → <Uk>людьми́</Uk> (with people) [src: goroh-dictionary].

## Uses of the instrumental

### Tools and transport

Use the instrumental alone for the tool you use, and for the transport you travel by. Compare *by bus*, *by train* in English.

<ExampleList title="Tools and transport">
  <Example uk="Ми ї́демо авто́бусом." en="We're going by bus." />
  <Example uk="Я ї́ду по́їздом." en="I'm going by train." />
  <Example uk="Він пи́ше олівце́м." en="He writes in pencil." note="олівце́м: instrumental of оліве́ць." />
</ExampleList>

The NaUKMA course gives <Uk>Я ї́ду по́їздом</Uk> as its example of the instrumental for means [src: ukma-ukrainian-foreign-language, Lesson 5].

### З: together with

With the preposition <Uk>з</Uk> (also <Uk>із</Uk>, <Uk>зі</Uk>), the instrumental means *with* in the sense of *together with* or *having* [src: goroh-dictionary]. The choice between <Uk>з</Uk>, <Uk>із</Uk> and <Uk>зі</Uk> depends on the sounds around it, as explained in [Common prepositions](/grammar/prepositions/).

<ExampleList title="З with the instrumental">
  <Example uk="Я живу́ з бра́том." en="I live with my brother." />
  <Example uk="Ка́ва з молоко́м, будь ла́ска." en="A coffee with milk, please." />
  <Example uk="Вона́ гуля́є із соба́кою." en="She's walking the dog." note="із before с." />
  <Example uk="Хо́чеш піти́ зі мно́ю?" en="Do you want to come with me?" note="Informal: ти (хо́чеш)." />
</ExampleList>

### Being, becoming and working as

After <Uk>працюва́ти</Uk> (to work), the job is in the instrumental. The dictionary's own example is the informal question <Uk>Ким працю́єш?</Uk> (What do you do for a living?) [src: goroh-dictionary]. After <Uk>ста́ти</Uk> (to become), the new role is in the instrumental too.

<ExampleList title="Jobs and roles">
  <Example uk="Він працю́є лі́карем." en="He works as a doctor." />
  <Example uk="Ким ви працю́єте?" en="What do you do for a living?" note="Formal or plural: ви." />
  <Example uk="Вона́ ста́ла вчи́телькою." en="She became a teacher." />
  <Example uk="Мій брат був студе́нтом у Льво́ві." en="My brother was a student in Lviv." />
</ExampleList>

With <Uk>бу́ти</Uk> (to be), the instrumental is common in the past and the future, as in the last example. The dictionary gives examples like <Uk>ота́маном бу́деш</Uk> (you will be a leader) [src: goroh-dictionary]. In the present tense Ukrainian usually leaves out *is*, and the noun stays in the nominative: <Uk>Він лі́кар.</Uk> (He's a doctor.)

### Time

Some time expressions have the form of the instrumental: <Uk>ра́нком</Uk> (in the morning), <Uk>весно́ю</Uk> (in spring). The dictionary also lists these as adverbs [src: goroh-dictionary]. A usage guide recommends instrumental phrases such as <Uk>оста́ннім ча́сом</Uk> (lately) [src: goroh-usage].

Other common time words look similar but are simply adverbs, not forms of a noun: <Uk>взи́мку</Uk> (in winter), <Uk>влі́тку</Uk> (in summer), <Uk>восени́</Uk> (in autumn), <Uk>навесні́</Uk> (in spring), <Uk>вночі́</Uk> (at night) [src: goroh-dictionary]. Learn them as words, not as case forms.

### The way you go

The instrumental can name the route along which someone moves. The dictionary quotes Shevchenko's line <Uk>Доли́ною коза́к іде́</Uk> (A Cossack walks along the valley) under <Uk>іти́</Uk> [src: goroh-dictionary].

<ExampleList title="The route">
  <Example uk="Ми йшли па́рком." en="We walked through the park." />
  <Example uk="Іді́ть лі́сом, так шви́дше." en="Go through the forest, it's quicker." note="Formal or plural: ви (іді́ть)." />
</ExampleList>

### Prepositions of place: під, над, за, перед, між

Five common prepositions take the instrumental when they describe where something is [src: goroh-dictionary].

<GrammarTable
  caption="Prepositions with the instrumental"
  headers={['Preposition', 'Meaning', 'Example']}
  rows={[
    ['під', 'under', 'під столо́м (under the table)'],
    ['над', 'above, over', 'над мо́рем (above the sea)'],
    ['за', 'behind', 'за буди́нком (behind the house)'],
    ['пе́ред', 'in front of; before', 'пе́ред шко́лою (in front of the school)'],
    ['між', 'between', 'між Ки́євом і Льво́вом (between Kyiv and Lviv)'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[0, 2]}
/>

<Uk>Пе́ред</Uk> is also used for time: <Uk>пе́ред обі́дом</Uk> (before lunch). <Uk>Між</Uk> takes the instrumental in most uses; the dictionary notes that it takes the genitive less often [src: goroh-dictionary].

<Uk>Під</Uk> and <Uk>за</Uk> take the accusative when they describe a direction: <Uk>Кіт під столо́м</Uk> (the cat is under the table), but <Uk>Кіт пішо́в під стіл</Uk> (the cat went under the table). [Common prepositions](/grammar/prepositions/) has more examples.

## Common mistakes and limits

<Note type="mistake">
  **Using <Uk>з</Uk> for a tool.** <Uk>З</Uk> means *together with*. For the thing you use, the instrumental stands alone: <Uk>пишу́ ру́чкою</Uk>, not *<Uk>пишу́ з ру́чкою</Uk>*, which sounds as if the pen were your companion.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Getting the doubling wrong.** Double the consonant after a vowel, not after another consonant: <Uk>ні́ччю</Uk> and <Uk>сі́ллю</Uk>, but <Uk>ра́дістю</Uk>. After <Uk>б</Uk>, <Uk>п</Uk>, <Uk>в</Uk>, <Uk>м</Uk>, <Uk>ф</Uk> or <Uk>р</Uk>, write an apostrophe instead: <Uk>любо́в’ю</Uk>.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Treating <Uk>взи́мку</Uk> as a case form.** <Uk>Взи́мку</Uk>, <Uk>влі́тку</Uk> and <Uk>восени́</Uk> are adverbs. Use them as they are.
</Note>

<Note type="remember">
  The instrumental singular ends in <Uk>-ою</Uk> or <Uk>-ею</Uk> for feminine nouns in <Uk>-а</Uk> or <Uk>-я</Uk>, and in <Uk>-ом</Uk> or <Uk>-ем</Uk> for masculine and neuter nouns. In the plural, most nouns end in <Uk>-ами</Uk> or <Uk>-ями</Uk>. Learn the uses in phrases: <Uk>пишу́ ру́чкою</Uk>, <Uk>ї́ду авто́бусом</Uk>, <Uk>з бра́том</Uk>, <Uk>працю́ю лі́карем</Uk>.
</Note>

<Note>
  This page covers nouns and personal pronouns. Adjectives and words like *my* and *this* also have instrumental forms that agree with the noun. They are covered on [a separate page](/grammar/adjective-cases/).
</Note>
