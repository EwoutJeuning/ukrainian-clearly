After this page you will be able to guess the gender of most Ukrainian nouns from their dictionary form. You will know the main exceptions to that guess and how to check a noun's gender in a dictionary. You will also see, as a preview, why gender matters: other words change to agree with it.

## Three genders

Every Ukrainian noun is **masculine**, **feminine** or **neuter**. This is a grammatical category, not a statement about sex. Tables, books and windows have a gender just as people and animals do. You can often tell which pronoun replaces a noun from its gender: <Uk>він</Uk> (he) for masculine nouns, <Uk>вона́</Uk> (she) for feminine nouns and <Uk>воно́</Uk> (it) for neuter nouns. In English, all three are usually *it* when the noun is a thing.

## Guessing gender from the ending

Look at the last letter of the dictionary form (the nominative singular). It is a good first guess, and it works for most nouns a beginner meets.

<GrammarTable caption="Typical dictionary-form endings: a beginner's guide, not a rule">

| Gender | Usual ending | Examples |
| ------ | ------------ | -------- |
| Masculine | a consonant (sometimes followed by <Uk>ь</Uk> or <Uk>й</Uk>) | <Uk>стіл</Uk> table, <Uk>брат</Uk> brother, <Uk>чай</Uk> tea, <Uk>день</Uk> day |
| Feminine | <Uk>-а</Uk> or <Uk>-я</Uk> | <Uk>ма́ма</Uk> mum, <Uk>кни́га</Uk> book, <Uk>ка́ва</Uk> coffee, <Uk>земля́</Uk> earth, land |
| Neuter | <Uk>-о</Uk>, <Uk>-е</Uk> or <Uk>-я</Uk> | <Uk>вікно́</Uk> window, <Uk>молоко́</Uk> milk, <Uk>мо́ре</Uk> sea, <Uk>навча́ння</Uk> studying |

</GrammarTable>

The guess fails often enough that the best habit is to learn every noun together with its gender. The sections below show where it fails.

## Where the guess goes wrong

### A final -я can be feminine or neuter

<Uk>Земля́</Uk> and <Uk>столи́ця</Uk> (capital city) are feminine. But many neuter nouns also end in <Uk>-я</Uk>. Two groups are easy to spot:

- **A doubled consonant before -я** usually means a neuter noun: <Uk>навча́ння</Uk> (studying), <Uk>життя́</Uk> (life). Many nouns of this kind are made from verbs and end in <Uk>-ння</Uk>.
- **Words for young animals, and a few others,** in <Uk>-я</Uk> are neuter and add extra letters before most endings: <Uk>кошеня́</Uk> (kitten), plural <Uk>кошеня́та</Uk>; <Uk>ім’я́</Uk> (name), plural <Uk>імена́</Uk>.

<ExampleList title="Neuter nouns in -я">
  <Example uk="навча́ння" en="studying, education (neuter)" />
  <Example uk="кошеня́" en="kitten (neuter); plural кошеня́та" />
  <Example uk="ім’я́" en="name, first name (neuter); plural імена́" />
</ExampleList>

### A final consonant can be feminine

Most nouns that end in a consonant are masculine, but some feminine nouns do too. A final <Uk>ь</Uk> does not settle it either way.

<ExampleList title="Same kind of ending, different gender">
  <Example uk="день" en="day (masculine)" />
  <Example uk="кінь" en="horse (masculine)" />
  <Example uk="сіль" en="salt (feminine)" />
  <Example uk="ніч" en="night (feminine)" />
</ExampleList>

### Masculine nouns in -о, -а and -я

Some masculine nouns, mostly words for men, end in a vowel:

- in <Uk>-о</Uk>: <Uk>ба́тько</Uk> (father), <Uk>та́то</Uk> (dad);
- in <Uk>-а</Uk> or <Uk>-я</Uk>: men's names such as <Uk>Мико́ла</Uk> and <Uk>Ілля́</Uk>.

These words still take masculine agreement: <Uk>мій та́то</Uk> (my dad), <Uk>мій ба́тько</Uk> (my father).

### Nouns of common gender

A few nouns in <Uk>-а</Uk> or <Uk>-я</Uk> can be either masculine or feminine. This is called **common gender**. <Uk>Сирота́</Uk> (orphan) is one: the orthography lists it as a noun of common gender, and Горох marks it as both masculine and feminine. Which gender applies depends on whether a boy or a girl, a man or a woman, is meant.

### Grammatical gender and the person meant

Gender is a property of the word. <Uk>Дити́на</Uk> (child) and <Uk>люди́на</Uk> (person, human being) are feminine nouns whether they refer to a boy or a girl, a man or a woman.

### Loanwords that never change

Some borrowed words end in a vowel and are never declined. <Uk>Кафе́</Uk> (café) and <Uk>кіно́</Uk> (cinema) are neuter, and they have the same form in every case.

### Nouns that are only plural

Some nouns exist only in the plural, even when they name a single object. They have no singular, so there is no gender to learn: words that agree with them simply take plural forms.

<ExampleList title="Plural-only nouns">
  <Example uk="две́рі" en="door, doors" />
  <Example uk="но́жиці" en="scissors" />
  <Example uk="окуля́ри" en="glasses, spectacles" />
</ExampleList>

## Why gender matters

Gender is not just a label in the dictionary. Words that go with a noun change their ending to match its gender (and its number and case). This is called **agreement**. Here is a preview with the dictionary forms of three kinds of words. You will meet each of them properly on later pages.

Every word in a phrase has to agree, so a wrong guess about gender shows up several times in a sentence. That is why it pays to learn the gender with the noun.

## How to check a noun's gender

When you are not sure, look the noun up. Ukrainian dictionaries give the gender of each noun.

- **Горох** (goroh.pp.ua) shows the gender on the word's inflection page, <Uk>словозмі́на</Uk>: for example <Uk>іме́нник, жіно́чий рід</Uk> (noun, feminine) for <Uk>кни́га</Uk>. For plural-only nouns such as <Uk>две́рі</Uk> it says <Uk>множи́нний</Uk> instead of a gender.
- **Dictionary definitions** use abbreviations after the headword, such as <Uk>ж.</Uk> (<Uk>жіно́чий</Uk>, feminine), <Uk>с.</Uk> (<Uk>сере́дній</Uk>, neuter) and <Uk>мн.</Uk> (<Uk>множина́</Uk>, plural; used for plural-only nouns).

The inflection page also shows all the case forms and the stress, which you will need later.

## Common mistakes and limits

<Note type="mistake">
  **Choosing gender by meaning.** A noun's gender comes from the word, not from the thing. <Uk>Кни́га</Uk> is feminine, and <Uk>дити́на</Uk> is feminine even for a boy.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Treating every -я as feminine, or every -ь as masculine.** <Uk>Ім’я́</Uk> and <Uk>навча́ння</Uk> are neuter. <Uk>Сіль</Uk> is feminine.
</Note>

<Note type="remember">
  The ending is a good first guess for most everyday nouns, not a rule. Learn each new noun with its gender, and check in a dictionary when the ending could point either way.
</Note>
