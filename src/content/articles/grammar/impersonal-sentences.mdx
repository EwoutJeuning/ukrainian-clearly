After this page you will be able to say how you feel with <Uk>мені́ хо́лодно</Uk> and <Uk>нам ве́село</Uk>, talk about the weather, and say what you must or may do with <Uk>тре́ба</Uk> and <Uk>мо́жна</Uk>. You will recognise verbs like <Uk>світа́є</Uk> (it is getting light), sentences like <Uk>Тут гово́рять украї́нською</Uk> (Ukrainian is spoken here), and written forms like <Uk>зачи́нено</Uk> (closed).

## Sentences without a subject

An English sentence almost always has a subject, even an empty one: *it* is cold, *it* is getting dark, *they* say. Ukrainian often has no subject at all. There is no word for the empty *it*, and the person who feels or needs something is not in the nominative. These are called **impersonal sentences** (<Uk>безособо́ві ре́чення</Uk>).

In the present tense, many of them have no verb either. In the past and future, the verb is in the neuter singular, whoever is involved: <Uk>було́</Uk>, <Uk>бу́де</Uk>, <Uk>світа́ло</Uk>.

## Мені холодно: the dative and a word in -о

The most common pattern is a person in the **[dative](/glossary/#dative)** plus a word in <Uk>-о</Uk> that describes a state. Literally, *to me [it is] cold*. [The dative case](/grammar/dative/) introduced it; here are more examples.

<ExampleList title="How someone feels">
  <Example uk="Мені́ хо́лодно." en="I'm cold." />
  <Example uk="Нам ве́село." en="We're having fun." note="Literally: to us it is merry." />
  <Example uk="Тобі́ не су́мно?" en="Aren't you sad?" note="Informal: ти (тобі́)." />
  <Example uk="Ді́тям ціка́во." en="The children are interested." />
  <Example uk="Мені́ було́ хо́лодно." en="I was cold." note="Past: було́, always neuter." />
  <Example uk="Вам бу́де ціка́во." en="You'll find it interesting." note="Formal or plural: ви (вам). Future: бу́де." />
</ExampleList>

The dictionary gives words like <Uk>хо́лодно</Uk> with the dative of the person who feels the state [src: goroh-dictionary]. Because there is no subject, <Uk>було́</Uk> stays neuter even for a man or a group: <Uk>Йому́ було́ ве́село</Uk>, <Uk>Нам було́ ве́село</Uk>.

## The weather

Without a person, the same words describe the weather or the surroundings.

<ExampleList title="Weather without a subject">
  <Example uk="Сього́дні хо́лодно." en="It's cold today." />
  <Example uk="Надво́рі жа́рко." en="It's hot outside." />
  <Example uk="Учо́ра було́ хо́лодно." en="It was cold yesterday." />
</ExampleList>

Compare sentences about rain and snow. These do have a subject, <Uk>дощ</Uk> or <Uk>сніг</Uk>, in the nominative, and the verb agrees with it. Literally, *rain goes* and *snow falls*.

<ExampleList title="Weather with a subject">
  <Example uk="Іде́ дощ." en="It's raining." note="дощ is the subject." />
  <Example uk="Па́дає сніг." en="It's snowing." note="сніг is the subject." />
  <Example uk="Ішо́в дощ." en="It was raining." note="Past: the verb is masculine, to agree with дощ." />
</ExampleList>

The last example shows the difference: <Uk>ішо́в</Uk> is masculine because <Uk>дощ</Uk> is masculine. In an impersonal sentence the past is always neuter, as in <Uk>було́ хо́лодно</Uk>.

## Треба, можна, не можна: need and permission

<Uk>Тре́ба</Uk> (it is necessary) and <Uk>мо́жна</Uk> (it is allowed, it is possible) are followed by an infinitive. The person, if there is one, is in the dative. The dictionary labels <Uk>мо́жна</Uk> as a predicative word (<Uk>присудко́ве сло́во</Uk>) [src: goroh-dictionary].

<ExampleList title="Тре́ба and мо́жна">
  <Example uk="Мені́ тре́ба працюва́ти." en="I need to work. / I have to work." />
  <Example uk="Нам тре́ба було́ йти." en="We had to go." />
  <Example uk="Тут мо́жна сі́сти?" en="May I sit here? / Can one sit here?" />
  <Example uk="Тут не мо́жна пали́ти." en="Smoking is not allowed here." />
</ExampleList>

<Uk>Не мо́жна</Uk> is written as two words and means "it is not allowed" [src: pravopys-2026, §44].

<Uk>Слід</Uk> (one should) works the same way: <Uk>Вам слід відпочи́ти.</Uk> (You should rest. Formal or plural: <Uk>ви</Uk>.) It sounds more formal. When you mean necessity, one usage guide recommends <Uk>тре́ба</Uk> as the stylistically better choice, and it marks the verb form <Uk>слі́дує</Uk> in this sense as an error [src: goroh-usage].

## Немає with the genitive

<Uk>Нема́є</Uk> (there is no) is also impersonal. What is missing goes into the **[genitive](/glossary/#genitive)**, not the nominative. [Negation](/grammar/negation/) covers this in detail.

<ExampleList title="Нема́є">
  <Example uk="У ме́не нема́є ча́су." en="I don't have time." note="ча́су: genitive of час." />
  <Example uk="Тут нема́є хлі́ба." en="There's no bread here." note="хлі́ба: genitive of хліб." />
  <Example uk="Учо́ра хлі́ба не було́." en="There was no bread yesterday." note="Past: не було́, always neuter." />
</ExampleList>

## Verbs with no subject

A few verbs describe nature or time and are used without a subject: <Uk>світа́є</Uk> (it is getting light), <Uk>смерка́ється</Uk> (it is getting dark), <Uk>вечорі́є</Uk> (evening is coming). They appear in the *he/she/it* form in the present and in the neuter in the past [src: goroh-dictionary].

<ExampleList title="Impersonal verbs">
  <Example uk="Світа́є." en="Day is breaking." />
  <Example uk="Уже́ смерка́ється." en="It's already getting dark." />
  <Example uk="Світа́ло." en="It was getting light." note="Past: neuter." />
  <Example uk="Похолода́ло." en="It has turned cold." note="Perfective past: neuter." />
</ExampleList>

## Говорять українською: "people" with the they form

To talk about people in general, Ukrainian uses the *they* form of the verb with no pronoun. English uses *they*, *people*, *one* or the passive.

<ExampleList title="The they form without вони́">
  <Example uk="Тут гово́рять украї́нською." en="Ukrainian is spoken here. / People speak Ukrainian here." />
  <Example uk="Ка́жуть, що за́втра бу́де дощ." en="They say it will rain tomorrow." />
  <Example uk="Тут продаю́ть хліб." en="Bread is sold here." />
</ExampleList>

If you add <Uk>вони́</Uk>, the sentence is about particular people: <Uk>Вони́ гово́рять украї́нською</Uk> means "They (those people) speak Ukrainian".

## Зроблено, зачинено: forms in -но and -то

Formal and written Ukrainian has a verb form that ends in <Uk>-но</Uk> or <Uk>-то</Uk> and never changes: <Uk>зро́блено</Uk> (done), <Uk>зачи́нено</Uk> (closed), <Uk>відчи́нено</Uk> (open). It is made from a passive participle such as <Uk>зро́блений</Uk> (done, made) by replacing the ending with <Uk>-о</Uk> [src: goroh-usage]. [Participles](/grammar/participles/) explains those adjectives.

A sentence with this form has no subject. The thing that the action affected is a direct object in the accusative [src: goroh-usage].

<ExampleList title="Forms in -но and -то">
  <Example uk="Робо́ту зро́блено." en="The work has been done." note="робо́ту: accusative of робо́та." />
  <Example uk="Две́рі зачи́нено." en="The door is closed." note="две́рі has only plural forms; accusative." />
  <Example uk="Зачи́нено." en="Closed." note="On a shop door." />
  <Example uk="Відчи́нено." en="Open." note="On a shop door." />
  <Example uk="Вхід заборо́нено." en="No entry." note="On a sign. Literally: entry [is] forbidden." />
  <Example uk="Робо́ту бу́де зро́блено за́втра." en="The work will be done tomorrow." note="Future: add бу́де." />
</ExampleList>

The usage note in the dictionary makes several points about these forms [src: goroh-usage]:

- **They describe an action that has just happened.** For an action further in the past, add <Uk>було́</Uk>; for the future, add <Uk>бу́де</Uk>.
- **With <Uk>не</Uk>, the object can be in the genitive.** The usage note mentions both cases; the orthography's examples use the genitive: <Uk>Пра́ці не закі́нчено</Uk> (the work has not been finished) [src: pravopys-2026, §44].
- **They are preferred for stressing the action.** The note recommends the <Uk>-но</Uk> form over a passive participle when the action matters more than the resulting state. Passive participles stay natural as descriptions, especially before a noun.

<Uk>Не</Uk> is written separately before these forms: <Uk>не зро́блено</Uk>, <Uk>не закі́нчено</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §44].

## Common mistakes and limits

<Note type="mistake">
  **Putting the person in the nominative.** *<Uk>Я хо́лодно</Uk>* is wrong. The person who feels something is in the dative: <Uk>Мені́ хо́лодно</Uk>.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Making the past agree with the person.** <Uk>Йому́ було́ хо́лодно</Uk>, <Uk>Їй було́ хо́лодно</Uk>: <Uk>було́</Uk> stays neuter. Only in sentences with a subject, like <Uk>Ішо́в дощ</Uk>, does the verb agree.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Adding the doer in the instrumental.** A usage guide marks *<Uk>Ким ви́конано завда́ння?</Uk>* (By whom has the task been done?) as a calque and recommends an ordinary active sentence: <Uk>Хто ви́конав завда́ння?</Uk> (Who did the task?) [src: goroh-usage]. If you want to say who did something, use a normal verb.
</Note>

<Note type="remember">
  Not every sentence without <Uk>я</Uk> or <Uk>він</Uk> is impersonal. In <Uk>Чита́ю</Uk> (I'm reading), the ending shows the person, and the pronoun is simply left out. An impersonal sentence has no subject at all, and the verb, if there is one, is in the *he/she/it* form or the neuter past.
</Note>
