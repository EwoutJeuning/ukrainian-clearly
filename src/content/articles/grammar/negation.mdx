After this page you will be able to make Ukrainian sentences negative, answer *no*, and use words like *nobody*, *nothing* and *never*. You will also know how to say "there is no…" and "I don't have…", which need a different case.

[Sentence structure](/grammar/sentence-structure/) gave a first look at <Uk>не</Uk>. This page goes further.

## Не: not

To make a word negative, put <Uk>не</Uk> directly in front of it. Most often this is the verb. Ukrainian has no helper verb like English *do*: <Uk>не</Uk> on its own does the job of *don't*, *doesn't* and *didn't*.

<ExampleList title="Не before the verb">
  <Example uk="Я не зна́ю." en="I don't know." />
  <Example uk="Вона́ не працю́є сього́дні." en="She isn't working today." />
  <Example uk="Ми не були́ вдо́ма." en="We weren't at home." />
  <Example uk="Я не бу́ду чита́ти." en="I won't read." />
</ExampleList>

In the future with <Uk>бу́ду</Uk>, <Uk>не</Uk> goes before <Uk>бу́ду</Uk>, as in the last example.

<Uk>Не</Uk> can also stand in front of another word, and then only that word is negated. In a sentence without a verb, it goes in front of the noun or adjective.

<ExampleList title="Не before other words">
  <Example uk="Це не мій телефо́н." en="This isn't my phone." />
  <Example uk="Не я, а брат." en="Not me, but my brother." />
  <Example uk="Він живе́ не в Ки́єві, а у Льво́ві." en="He lives not in Kyiv but in Lviv." />
</ExampleList>

### Writing не

<Uk>Не</Uk> is written as a separate word before a verb: <Uk>не зна́ю</Uk>, <Uk>не працю́ю</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §44]. A few verbs never appear without <Uk>не</Uk>, and those are written as one word. One you will meet early is <Uk>нена́видіти</Uk> (to hate). The spelling rules for <Uk>не</Uk> with nouns and adjectives are more complicated, and are left for a later page.

## Ні: no

<Uk>Ні</Uk> is the answer *no*. It stands at the start of the reply. If the reply goes on to deny an action, the verb still needs its own <Uk>не</Uk>.

<ExampleList title="Ні and не together">
  <Example uk="— Ти зна́єш? — Ні." en="“Do you know?” “No.”" note="Informal: ти." />
  <Example uk="— Ти зна́єш? — Ні, не зна́ю." en="“Do you know?” “No, I don't.”" note="Informal: ти." />
  <Example uk="— Ви студе́нтка? — Ні, я вчи́телька." en="“Are you a student?” “No, I'm a teacher.”" note="Formal: ви, to one woman." />
</ExampleList>

## Double negatives are correct

In standard English, *I don't know nothing* is a mistake. In Ukrainian, it is the normal pattern. Words like *nobody*, *nothing* and *never* start with <Uk>ні-</Uk>, and the verb also keeps its <Uk>не</Uk>.

<GrammarTable
  caption="Common negative words"
  headers={['Ukrainian', 'English', 'Example']}
  rows={[
    ['ніхто́', 'nobody, no one', 'Ніхто́ не зна́є.'],
    ['нічо́го', 'nothing', 'Я нічо́го не ба́чу.'],
    ['ніко́ли', 'never', 'Я ніко́ли не був у Льво́ві.'],
    ['ніде́', 'nowhere (at no place)', 'Він ніде́ не працю́є.'],
    ['ніку́ди', 'nowhere (to no place)', 'Я ніку́ди не йду.'],
    ['нія́кий, жо́ден', 'no, not a single', 'Жо́ден авто́бус не йде туди́.'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[0, 2]}
/>

The examples mean: *Nobody knows. I don't see anything. I've never been to Lviv* (said by a man). *He doesn't work anywhere. I'm not going anywhere. No bus goes there.*

A few notes on these words:

- **They follow the question words.** <Uk>Хто</Uk> → <Uk>ніхто́</Uk>, <Uk>де</Uk> → <Uk>ніде́</Uk>, <Uk>куди́</Uk> → <Uk>ніку́ди</Uk>, <Uk>яки́й</Uk> → <Uk>нія́кий</Uk>. See [Questions](/grammar/questions/).
- **The dictionary form of *nothing* is <Uk>ніщо́</Uk>.** In a sentence with a negative verb you will usually see the form <Uk>нічо́го</Uk>: <Uk>Я нічо́го не зна́ю.</Uk> (I don't know anything.)
- **They change their form like <Uk>хто</Uk> and <Uk>що</Uk>:** <Uk>ніко́го</Uk>, <Uk>ніко́му</Uk>, <Uk>ніки́м</Uk>. With a preposition, the word splits into two and the preposition goes in the middle: <Uk>ні з ким</Uk> (with nobody), <Uk>ні про що</Uk> (about nothing).
- **Stress matters.** <Uk>Ніко́ли</Uk> means *never*. The dictionary also lists <Uk>ні́коли</Uk>, with a different stress and the meaning "there's no time". <Uk>Ніде́</Uk> and <Uk>ні́де</Uk> differ in the same way: "nowhere" and "there's nowhere to…", as in <Uk>ні́де сі́сти</Uk> (there's nowhere to sit).

All of these are written as one word [src: pravopys-2026, §44], except when a preposition splits them.

## "There is no…" and "I don't have…"

To say that something exists or someone has something, Ukrainian uses <Uk>є</Uk> with the nominative. To say that something does not exist or is not there, it uses <Uk>нема́є</Uk>, and the noun changes to the **[genitive](/glossary/#genitive)**.

<ExampleList title="Є with the nominative, нема́є with the genitive">
  <Example uk="Тут є апте́ка." en="There is a pharmacy here." note="апте́ка: nominative." />
  <Example uk="Тут нема́є апте́ки." en="There is no pharmacy here." note="апте́ки: genitive." />
  <Example uk="У ме́не є час." en="I have time." note="Literally “By me there is time.” час: nominative." />
  <Example uk="У ме́не нема́є ча́су." en="I don't have time." note="ча́су: genitive." />
  <Example uk="Ма́ми нема́є вдо́ма." en="Mum isn't at home." note="ма́ми: genitive." />
</ExampleList>

<Uk>Нема́</Uk> is a shorter form of <Uk>нема́є</Uk> with the same meaning; the dictionary lists both. In this pattern the thing that is missing is not the subject, so it does not take the nominative.

You can also negate the person directly, with <Uk>не</Uk> and the nominative: <Uk>Ма́ма не вдо́ма.</Uk> (Mum isn't at home.) Both are correct; <Uk>нема́є</Uk> puts the weight on the absence. For "I don't have", you can also say <Uk>я не ма́ю</Uk>, again with the genitive: <Uk>Я не ма́ю ча́су.</Uk>

Repeated, <Uk>ні… ні…</Uk> means *neither… nor…*: <Uk>У ме́не нема́є ні бра́та, ні сестри́.</Uk> (I have neither a brother nor a sister.)

The past and future work the same way, with <Uk>не було́</Uk> and <Uk>не бу́де</Uk>. These forms do not change, whatever the gender or number of the noun.

<ExampleList title="In the past and future">
  <Example uk="Учо́ра ма́ми не було́ вдо́ма." en="Mum wasn't at home yesterday." />
  <Example uk="За́втра ма́ми не бу́де вдо́ма." en="Mum won't be at home tomorrow." />
</ExampleList>

## Not yet, no longer

Two small words often come before <Uk>не</Uk>:

- <Uk>ще не</Uk>: *not yet*. <Uk>Я ще не зна́ю.</Uk> (I don't know yet.)
- <Uk>уже́ не</Uk> or <Uk>вже не</Uk>: *no longer, not any more*. <Uk>Він уже́ не живе́ тут.</Uk> (He doesn't live here any more.)

## Common mistakes and limits

<Note type="mistake">
  **Dropping <Uk>не</Uk> after a negative word.** <Uk>Ніхто́ не зна́є</Uk>, not *<Uk>Ніхто́ зна́є</Uk>*. Unlike in English, the double negative is required.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **The nominative after <Uk>нема́є</Uk>.** Say <Uk>нема́є ча́су</Uk>, not *<Uk>нема́є час</Uk>*. What is missing goes into the genitive.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Writing <Uk>не</Uk> together with a verb.** <Uk>не зна́ю</Uk> is two words. Only the few verbs that do not exist without <Uk>не</Uk>, like <Uk>нена́видіти</Uk>, are written as one.
</Note>

<Note type="remember">
  Negation affects other parts of the sentence too. After a negative verb, the object is sometimes in the genitive rather than the accusative. That is a topic for later; for now, use the patterns on this page.
</Note>
