After this page you will be able to use the few Ukrainian verbs that do not follow the two groups of endings: <Uk>бу́ти</Uk> (to be), <Uk>да́ти</Uk> (to give), <Uk>ї́сти</Uk> (to eat) and verbs like <Uk>розпові́м</Uk> (I'll tell). You will also recognise the common verbs that change a consonant in the *I* form, such as <Uk>ходжу́</Uk> (I walk, I go), and the verbs whose stem looks different from the infinitive, such as <Uk>беру́</Uk> (I take) from <Uk>бра́ти</Uk>.

[The present tense](/grammar/present-tense/) introduced the two groups of endings, the first and second **[conjugation](/glossary/#conjugation)**. Almost every Ukrainian verb belongs to one of them. This page covers the exceptions and the changes you have to learn verb by verb.

## Only four verbs are truly irregular

The official orthography lists a separate group of just four verbs, with their derivatives: <Uk>да́ти</Uk>, <Uk>ї́сти</Uk>, the verbs in <Uk>-вісти</Uk> such as <Uk>відпові́м</Uk> (I'll answer) and <Uk>розпові́м</Uk> (I'll tell), and <Uk>бу́ти</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §115]. Their endings belong to neither group. Every other verb on this page is regular in its endings and only changes its stem.

## Бути: є for every person

In the present tense, <Uk>бу́ти</Uk> has one form, <Uk>є</Uk>, for every person [src: pravopys-2026, §115].

<GrammarTable
  caption="To be: present tense"
  headers={['Person', 'Ukrainian', 'English']}
  rows={[
    ['I', 'я є', 'I am'],
    ['you (informal)', 'ти є', 'you are'],
    ['he / she / it', 'він / вона́ / воно́ є', 'he / she / it is'],
    ['we', 'ми є', 'we are'],
    ['you (formal or plural)', 'ви є', 'you are'],
    ['they', 'вони́ є', 'they are'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[1]}
/>

In a sentence like <Uk>Я студе́нт</Uk> (I'm a student), the verb is normally left out, as [Sentence structure](/grammar/sentence-structure/) explains. You will hear <Uk>є</Uk> most often in the sense "there is" or "there are", and in "I have": <Uk>У ме́не є сестра́.</Uk> (I have a sister.) Its opposite is <Uk>нема́є</Uk>, which takes the genitive; see [Negation](/grammar/negation/).

The other tenses of <Uk>бу́ти</Uk> are regular and change for person, number or gender: <Uk>був</Uk>, <Uk>була́</Uk>, <Uk>було́</Uk>, <Uk>були́</Uk> in the past and <Uk>бу́ду</Uk>, <Uk>бу́деш</Uk>, <Uk>бу́де</Uk>, <Uk>бу́демо</Uk>, <Uk>бу́дете</Uk>, <Uk>бу́дуть</Uk> in the future.

<Note>
  In older texts and poetry you may meet other present forms. The orthography mentions <Uk>єсть</Uk>, sometimes used in poetry for *I* and *he/she/it*, and the archaic forms <Uk>єси́</Uk> (*you*, informal) and <Uk>суть</Uk> (*they*), which are rare [src: pravopys-2026, §115]. You only need to recognise them; in your own Ukrainian, use <Uk>є</Uk>.
</Note>

## Дати and давати: to give

<Uk>Да́ти</Uk> is perfective: it describes one complete act of giving. Perfective verbs have no present tense, so its personal forms have a future meaning: <Uk>я дам</Uk> means "I will give" [src: goroh-dictionary]. The [future tense](/grammar/future-tense/) page explains how this works.

    ['he / she / it', 'він / вона́ / воно́ дасть', 'he / she / it will give'],
    ['we', 'ми дамо́', 'we will give'],
    ['you (formal or plural)', 'ви дасте́', 'you will give'],
    ['they', 'вони́ даду́ть', 'they will give'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[1]}
/>

These forms come from the orthography's table [src: pravopys-2026, §115], with the stress from the dictionary [src: goroh-dictionary]. Notice the endings: <Uk>-м</Uk> for *I*, <Uk>-си</Uk> for *you* and <Uk>-сть</Uk> for *he/she/it*. No other kind of verb has them.

For "I give" or "I am giving", use the imperfective <Uk>дава́ти</Uk>. It is a regular first-group verb, but it drops <Uk>-ва-</Uk> in the present tense, and the stress falls on the ending:

<GrammarTable

    ['they', 'вони́ даю́ть', 'they give'],
  ]}

/>

<Uk>Встава́ти</Uk> (to get up) works the same way: <Uk>встаю́</Uk>, <Uk>встає́ш</Uk>, <Uk>встаю́ть</Uk> [src: goroh-dictionary].

## Їсти: to eat

<Uk>Ї́сти</Uk> is imperfective, so these forms are a real present tense [src: pravopys-2026, §115; goroh-dictionary].

<GrammarTable
  caption="To eat: present tense"
  headers={['Person', 'Ukrainian', 'English']}
  rows={[
    ['I', 'я їм', 'I eat, I am eating'],
    ['you (informal)', 'ти їси́', 'you eat'],
    ['he / she / it', 'він / вона́ / воно́ їсть', 'he / she / it eats'],
    ['we', 'ми їмо́', 'we eat'],
    ['you (formal or plural)', 'ви їсте́', 'you eat'],
    ['they', 'вони́ їдя́ть', 'they eat'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[1]}
/>

The *they* form has a <Uk>д</Uk> that appears nowhere else in the present: <Uk>їдя́ть</Uk>. The past tense is short: <Uk>він їв</Uk>, <Uk>вона́ ї́ла</Uk>, <Uk>вони́ ї́ли</Uk>.

The perfective <Uk>з’ї́сти</Uk> (to eat up) has the same endings with a future meaning: <Uk>з’їм</Uk>, <Uk>з’їси́</Uk>, <Uk>з’їсть</Uk>, <Uk>з’їмо́</Uk>, <Uk>з’їсте́</Uk>, <Uk>з’їдя́ть</Uk> [src: goroh-dictionary].

## Verbs in -вісти: to answer, to tell

<Uk>Відповісти</Uk> (to answer) and <Uk>розповісти</Uk> (to tell, to recount) are perfective, so again the forms have a future meaning. They take the same endings as <Uk>да́ти</Uk> and <Uk>ї́сти</Uk>.

<GrammarTable
  caption="To answer and to tell (perfective): future tense"
  headers={['Person', 'To answer', 'To tell']}
  rows={[
    ['I', 'я відпові́м', 'я розпові́м'],
    ['you (informal)', 'ти відповіси́', 'ти розповіси́'],
    ['he / she / it', 'він / вона́ відпові́сть', 'він / вона́ розпові́сть'],
    ['we', 'ми відповімо́', 'ми розповімо́'],
    ['you (formal or plural)', 'ви відповісте́', 'ви розповісте́'],
    ['they', 'вони́ відповідять', 'вони́ розповідять'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[1, 2]}
/>

The dictionary accepts two stresses in the infinitives, so they are shown unmarked on this page: <Uk>відповісти</Uk>, <Uk>розповісти</Uk> [src: goroh-dictionary]. It gives the *they* forms without a stress mark, so they are unmarked in the table. In the orthography's own table, the *they* form of <Uk>відповісти</Uk> is replaced with the phrase <Uk>даду́ть ві́дповідь</Uk> (they will give an answer) [src: pravopys-2026, §115]. Use that phrase when you need it.

The past tense is regular: <Uk>він відпові́в</Uk>, <Uk>вона́ відповіла́</Uk>; <Uk>він розпові́в</Uk>, <Uk>вона́ розповіла́</Uk> [src: goroh-dictionary]. For "I answer" or "I am telling", use the imperfective partners, which are regular first-group verbs: <Uk>відповіда́ти</Uk> (<Uk>відповіда́ю</Uk>, <Uk>відповіда́єш</Uk>) and <Uk>розповіда́ти</Uk> (<Uk>розповіда́ю</Uk>, <Uk>розповіда́єш</Uk>).

## A changed consonant in the I form

Many second-group verbs change the last consonant of the stem in the *I* form only. The other five forms keep the consonant of the infinitive. The orthography lists these changes: <Uk>д</Uk> → <Uk>дж</Uk>, <Uk>т</Uk> → <Uk>ч</Uk>, <Uk>з</Uk> → <Uk>ж</Uk>, <Uk>с</Uk> → <Uk>ш</Uk>, and <Uk>зд</Uk> → <Uk>ждж</Uk>, <Uk>ст</Uk> → <Uk>щ</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §15–17, §115].

<Note type="remember">
  **The stress often moves too.** In <Uk>ходжу́</Uk>, <Uk>ношу́</Uk> and <Uk>плачу́</Uk>, the stress is on the ending. In the other forms it moves back to the stem: <Uk>хо́диш</Uk>, <Uk>но́сиш</Uk>, <Uk>пла́тиш</Uk>. <Uk>Сиді́ти</Uk> is different: its stress stays near the end in every form (<Uk>сиджу́</Uk>, <Uk>сиди́ш</Uk>, <Uk>сидя́ть</Uk>).
</Note>

## A different stem in every form

Some first-group verbs look quite different in the present tense from their infinitive. The change runs through all six forms, so once you know the *I* and *you* forms you can build the rest. The orthography notes that in first-group verbs a consonant change in the *I* form is kept in every present form, as in <Uk>могти́</Uk> → <Uk>мо́жу</Uk>, <Uk>мо́жеш</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §115].

    ['жи́ти', 'живу́', 'живе́ш', 'живу́ть', 'to live'],

All the forms are from the dictionary [src: goroh-dictionary]. A few notes:

- **<Uk>Хоті́ти</Uk> is a first-group verb**, although it ends in <Uk>-іти</Uk>: <Uk>хо́чу</Uk>, <Uk>хо́чеш</Uk>, <Uk>хо́че</Uk>, <Uk>хо́чемо</Uk>, <Uk>хо́чете</Uk>, <Uk>хо́чуть</Uk>. The dictionary marks it as the first conjugation [src: goroh-dictionary].
- **<Uk>Бра́ти</Uk> gains an <Uk>е</Uk>**: <Uk>беру́</Uk>, <Uk>бере́ш</Uk>, <Uk>бере́</Uk>, <Uk>беремо́</Uk>, <Uk>берете́</Uk>, <Uk>беру́ть</Uk>. The stress stays on the ending.
- **<Uk>Жи́ти</Uk> gains a <Uk>в</Uk>**: <Uk>живу́</Uk>, <Uk>живе́ш</Uk>, <Uk>живе́</Uk>, <Uk>живемо́</Uk>, <Uk>живете́</Uk>, <Uk>живу́ть</Uk>.
- **<Uk>Могти́</Uk> has an unusual past tense**: <Uk>він міг</Uk>, <Uk>вона́ могла́</Uk>, <Uk>вони́ могли́</Uk>.

### Іти or йти

<Uk>Іти́</Uk> and <Uk>йти</Uk> are the same verb, and so are <Uk>іду́</Uk> and <Uk>йду</Uk>. The choice follows the same sound rule as the word for "and": <Uk>і-</Uk> after a consonant, <Uk>й-</Uk> after a vowel [src: pravopys-2026, §24]. The orthography's own examples are <Uk>Він іде́</Uk> and <Uk>Вона́ йде</Uk>.

All twelve forms are in the dictionary [src: goroh-dictionary]. Since the pronouns <Uk>я</Uk>, <Uk>ти</Uk>, <Uk>ми</Uk>, <Uk>ви</Uk> and <Uk>вони́</Uk> end in a vowel, the rule gives the <Uk>й-</Uk> forms after them: <Uk>я йду</Uk>, <Uk>ми йдемо́</Uk>.

The past tense has the same two versions: <Uk>він ішо́в</Uk>, <Uk>вона́ йшла</Uk>, <Uk>вони́ йшли</Uk> [src: goroh-dictionary].

## Common mistakes and limits

<Note type="mistake">
  **Using <Uk>дам</Uk> for "I give".** <Uk>Я дам</Uk> means "I will give". For the present, use the imperfective: <Uk>я даю́</Uk>. The same goes for <Uk>відпові́м</Uk> (I'll answer) and <Uk>відповіда́ю</Uk> (I answer, I am answering).
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Keeping the infinitive's consonant in the I form.** Say <Uk>ходжу́</Uk>, <Uk>ношу́</Uk>, <Uk>плачу́</Uk>, not forms built straight from the infinitive with <Uk>д</Uk>, <Uk>с</Uk> or <Uk>т</Uk>. And do not carry the change into the other forms: <Uk>хо́диш</Uk>, not a form with <Uk>дж</Uk>.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Confusing <Uk>плачу́</Uk> and <Uk>пла́чу</Uk>.** Only the stress tells them apart. <Uk>Плачу́</Uk>, stressed on the ending, is "I pay" (from <Uk>плати́ти</Uk>). <Uk>Пла́чу</Uk>, stressed on the first syllable, is "I cry" (from <Uk>пла́кати</Uk>) [src: goroh-dictionary].
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Giving <Uk>хоті́ти</Uk> second-group endings.** <Uk>Ти хо́чеш</Uk>, <Uk>вона́ хо́че</Uk>: the endings have <Uk>е</Uk>, like <Uk>пи́шеш</Uk>, <Uk>пи́ше</Uk>.
</Note>

<Note type="remember">
  For every new verb, learn the infinitive, the *I* form and the *you* (<Uk>ти</Uk>) form. Together they show the group, any consonant change and where the stress goes. A dictionary such as <Uk>Горох</Uk> lists all the present-tense forms with their stress.
</Note>
