After this page you will be able to put an adjective in any case to match its noun: <Uk>у ново́му буди́нку</Uk> (in the new house), <Uk>з си́ньою ру́чкою</Uk> (with the blue pen), <Uk>для мого́ бра́та</Uk> (for my brother). You will know the full sets of hard and soft endings, when the accusative looks like the genitive, and how <Uk>мій</Uk>, <Uk>твій</Uk>, <Uk>наш</Uk> and <Uk>ваш</Uk> change.

[Adjectives and agreement](/grammar/adjectives-and-agreement/) covered the nominative: <Uk>нови́й</Uk>, <Uk>нова́</Uk>, <Uk>нове́</Uk>, <Uk>нові́</Uk>. Here are the other cases.

## Agreement in case

An adjective agrees with its noun in gender, number **and case**. If the noun is in the instrumental, so is the adjective. The noun's case comes from its job in the sentence or from a preposition, as the [case overview](/grammar/case-overview/) explains.

<ExampleList title="One phrase in three cases">
  <Example uk="Це нова́ кварти́ра." en="This is a new flat." note="Nominative: the subject." />
  <Example uk="У ме́не нема́є ново́ї кварти́ри." en="I don't have a new flat." note="Genitive: after a negative." />
  <Example uk="Я живу́ в нові́й кварти́рі." en="I live in a new flat." note="Locative: after в (in)." />
</ExampleList>

## Hard adjectives: новий

Most adjectives are hard, with <Uk>-ий</Uk> in the dictionary form. These are the forms of <Uk>нови́й</Uk> (new). The masculine and neuter columns are side by side because they differ only in the nominative and accusative.

The endings to learn, without the stem:

- **Masculine and neuter:** <Uk>-ого</Uk>, <Uk>-ому</Uk>, <Uk>-им</Uk>, <Uk>-ому</Uk>.
- **Feminine:** <Uk>-ої</Uk>, <Uk>-ій</Uk>, <Uk>-у</Uk>, <Uk>-ою</Uk>, <Uk>-ій</Uk>.
- **Plural, all genders:** <Uk>-их</Uk>, <Uk>-им</Uk>, <Uk>-ими</Uk>, <Uk>-их</Uk>.

The stress usually stays on the same part of the word as in the dictionary form. <Uk>Нови́й</Uk> is stressed on the ending in every form. <Uk>До́брий</Uk> (good) is stressed on the stem in every form: <Uk>до́брого</Uk>, <Uk>до́брій</Uk>, <Uk>до́брими</Uk>.

In the masculine and neuter locative, the spelling standard gives <Uk>-ому</Uk> first and <Uk>-ім</Uk> as a second form. This page uses <Uk>-ому</Uk>.

## Soft adjectives: синій

Soft adjectives, with <Uk>-ій</Uk> in the dictionary form, have the same endings with a soft <Uk>н</Uk>. In writing, <Uk>о</Uk> after the soft consonant becomes <Uk>ьо</Uk>, <Uk>а</Uk> becomes <Uk>я</Uk>, <Uk>у</Uk> becomes <Uk>ю</Uk>, and <Uk>и</Uk> becomes <Uk>і</Uk>.

Compare <Uk>ново́го</Uk> and <Uk>си́нього</Uk>, <Uk>ново́ю</Uk> and <Uk>си́ньою</Uk>, <Uk>нови́х</Uk> and <Uk>си́ніх</Uk>. Other soft adjectives, such as <Uk>оста́нній</Uk> (last) and <Uk>лі́тній</Uk> (summer), follow <Uk>си́ній</Uk>.

<ExampleList title="Hard and soft adjectives in different cases">

  <Example uk="Ми живемо́ в ново́му буди́нку." en="We live in a new house." note="Locative after в." />
  <Example uk="Я зателефону́ю ново́му сусі́дові." en="I'll call the new neighbour." note="Dative: телефонува́ти and зателефонува́ти take the dative." />
  <Example uk="Ми говори́ли з нови́м сусі́дом." en="We were talking to the new neighbour." note="Instrumental after з." />
  <Example uk="Це оста́нній авто́бус." en="This is the last bus." />
  <Example uk="Ми чека́ли на оста́нній авто́бус." en="We were waiting for the last bus." note="Accusative of a thing: the same as the nominative." />
</ExampleList>

## The accusative of people and animals

In the accusative, masculine singular and all plural adjectives have two possible forms. Which one you use depends on the noun:

- **For people and animals,** the accusative is the same as the genitive: <Uk>ново́го студе́нта</Uk>, <Uk>нови́х студе́нтів</Uk>.
- **For things,** the accusative is the same as the nominative: <Uk>нови́й буди́нок</Uk>, <Uk>нові́ буди́нки</Uk>.

The noun follows the same rule, and the adjective simply agrees with it. Feminine and neuter singular adjectives are not affected: feminine has its own accusative <Uk>-у</Uk> (<Uk>-ю</Uk>), and neuter is like the nominative.

<ExampleList title="People and things in the accusative">
  <Example uk="Я ба́чу нови́й буди́нок." en="I can see a new house." note="A thing: like the nominative." />
  <Example uk="Я ба́чу ново́го студе́нта." en="I can see the new student." note="A person: like the genitive." />
  <Example uk="Я ба́чу нові́ буди́нки." en="I can see new houses." note="Things, plural: like the nominative." />
  <Example uk="Я ба́чу нови́х студе́нтів." en="I can see the new students." note="People, plural: like the genitive." />
</ExampleList>

The same split is behind <Uk>Я ба́чу бра́та</Uk> but <Uk>Я ба́чу стіл</Uk> in the [case overview](/grammar/case-overview/).

## My, your, our: possessives in all cases

The possessives change for case too, and they agree with the noun that is owned, as [Pronouns](/grammar/pronouns/) explained. <Uk>Наш</Uk> and <Uk>ваш</Uk> take the endings of hard adjectives. <Uk>Мій</Uk> and <Uk>твій</Uk> have endings of their own.

<Uk>Твій</Uk> (your, informal) changes in exactly the same way: <Uk>твого́</Uk>, <Uk>твоє́му</Uk>, <Uk>твої́й</Uk>, <Uk>твоє́ю</Uk>, <Uk>твої́ми</Uk>.

<Uk>Ваш</Uk> (your, formal or plural) changes in exactly the same way: <Uk>ва́шого</Uk>, <Uk>ва́шому</Uk>, <Uk>ва́шій</Uk>, <Uk>ва́шою</Uk>, <Uk>ва́шими</Uk>. <Uk>Ї́хній</Uk> (their) takes the endings of soft adjectives, like <Uk>си́ній</Uk>.

<ExampleList title="Possessives in different cases">
  <Example uk="Це кварти́ра мого́ бра́та." en="This is my brother's flat." note="Genitive: of my brother." />
  <Example uk="Мого́ бра́та зва́ти Андрі́й." en="My brother's name is Andrii." note="Accusative of a person: мого́ бра́та." />
  <Example uk="Я зателефону́ю твої́й сестрі́." en="I'll call your sister." note="Informal: твої́й goes with ти. Dative." />
  <Example uk="Ви зна́єте на́шого вчи́теля?" en="Do you know our teacher?" note="Formal or plural: ви. Accusative of a person." />
  <Example uk="Вона́ живе́ в на́шому мі́сті." en="She lives in our town." note="Locative after в." />
  <Example uk="Я живу́ з мої́ми батька́ми." en="I live with my parents." note="Instrumental after з." />
</ExampleList>

His, her and their with <Uk>його́</Uk>, <Uk>її́</Uk> and <Uk>їх</Uk> never change, whatever the case: <Uk>до його́ бра́та</Uk>, <Uk>з її́ сестро́ю</Uk>. [Personal pronouns in all cases](/grammar/pronoun-cases/) shows why they do not take <Uk>н-</Uk>.

## Common mistakes and limits

<Note type="mistake">
  **Leaving the adjective in the nominative.** The adjective takes the case of its noun: <Uk>у нові́й кварти́рі</Uk>, not *<Uk>у нова́ кварти́рі</Uk>*; <Uk>з мої́м бра́том</Uk>, not *<Uk>з мій бра́том</Uk>*.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Using the nominative-like accusative for a person.** <Uk>Я ба́чу ново́го студе́нта</Uk>, not *<Uk>Я ба́чу нови́й студе́нт</Uk>*. With things, the nominative-like form is right: <Uk>Я ба́чу нови́й буди́нок</Uk>.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Hard endings on a soft adjective.** <Uk>Си́ній</Uk> keeps soft endings in every case: <Uk>си́нього</Uk>, <Uk>си́ньою</Uk>, <Uk>си́ніх</Uk>.
</Note>

<Note type="remember">
  The masculine and neuter share every form except the nominative and accusative. The genitive, dative, instrumental and locative plural are the same for all three genders. So the full table is smaller than it looks.
</Note>

<Note>
  This page does not cover <Uk>свій</Uk> (one's own), which changes like <Uk>мій</Uk> but has its own rules for when to use it. It comes on a later page. Ukrainian also has a few short masculine adjective forms, such as <Uk>зго́ден</Uk> (agreed) and <Uk>пови́нен</Uk> (must), which the spelling standard lists. Learn them as set words.
</Note>
