After this page you will be able to talk about the future in Ukrainian in two ways: with <Uk>бу́ду</Uk> and an infinitive, and with a one-word form such as <Uk>чита́тиму</Uk>. You will also recognise a third kind of future form, which looks like the present tense but is not.

Which form a verb uses depends on its **[aspect](/glossary/#aspect)**. [The past tense](/grammar/past-tense/) introduced it briefly. Here is a reminder, because the future is where aspect first changes the forms, not just the meaning.

## Two kinds of verb

Most Ukrainian verbs come in pairs that share one English translation:

- the **imperfective** verb presents an action as a process, or as something repeated;
- the **perfective** verb presents an action as completed, with a result.

At this stage you only need to recognise the two kinds. The <Uk>Горох</Uk> dictionary labels each verb as imperfective (<Uk>недоко́наний вид</Uk>) or perfective (<Uk>доко́наний вид</Uk>). The rest of this page shows what each kind does in the future.

## Imperfective verbs: two future forms

An imperfective verb has two future forms. They mean the same thing, and both are correct.

### The helper verb and an infinitive

The first form is like English *will* + verb. Take the future of <Uk>бу́ти</Uk> (to be) and add the infinitive. Only <Uk>бу́ти</Uk> changes for person; the infinitive stays the same.

<GrammarTable
  caption="бу́ду чита́ти (I will read, I will be reading)"
  headers={['Person', 'Ukrainian', 'English (rough)']}
  rows={[
    ['I', 'я бу́ду чита́ти', 'I will read'],
    ['you (informal)', 'ти бу́деш чита́ти', 'you will read'],
    ['he / she / it', 'він / вона́ / воно́ бу́де чита́ти', 'he / she / it will read'],
    ['we', 'ми бу́демо чита́ти', 'we will read'],
    ['you (formal or plural)', 'ви бу́дете чита́ти', 'you will read'],
    ['they', 'вони́ бу́дуть чита́ти', 'they will read'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[1]}
/>

### One word

The second form is a single word. Take the whole infinitive, including <Uk>-ти</Uk>, and add <Uk>-му</Uk>, <Uk>-меш</Uk>, <Uk>-ме</Uk>, <Uk>-мемо</Uk>, <Uk>-мете</Uk> or <Uk>-муть</Uk>. In the verbs on this page, the stress stays where it is in the infinitive.

<ExampleList title="Two forms, one meaning">
  <Example uk="За́втра я бу́ду працюва́ти." en="Tomorrow I will work. / Tomorrow I will be working." />
  <Example uk="За́втра я працюва́тиму." en="Tomorrow I will work. / Tomorrow I will be working." note="The same meaning as the sentence above." />
  <Example uk="Що ти бу́деш роби́ти за́втра?" en="What will you do tomorrow?" note="Informal: ти." />
  <Example uk="Ми бу́демо жи́ти в Ки́єві." en="We will live in Kyiv." />
  <Example uk="Вони́ писа́тимуть." en="They will write. / They will be writing." />
</ExampleList>

Like the present tense, both forms show the person in their ending, so the pronoun can be left out when the context is clear: <Uk>За́втра працюва́тиму.</Uk> (Tomorrow I'll be working.)

## Perfective verbs: the simple future

Perfective verbs have no present tense, because an action seen as completed cannot be going on now. Their forms with present-tense-style endings mean the **future**: a single action that will be completed.

<GrammarTable
  caption="прочита́ти (to read, perfective): future"
  headers={['Person', 'Ukrainian', 'English (rough)']}
  rows={[
    ['I', 'я прочита́ю', 'I will read (and finish)'],
    ['you (informal)', 'ти прочита́єш', 'you will read'],
    ['he / she / it', 'він / вона́ / воно́ прочита́є', 'he / she / it will read'],
    ['we', 'ми прочита́ємо', 'we will read'],
    ['you (formal or plural)', 'ви прочита́єте', 'you will read'],
    ['they', 'вони́ прочита́ють', 'they will read'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[1]}
/>

Compare <Uk>чита́ю</Uk> (I am reading: imperfective, present) with <Uk>прочита́ю</Uk> (I will read it: perfective, future). The endings are the same. The verb is different, and so is the time.

As in the present tense, the stress of some verbs moves between the forms. <Uk>Написа́ти</Uk> has <Uk>напишу́</Uk> (I will write), but <Uk>напи́шеш</Uk> (you will write, informal) and <Uk>напи́шуть</Uk> (they will write). <Uk>Купи́ти</Uk> has <Uk>куплю́</Uk> (I will buy), but <Uk>ку́пиш</Uk> (you will buy, informal) and <Uk>ку́плять</Uk>. Check new verbs in a dictionary.

<ExampleList title="The simple future of perfective verbs">
  <Example uk="Я прочита́ю цю кни́гу." en="I will read this book (to the end)." />
  <Example uk="Я напишу́ повідо́млення." en="I will write a message." />
  <Example uk="За́втра я куплю́ ка́ву." en="Tomorrow I will buy coffee." />
  <Example uk="Що ти зро́биш?" en="What will you do?" note="Informal: ти. Perfective: asking about one completed action." />
</ExampleList>

## Imperfective or perfective future?

As a rough guide only:

- Use an **imperfective** future (<Uk>бу́ду чита́ти</Uk> or <Uk>чита́тиму</Uk>) when you are thinking of the activity itself, how long it lasts, or something that will happen repeatedly.
- Use a **perfective** future (<Uk>прочита́ю</Uk>) when you are thinking of one action that will be finished, with a result.

<ExampleList title="Process or result">
  <Example uk="За́втра я бу́ду чита́ти." en="Tomorrow I will be reading." note="Imperfective: the activity." />
  <Example uk="За́втра я прочита́ю кни́гу." en="Tomorrow I will read (and finish) the book." note="Perfective: the result." />
</ExampleList>

Choosing between the two takes practice; [Aspect](/grammar/aspect/) explains it in detail. For now, aim to recognise which kind of future you are reading or hearing.

## The future of "to be"

The verb <Uk>бу́ти</Uk> (to be) has its own future forms, the same ones used in <Uk>бу́ду чита́ти</Uk>: <Uk>бу́ду</Uk>, <Uk>бу́деш</Uk>, <Uk>бу́де</Uk>, <Uk>бу́демо</Uk>, <Uk>бу́дете</Uk>, <Uk>бу́дуть</Uk>. On their own they mean *will be*. In the present tense *to be* is usually left out, but in the future it is used.

<ExampleList title="To be, in the future">
  <Example uk="За́втра я бу́ду вдо́ма." en="Tomorrow I will be at home." />
  <Example uk="Ви бу́дете вдо́ма?" en="Will you be at home?" note="Formal or plural: ви." />
</ExampleList>

## Common mistakes and limits

<Note type="mistake">
  **<Uk>бу́ду</Uk> with a perfective verb.** The form with <Uk>бу́ду</Uk> is only for imperfective verbs. Say <Uk>я бу́ду чита́ти</Uk> or <Uk>я прочита́ю</Uk>, never <Uk>бу́ду</Uk> with <Uk>прочита́ти</Uk>.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Reading a perfective form as present.** <Uk>Я прочита́ю</Uk> looks like <Uk>я чита́ю</Uk>, but it means *I will read*, not *I am reading*.
</Note>

<Note type="remember">
  For an imperfective verb, <Uk>бу́ду чита́ти</Uk> and <Uk>чита́тиму</Uk> mean the same. Pick one and use it; learn to recognise both.
</Note>
