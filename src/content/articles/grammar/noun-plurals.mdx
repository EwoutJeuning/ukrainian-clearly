After this page you will be able to make the plural of most Ukrainian nouns from the dictionary form. You will know which ending to choose, why the stress often moves in the plural, and how to handle the common irregular plurals. You will also recognise nouns that exist only in the plural and nouns that never change at all.

This page covers the **[nominative](/glossary/#nominative)** plural: the form for the subject, and the form a dictionary gives as "the plural". The plural has all seven cases too. The [accusative](/grammar/accusative/) and [genitive](/grammar/genitive/) plurals have their own pages.

## Hard, soft and mixed nouns

English mostly adds *-s*. Ukrainian chooses an ending from two things: the noun's gender, and the last consonant of its stem (the noun without its ending). Grammars divide nouns into three groups by that consonant [src: pravopys-2026, §67]:

- **Hard:** the stem ends in an ordinary hard consonant, as in <Uk>кни́га</Uk>, <Uk>студе́нт</Uk>, <Uk>мі́сто</Uk>.
- **Soft:** the stem ends in a soft consonant. You can usually see this from <Uk>-я</Uk>, <Uk>-ь</Uk> or <Uk>-й</Uk> in the dictionary form, as in <Uk>пі́сня</Uk>, <Uk>учи́тель</Uk>, <Uk>музе́й</Uk>, <Uk>мо́ре</Uk>.
- **Mixed:** the stem ends in one of the "hushing" consonants <Uk>ж</Uk>, <Uk>ч</Uk>, <Uk>ш</Uk> or <Uk>щ</Uk>, as in <Uk>пло́ща</Uk>, <Uk>ключ</Uk>, <Uk>прі́звище</Uk>.

The rule of thumb is short. **Hard nouns take <Uk>-и</Uk>. Soft and mixed nouns take <Uk>-і</Uk>**, written <Uk>-ї</Uk> after a vowel or an apostrophe. **Neuter nouns take <Uk>-а</Uk> or <Uk>-я</Uk>.** The tables below show this group by group.

The same endings apply to the few masculine nouns that end in <Uk>-а</Uk> or <Uk>-я</Uk>, such as <Uk>суддя́</Uk> (judge): <Uk>су́дді</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §75].

## Masculine nouns ending in a consonant

<GrammarTable

You can't always see from the dictionary form that a noun in <Uk>-р</Uk> is soft. <Uk>Лі́кар</Uk> is soft (<Uk>лікарі́</Uk>), but <Uk>до́лар</Uk> is hard (<Uk>до́лари</Uk>) [src: pravopys-2026, §67]. The dictionary plural settles it.

### The stem can change

Two changes inside the stem are common in masculine nouns [src: pravopys-2026, §9]:

- **<Uk>і</Uk> becomes <Uk>о</Uk> or <Uk>е</Uk>.** Many nouns have <Uk>і</Uk> in the last syllable of the dictionary form, but <Uk>о</Uk> or <Uk>е</Uk> in every other form: <Uk>стіл</Uk> → <Uk>столи́</Uk>, <Uk>кіт</Uk> → <Uk>коти́</Uk>, <Uk>ніж</Uk> → <Uk>ножі́</Uk>, <Uk>кінь</Uk> → <Uk>ко́ні</Uk>.
- **A vowel drops out.** In some nouns the <Uk>о</Uk> or <Uk>е</Uk> of the last syllable disappears: <Uk>буди́нок</Uk> → <Uk>буди́нки</Uk>, <Uk>день</Uk> → <Uk>дні</Uk>, <Uk>оліве́ць</Uk> → <Uk>олівці́</Uk>.

## Neuter nouns

Neuter nouns take <Uk>-а</Uk> or <Uk>-я</Uk> in the plural [src: pravopys-2026, §88].

<GrammarTable
  caption="Nominative plural of neuter nouns"
  headers={['Singular ends in', 'Singular', 'Plural', 'Ending']}
  rows={[
    ['-о', 'мі́сто, вікно́, я́блуко', 'міста́, ві́кна, я́блука', '-о → -а'],
    ['-е (soft)', 'мо́ре, по́ле, се́рце', 'моря́, поля́, серця́', '-е → -я'],
    ['-е after ж, ч, ш, щ', 'прі́звище', 'прі́звища', '-е → -а'],
    ['-я', 'завда́ння, пита́ння', 'завда́ння, пита́ння', 'no visible change'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[1, 2]}
/>

Nouns like <Uk>завда́ння</Uk> (task) look the same in the singular and the plural. The words around them show the number: <Uk>це завда́ння</Uk> (this task), <Uk>ці завда́ння</Uk> (these tasks).

## Feminine nouns ending in a consonant

Feminine nouns that end in a consonant take <Uk>-і</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §96]. The <Uk>і</Uk> of the stem often changes here as well: <Uk>ніч</Uk> → <Uk>но́чі</Uk> (nights).

## Stress often moves in the plural

The stress of the plural is often on a different syllable from the singular. It can move towards the start of the word or towards the end, and you can't predict which.

<GrammarTable
  caption="Stress that moves in the plural"
  headers={['Singular', 'Plural', 'Meaning']}
  rows={[
    ['сестра́', 'се́стри', 'sister, sisters'],
    ['вода́', 'во́ди', 'water, waters'],
    ['рука́', 'ру́ки', 'hand, hands'],
    ['вікно́', 'ві́кна', 'window, windows'],
    ['село́', 'се́ла', 'village, villages'],
    ['мі́сто', 'міста́', 'city, cities'],
    ['сло́во', 'слова́', 'word, words'],
    ['жі́нка', 'жінки́', 'woman, women'],
    ['брат', 'брати́', 'brother, brothers'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[0, 1]}
/>

Stress matters because some forms are spelled alike. <Uk>Сестри́</Uk> (of a sister) and <Uk>се́стри</Uk> (sisters) differ only in stress. So do <Uk>кни́жки</Uk> (of a book) and <Uk>книжки́</Uk> (books) [src: pravopys-2026, §69, note]. Learn each plural together with its stress. Dictionaries such as <Uk>Горох</Uk> show it.

## Irregular plurals

A few common nouns form their plural in an unexpected way.

<GrammarTable
  caption="Common irregular plurals"
  headers={['Singular', 'Plural', 'Meaning', 'What happens']}
  rows={[
    ['люди́на', 'лю́ди', 'person, people', 'a different word in the plural'],
    ['дити́на', 'ді́ти', 'child, children', 'a different word in the plural'],
    ['друг', 'дру́зі', 'friend, friends', 'г becomes з before -і'],
    ['о́ко', 'о́чі', 'eye, eyes', 'к becomes ч; ending -і'],
    ['ім’я́', 'імена́', 'name, names', '-ен- is added'],
    ['кошеня́', 'кошеня́та', 'kitten, kittens', '-ят- is added'],
    ['кия́нин', 'кия́ни', 'person from Kyiv, people from Kyiv', '-ин is dropped'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[0, 1]}
/>

Some notes on these groups:

- **<Uk>Лю́ди</Uk> and <Uk>ді́ти</Uk>** are separate words that serve as the plurals of <Uk>люди́на</Uk> and <Uk>дити́на</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §100, note].
- **Young animals** follow the pattern of <Uk>кошеня́</Uk>: <Uk>теля́</Uk> → <Uk>теля́та</Uk> (calves), <Uk>курча́</Uk> → <Uk>курча́та</Uk> (chicks). <Uk>Дівча́</Uk> (girl) has the plural <Uk>дівча́та</Uk>, which you will hear very often [src: pravopys-2026, §99].
- **Nouns in <Uk>-анин</Uk>, <Uk>-янин</Uk>**, often names for where people come from, lose <Uk>-ин</Uk> in the plural: <Uk>громадя́нин</Uk> → <Uk>громадя́ни</Uk> (citizens) [src: pravopys-2026, §88, note].
- **<Uk>Ма́ти</Uk>** (mother) has the plural <Uk>матері́</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §97]. In everyday speech, <Uk>ма́ма</Uk> (mum) is more common.

## Nouns that are only plural

Some nouns have no singular, even when they name one object [src: pravopys-2026, §100]. [Nouns and gender](/grammar/nouns-and-gender/) introduced a few of them.

## Nouns that never change

Many nouns borrowed from other languages end in a vowel such as <Uk>-е</Uk>, <Uk>-і</Uk>, <Uk>-о</Uk> or <Uk>-ю</Uk>. These never change their form: the singular, the plural and every case look the same [src: pravopys-2026, §140].

<GrammarTable
  caption="Some nouns that never change"

  rows={[

    ['кіно́', 'cinema, film'],
    ['метро́', 'underground, metro'],
    ['таксі́', 'taxi'],
    ['ра́діо', 'radio'],
    ['меню́', 'menu'],
  ]}

/>

<ExampleList title="The same form in the singular and plural">
  <Example uk="Це кафе́ нове́." en="This café is new." note="нове́: singular." />
  <Example uk="Ці кафе́ нові́." en="These cafés are new." note="нові́: plural. Only the other words show the number." />
</ExampleList>

One common word is an exception. <Uk>Пальто́</Uk> (coat) ends in <Uk>-о</Uk> but changes like an ordinary noun, by tradition: <Uk>па́льта</Uk> (coats) [src: pravopys-2026, §140; goroh-dictionary].

## Words that agree with a plural noun

In the plural, adjectives and the past tense have one form for all three genders. So the plural ending of the adjective is the same whatever the noun's gender: <Uk>нові́ кни́ги</Uk>, <Uk>нові́ студе́нти</Uk>, <Uk>нові́ ві́кна</Uk>. See [Adjectives and agreement](/grammar/adjectives-and-agreement/).

## Common mistakes and limits

<Note type="mistake">
  **Using <Uk>-и</Uk> after a soft or mixed stem.** Say <Uk>ку́хні</Uk> (kitchens), not *<Uk>ку́хни</Uk>*, and <Uk>това́риші</Uk>, not *<Uk>това́риши</Uk>*. After <Uk>-я</Uk>, <Uk>-ь</Uk>, <Uk>-й</Uk> and the consonants <Uk>ж</Uk>, <Uk>ч</Uk>, <Uk>ш</Uk>, <Uk>щ</Uk>, the plural ends in <Uk>-і</Uk> or <Uk>-ї</Uk>.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Giving neuter nouns <Uk>-и</Uk>.** Neuter nouns take <Uk>-а</Uk> or <Uk>-я</Uk>: <Uk>ві́кна</Uk>, not *<Uk>ві́кни</Uk>*; <Uk>моря́</Uk>, not *<Uk>мо́рі</Uk>*.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Changing a noun that never changes.** <Uk>кафе́</Uk> stays <Uk>кафе́</Uk> in the plural, and <Uk>метро́</Uk> stays <Uk>метро́</Uk>.
</Note>

<Note type="remember">
  Learn every noun with its plural and the stress of the plural: <Uk>сестра́ — се́стри</Uk>, <Uk>мі́сто — міста́</Uk>, <Uk>стіл — столи́</Uk>. The endings follow rules, but the stress and the stem changes have to be learned word by word.
</Note>

<Note>
  This page shows the main patterns. Some nouns have two accepted plurals, sometimes with different meanings, and a few have no plural at all. A dictionary entry is the final word.
</Note>
