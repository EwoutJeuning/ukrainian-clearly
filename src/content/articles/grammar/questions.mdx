After this page you will be able to ask simple questions in Ukrainian: yes/no questions, and questions with words like *who*, *where* and *when*. You will know the difference between the three words for *where*, and why *who*, *what*, *which* and *whose* change their form.

[Sentence structure](/grammar/sentence-structure/) gave a first look at questions. This page goes further.

## Yes/no questions

### With intonation alone

The simplest yes/no question has the same words and the same order as a statement. Only the intonation changes, and in writing the question mark shows it.

<ExampleList title="Statement and question">
  <Example uk="Ти вдо́ма." en="You are at home." note="Informal: ти." />
  <Example uk="Ти вдо́ма?" en="Are you at home?" note="Informal: ти." />
  <Example uk="Ви розмовля́єте украї́нською?" en="Do you speak Ukrainian?" note="Formal or plural: ви." />
</ExampleList>

### With чи

You can also start a yes/no question with the small word <Uk>чи</Uk>. It has no English translation: it simply marks the sentence as a question. The rest of the sentence stays the same.

<ExampleList title="Questions with чи">
  <Example uk="Чи ти вдо́ма?" en="Are you at home?" note="Informal: ти." />
  <Example uk="Чи є тут апте́ка?" en="Is there a pharmacy here?" />
  <Example uk="Чи мо́жна тут сі́сти?" en="May I sit here? / Can one sit here?" />
</ExampleList>

Both kinds of question are correct. Intonation alone is very common in everyday speech. Starting with <Uk>чи</Uk> makes the question clear even in writing, where you cannot hear the intonation.

<Uk>Чи</Uk> also means *whether* or *if* in the middle of a sentence: <Uk>Я не зна́ю, чи він удо́ма.</Uk> (I don't know whether he is at home.) And between two options it means *or*: <Uk>ка́ва чи чай?</Uk> (coffee or tea?).

### Answering

*Yes* is <Uk>так</Uk> and *no* is <Uk>ні</Uk>. Because the verb ending already shows the person, you can also answer with the verb alone.

<ExampleList title="Short answers">
  <Example uk="— Ти зна́єш Ки́їв? — Так, зна́ю." en="“Do you know Kyiv?” “Yes, I do.”" note="Informal: ти. Зна́ю alone means “I know”." />
  <Example uk="— Ви студе́нт? — Ні, я лі́кар." en="“Are you a student?” “No, I'm a doctor.”" note="Formal: ви, to one man." />
</ExampleList>

To answer *no* to a question about an action, you usually also need <Uk>не</Uk> before the verb: <Uk>Ні, не зна́ю.</Uk> [Negation](/grammar/negation/) explains how <Uk>ні</Uk> and <Uk>не</Uk> work together.

## Question words

A question with a question word puts that word at the front. These are the most common ones.

<GrammarTable
  caption="Common question words"
  headers={['Ukrainian', 'English', 'Example']}
  rows={[
    ['хто', 'who', 'Хто це?'],
    ['що', 'what', 'Що ти ро́биш?'],
    ['де', 'where (at what place)', 'Де ти живе́ш?'],
    ['куди́', 'where (to), which way', 'Куди́ ти йдеш?'],
    ['зві́дки', 'where from', 'Зві́дки ви?'],
    ['коли́', 'when', 'Коли́ ти бу́деш удо́ма?'],
    ['чому́', 'why', 'Чому́ ти не спиш?'],
    ['наві́що', 'what for, why (for what purpose)', 'Наві́що тобі́ це?'],
    ['як', 'how', 'Як тебе́ звуть?'],
    ['скі́льки', 'how much, how many', 'Скі́льки це коштує?'],
    ['яки́й', 'which, what kind of', 'Яки́й сього́дні день?'],
    ['чий', 'whose', 'Чий це телефо́н?'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[0, 2]}
/>

The examples mean: *Who is this? What are you doing? Where do you live? Where are you going? Where are you from? When will you be at home? Why aren't you asleep? What do you need this for? What's your name?* (literally "How do they call you?") *How much does this cost? What day is it today? Whose phone is this?* The examples with <Uk>ти</Uk>, <Uk>тебе́</Uk> and <Uk>тобі́</Uk> are informal; <Uk>Зві́дки ви?</Uk> is formal or plural.

After the question word, the subject can come before or after the verb: <Uk>Де ти живе́ш?</Uk> and <Uk>Де живе́ твій брат?</Uk> are both normal.

## Three words for "where"

English uses *where* for both place and direction. Ukrainian has three separate words.

- <Uk>Де</Uk> asks about a place: where something is, or where something happens.
- <Uk>Куди́</Uk> asks about a direction: where someone or something is going to. Use it with verbs of motion such as <Uk>іти́</Uk> (to go, on foot) and <Uk>ї́хати</Uk> (to go, by transport).
- <Uk>Зві́дки</Uk> asks where someone or something comes from.

<ExampleList title="Де, куди́ and зві́дки">
  <Example uk="Де ти?" en="Where are you?" note="Informal: ти." />
  <Example uk="Куди́ ви ї́дете?" en="Where are you going?" note="Formal or plural: ви. Going by transport." />
  <Example uk="— Зві́дки ви? — Я з Кана́ди." en="“Where are you from?” “I'm from Canada.”" note="Formal: ви, to one person." />
</ExampleList>

The answers follow the same split: a place after <Uk>де</Uk>, a direction after <Uk>куди́</Uk>. [Common prepositions](/grammar/prepositions/) shows how the noun's case changes to match.

## Who and what change their form

<Uk>Хто</Uk> and <Uk>що</Uk> are pronouns, and they have cases like nouns. The form depends on the job of the word in the question, and on any preposition in front of it.

<GrammarTable
  caption="хто (who) and що (what) in four cases"
  headers={['Case', 'хто', 'що', 'Example']}
  rows={[
    ['Nominative (subject)', 'хто', 'що', 'Хто там? (Who is there?)'],
    ['Accusative (object)', 'кого́', 'що', 'Кого́ ти ба́чиш? (Who do you see?)'],
    ['Dative (to whom)', 'кому́', 'чому́', 'Кому́ ти пи́шеш? (Who are you writing to?)'],
    ['Instrumental (with)', 'ким', 'чим', 'З ким ти живе́ш? (Who do you live with?)'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[1, 2, 3]}
/>

The examples with <Uk>ти</Uk> are informal. A few things to notice:

- In questions about a person who is the object, English often says *who*, but Ukrainian needs <Uk>кого́</Uk>.
- The preposition stays in front of the question word: <Uk>З ким?</Uk> (with whom?), <Uk>Про що?</Uk> (about what?). <Uk>Про що ця кни́га?</Uk> means "What is this book about?"
- <Uk>Чому́</Uk> is the dative of <Uk>що</Uk> and also the everyday word for *why*.

<Uk>Хто</Uk> and <Uk>що</Uk> also have genitive forms, <Uk>кого́</Uk> and <Uk>чого́</Uk>. You need them after prepositions that take the genitive, such as <Uk>до</Uk> and <Uk>для</Uk>: <Uk>До кого́?</Uk> (To whom?), <Uk>Для чого́?</Uk> (What for?). The genitive <Uk>кого́</Uk> looks the same as the accusative. They also have locative forms, which you will meet later.

## Which and whose agree with the noun

<Uk>Яки́й</Uk> (which, what kind of) and <Uk>чий</Uk> (whose) work like adjectives. They change to match the gender and number of the noun they ask about. [Adjectives and agreement](/grammar/adjectives-and-agreement/) explains this matching.

## Common mistakes and limits

<Note type="mistake">
  **Using <Uk>де</Uk> for a direction.** With *go*, *come* and other verbs of motion, ask <Uk>куди́</Uk>, not <Uk>де</Uk>: <Uk>Куди́ ти йдеш?</Uk> (Where are you going?).
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Leaving <Uk>хто</Uk> unchanged.** When the person you ask about is the object, use <Uk>кого́</Uk>: <Uk>Кого́ ти ба́чиш?</Uk> After a preposition, use the form that preposition needs: <Uk>з ким</Uk>, not *<Uk>з хто</Uk>*.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Forgetting agreement.** <Uk>Яки́й</Uk> and <Uk>чий</Uk> match the noun: <Uk>чия́ кни́га</Uk>, <Uk>чиє́ фо́то</Uk>, <Uk>чиї́ окуля́ри</Uk>.
</Note>

<Note type="remember">
  This page covers simple direct questions. Polite requests, questions with <Uk>хіба́</Uk> or <Uk>невже́</Uk> (which add surprise or doubt) and longer indirect questions come later.
</Note>
