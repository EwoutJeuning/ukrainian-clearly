After this page you will be able to form the genitive of nouns in the singular and the plural. You will know how masculine nouns choose between <Uk>-а</Uk> and <Uk>-у</Uk>, and how to handle the genitive plural, the hardest form in this group. You will also use the genitive in its main jobs: *of*, quantities, numbers, absence, and after a set of very common prepositions.

The **[genitive](/glossary/#genitive)** is one of the most used cases in Ukrainian. You have already met it after <Uk>нема́є</Uk> on the [Negation](/grammar/negation/) page and after <Uk>без</Uk>, <Uk>до</Uk> and <Uk>з</Uk> on the [prepositions](/grammar/prepositions/) page.

## What the genitive does

The main jobs of the genitive are:

- **Of, and belonging:** <Uk>кни́га сестри́</Uk> (my sister's book), <Uk>ча́шка ка́ви</Uk> (a cup of coffee).
- **Quantity:** after <Uk>бага́то</Uk> (a lot, many), <Uk>ма́ло</Uk> (little, few), <Uk>скі́льки</Uk> (how much, how many), and after numbers from five upwards.
- **Absence:** after <Uk>нема́є</Uk> (there is no).
- **After a negative verb**, often (see below).
- **After many prepositions**, such as <Uk>без</Uk> (without), <Uk>для</Uk> (for) and <Uk>до</Uk> (to).

Dates use the genitive as well [src: ukma-ukrainian-foreign-language]. They will be covered on a later page.

These endings come from the official orthography [src: pravopys-2026, §69, §82, §95]. Some notes:

- **Masculine nouns in <Uk>-а</Uk>** follow the feminine pattern: <Uk>Мико́ла</Uk> → <Uk>Мико́ли</Uk>.
- **Masculine nouns in <Uk>-о</Uk>** take <Uk>-а</Uk>: <Uk>ба́тько</Uk> → <Uk>ба́тька</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §82].
- **Neuter nouns** always take <Uk>-а</Uk> or <Uk>-я</Uk>, never <Uk>-у</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §82].
- **The stem can change**, as in the plural: <Uk>і</Uk> becomes <Uk>о</Uk> or <Uk>е</Uk> (<Uk>ніч</Uk> → <Uk>но́чі</Uk>, <Uk>стіл</Uk> → <Uk>стола́</Uk>), and a vowel can drop out (<Uk>день</Uk> → <Uk>дня</Uk>, <Uk>цу́кор</Uk> → <Uk>цу́кру</Uk>) [src: pravopys-2026, §9].
- **Stress can tell forms apart.** The genitive singular <Uk>сестри́</Uk> and the nominative plural <Uk>се́стри</Uk> are spelled the same.
- **Nouns like <Uk>ім’я́</Uk> and <Uk>кошеня́</Uk>** add <Uk>-ен-</Uk> or <Uk>-ят-</Uk>: <Uk>і́мені</Uk> (also <Uk>ім’я́</Uk>), <Uk>кошеня́ти</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §98].

## Masculine nouns: -а or -у?

This is the one real puzzle of the genitive singular. Masculine nouns that end in a consonant take either <Uk>-а</Uk> (<Uk>-я</Uk> after a soft consonant) or <Uk>-у</Uk> (<Uk>-ю</Uk>). The orthography says the choice depends on the meaning of the noun, and gives long lists of groups [src: pravopys-2026, §82]. The groups below are tendencies, not rules you can apply without checking. Each noun has to be learned with its own genitive.

The groups have exceptions, and the same noun can take both endings with different meanings [src: pravopys-2026, §82]:

- <Uk>Хліб</Uk> takes <Uk>-а</Uk> (<Uk>хлі́ба</Uk>), although many other foods take <Uk>-у</Uk>.
- <Uk>День</Uk> takes <Uk>-а</Uk> (<Uk>дня</Uk>), but <Uk>рік</Uk> (year) takes <Uk>-у</Uk> (<Uk>ро́ку</Uk>).
- <Uk>Лист</Uk> means both *letter* and *leaf*. A letter, or a single leaf, is <Uk>листа́</Uk>. Leaves as a mass are <Uk>ли́сту</Uk>.
- Some towns have both endings: <Uk>Ло́ндона</Uk> and <Uk>Ло́ндону</Uk>.

<Note type="remember">
  When you learn a masculine noun, learn its genitive with it: <Uk>брат — бра́та</Uk>, <Uk>час — ча́су</Uk>, <Uk>стіл — стола́</Uk>. Dictionaries give the genitive, and the paradigm pages on <Uk>Горох</Uk> show every form with its stress.
</Note>

## The genitive plural

The genitive plural has three possible endings: **no ending at all**, **<Uk>-ів</Uk>** and **<Uk>-ей</Uk>** [src: pravopys-2026, §76, §89, §96, §100]. Very roughly:

- **Feminine nouns in <Uk>-а</Uk>, <Uk>-я</Uk> and most neuter nouns** lose their ending and keep the bare stem.
- **Masculine nouns** take <Uk>-ів</Uk> (<Uk>-їв</Uk> after a vowel).
- **Feminine nouns ending in a consonant**, and a few others, take <Uk>-ей</Uk>.

### No ending

    ['мі́сто, я́блуко', 'міст, я́блук', 'the ending -о is dropped'],

When the ending goes, the last syllable of the stem often changes. Two changes are common.

**A vowel appears between two final consonants.** If the stem ends in two consonants, <Uk>о</Uk> or <Uk>е</Uk> usually appears between them [src: pravopys-2026, §76, note 1; §89, note]:

    ['ку́хня', 'ку́хонь'],

  ]}

The genitive plural of <Uk>сестра́</Uk> has two accepted stresses, so it is shown without a stress mark.

**<Uk>о</Uk> or <Uk>е</Uk> becomes <Uk>і</Uk>.** In the now closed last syllable, <Uk>о</Uk> and <Uk>е</Uk> often change to <Uk>і</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §9]: <Uk>шко́ла</Uk> → <Uk>шкіл</Uk>, <Uk>робо́та</Uk> → <Uk>робі́т</Uk>, <Uk>доро́га</Uk> → <Uk>дорі́г</Uk>, <Uk>сло́во</Uk> → <Uk>слів</Uk>, <Uk>село́</Uk> → <Uk>сіл</Uk>.

Most masculine nouns take <Uk>-ів</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §89]. <Uk>Мо́ре</Uk> and <Uk>по́ле</Uk> are among the few neuter nouns that take it too. The plural-only nouns <Uk>окуля́ри</Uk> and <Uk>штани́</Uk> also take it: <Uk>окуля́рів</Uk>, <Uk>штані́в</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §100].

All feminine nouns ending in a consonant, like <Uk>ніч</Uk>, take <Uk>-ей</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §96]. For the others it is a feature of the individual word.

Nouns like <Uk>кошеня́</Uk> and <Uk>ім’я́</Uk> have no ending: <Uk>кошеня́т</Uk>, <Uk>іме́н</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §99].

## Uses of the genitive

### Of, and belonging

The genitive does the work of English *of* and *'s*. The noun in the genitive comes **after** the noun it describes.

<ExampleList title="Of and belonging">
  <Example uk="Це маши́на бра́та." en="This is my brother's car." note="Literally: the car of my brother." />
  <Example uk="Ми живемо́ в це́нтрі мі́ста." en="We live in the city centre." note="Literally: in the centre of the city." />
  <Example uk="Ча́шку ка́ви, будь ла́ска." en="A cup of coffee, please." note="ча́шку: accusative (I'd like…). ка́ви: genitive." />
  <Example uk="Да́йте, будь ла́ска, кілогра́м цу́кру." en="A kilo of sugar, please." note="Formal or plural: ви. Literally: give me a kilo of sugar." />
  <Example uk="Це кімна́та сестри́." en="This is my sister's room." />
</ExampleList>

### Quantity: багато, мало, скільки

After words of quantity, a noun you can count goes into the genitive plural. A noun for a substance or an idea, which you can't count, goes into the genitive singular.

<ExampleList title="Words of quantity">
  <Example uk="У Льво́ві бага́то музе́їв." en="There are a lot of museums in Lviv." note="Countable: genitive plural." />
  <Example uk="У ме́не ма́ло ча́су." en="I have little time." note="Not countable: genitive singular." />
  <Example uk="Скі́льки у вас діте́й?" en="How many children do you have?" note="Formal or plural: ви." />
  <Example uk="Тут є кі́лька кафе́." en="There are a few cafés here." note="кафе́ never changes, but it is still in the genitive plural." />
</ExampleList>

### After numbers

Numbers affect the case of the noun after them [src: ukma-ukrainian-foreign-language]:

- After **1**, the noun stays in the nominative singular: <Uk>оди́н до́лар</Uk>, <Uk>одна́ гри́вня</Uk>.
- After **2, 3 and 4**, the noun is in the nominative plural: <Uk>два до́лари</Uk>, <Uk>дві гри́вні</Uk>.
- After **5 and above**, the noun is in the **genitive plural**: <Uk>п’ять до́ларів</Uk>, <Uk>п’ять гри́вень</Uk>.

With a longer number, the last word decides: <Uk>со́рок три гри́вні</Uk> (43 hryvnias), but <Uk>п’ятна́дцять гри́вень</Uk> (15 hryvnias).

<ExampleList title="Numbers and the genitive plural">
  <Example uk="Це коштує сто гри́вень." en="It costs a hundred hryvnias." note="коштує is shown without a stress mark: dictionaries accept two stresses." />
  <Example uk="Я чека́ю вже де́сять хвили́н." en="I've been waiting for ten minutes already." />
  <Example uk="У гру́пі шість студе́нтів." en="There are six students in the group." />
</ExampleList>

### Absence: немає

To say that something or someone is not there, use <Uk>нема́є</Uk> with the genitive. [Negation](/grammar/negation/) introduced this pattern.

<ExampleList title="Нема́є with the genitive">
  <Example uk="Сього́дні нема́є дощу́." en="There's no rain today." />
  <Example uk="У на́шому мі́сті нема́є теа́тру." en="There's no theatre in our town." />
  <Example uk="Бра́та нема́є вдо́ма." en="My brother isn't at home." />
  <Example uk="У ме́не нема́є грошей." en="I haven't got any money." note="гро́ші is plural only, so its genitive is plural too. Dictionaries give two stresses for this form." />
</ExampleList>

### After a negative verb

After a negative verb, the object is often in the genitive instead of the accusative [src: ukma-ukrainian-foreign-language]. Both cases are used, so you will hear both. Treat this as a tendency.

<ExampleList title="A negative verb with the genitive">
  <Example uk="Я не ма́ю ча́су." en="I don't have time." note="ча́су: genitive. Very common with ма́ти." />
  <Example uk="Він не п’є ка́ви." en="He doesn't drink coffee." note="ка́ви: genitive. Він не п’є ка́ву is also used." />
</ExampleList>

### Prepositions with the genitive

Many common prepositions take the genitive [src: goroh-dictionary]. [Common prepositions](/grammar/prepositions/) introduced most of them.

<GrammarTable
  caption="Prepositions with the genitive"
  headers={['Preposition', 'Meaning', 'Example']}
  rows={[
    ['без', 'without', 'ка́ва без цу́кру'],
    ['для', 'for', 'подару́нок для ма́ми'],
    ['до', 'to, as far as; until', 'до лі́каря; до ве́чора'],
    ['з, із, зі', 'from, out of', 'з Ки́єва; зі шко́ли'],
    ['від', 'from (a person or starting point)', 'лист від бра́та'],
    ['бі́ля', 'near, next to', 'бі́ля вокза́лу'],
    ['пі́сля', 'after', 'пі́сля уро́ку'],
    ['у, в', 'at someone’s (place); belonging to someone', 'у бра́та'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[0, 2]}
/>

The examples mean: *coffee without sugar; a present for Mum; to the doctor, until the evening; from Kyiv, from school; a letter from my brother; near the station; after the lesson; at my brother's.*

<ExampleList title="Prepositions with the genitive in sentences">
  <Example uk="Я йду до лі́каря." en="I'm going to the doctor's." />
  <Example uk="Ми живемо́ бі́ля університе́ту." en="We live near the university." />
  <Example uk="Пі́сля уро́ку ми п’ємо́ ка́ву." en="After the lesson we have a coffee." />
  <Example uk="Вона́ прийшла́ зі шко́ли." en="She has come from school." note="зі before шк-." />
  <Example uk="Він поверну́вся із села́." en="He came back from the village." note="із before с." />
</ExampleList>

### У + genitive: at someone's, and "have"

With a person in the genitive, <Uk>у</Uk> (<Uk>в</Uk>) means *at someone's place* or *with someone* [src: goroh-dictionary]. The same pattern gives the usual way to say *have*, which you know from <Uk>у ме́не є</Uk> (I have).

<ExampleList title="У with a person in the genitive">
  <Example uk="Я був у лі́каря." en="I was at the doctor's." note="Said by a man." />
  <Example uk="Ми святкува́ли в бра́та." en="We celebrated at my brother's." />
  <Example uk="У сестри́ є соба́ка." en="My sister has a dog." note="Literally: by my sister there is a dog." />
</ExampleList>

The forms <Uk>у ме́не</Uk>, <Uk>у нас</Uk> and <Uk>у вас</Uk> use pronouns. [Pronoun cases](/grammar/pronoun-cases/) shows the genitive of every pronoun.

## Common mistakes and limits

<Note type="mistake">
  **The nominative after a number from five up.** Say <Uk>п’ять до́ларів</Uk>, not *<Uk>п’ять до́лари</Uk>*. The nominative plural is only for 2, 3 and 4.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Guessing <Uk>-а</Uk> for every masculine noun.** Say <Uk>бага́то ча́су</Uk>, not *<Uk>бага́то ча́са</Uk>*. When you are not sure, check the dictionary.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Adding <Uk>-ів</Uk> to feminine nouns.** <Uk>Кни́га</Uk> has the genitive plural <Uk>книг</Uk>, not *<Uk>кни́гів</Uk>*. Feminine nouns in <Uk>-а</Uk> normally just lose their ending.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Forgetting the inserted vowel.** Say <Uk>бага́то пісе́нь</Uk>, not *<Uk>бага́то пісн</Uk>*. A vowel appears between the last two consonants.
</Note>

<Note type="remember">
  For every noun, learn three forms: the nominative singular, the genitive singular and the genitive plural, for example <Uk>кни́га — кни́ги — книг</Uk>, <Uk>брат — бра́та — браті́в</Uk>. With these three you can work out most of the other forms.
</Note>

<Note>
  This page covers nouns. Adjectives and pronouns have genitive forms too: see [Adjective cases](/grammar/adjective-cases/) and [Pronoun cases](/grammar/pronoun-cases/). Some nouns have two accepted genitive plurals, such as <Uk>мам</Uk> and <Uk>мамі́в</Uk> from <Uk>ма́ма</Uk>; the dictionary lists both.
</Note>
