After this page you will be able to choose between the two Ukrainian verbs for "go on foot", "go by transport", "run", "fly", "swim", "carry" and "take by vehicle". You will know when to say <Uk>іти́</Uk> and when <Uk>йти</Uk>, how to say "I went there and came back", and how prefixes such as <Uk>при-</Uk>, <Uk>ви-</Uk> and <Uk>у-</Uk> make verbs like *arrive*, *go out* and *go in*. You will also know which preposition and case to use for "to", "into" and "out of".

## Two verbs for one English verb

English has one verb *go*. Ukrainian has two for going on foot, two for going by transport, and so on. The two verbs in each pair are both imperfective, and they share an English translation.

- The first verb is used for movement **in one direction**, usually happening now or at a particular time: "I'm going to work".
- The second verb is used for movement **in different directions or many times**, and for round trips and abilities: "I go to work every day", "children run around", "I can swim".

The dictionary defines the second verb of each pair in these terms. <Uk>Ї́здити</Uk> is "to travel many times or in different directions", and <Uk>бі́гати</Uk>, <Uk>носи́ти</Uk> and <Uk>вози́ти</Uk> are "the same as" <Uk>бі́гти</Uk>, <Uk>нести́</Uk> and <Uk>везти́</Uk>, but repeated or in different directions [src: goroh-dictionary].

Notice the consonant changes in the *I* forms: <Uk>ходжу́</Uk>, <Uk>ї́жджу</Uk>, <Uk>лечу́</Uk>, <Uk>ношу́</Uk>, <Uk>вожу́</Uk>. The [present tense](/grammar/present-tense/) page shows the same change in <Uk>ходжу́</Uk>. <Uk>Пливти́</Uk> and <Uk>плисти́</Uk> are two spellings of one verb with the same present tense [src: goroh-dictionary].

## Іти or йти?

<Uk>Іти́</Uk> and <Uk>йти</Uk> are the same verb, and the dictionary lists both [src: goroh-dictionary]. The choice follows the same sound rule as <Uk>і</Uk> and <Uk>й</Uk> (and): <Uk>й</Uk> after a vowel, <Uk>і</Uk> after a consonant. The orthography's own example is <Uk>Вона́ йде</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §24].

So: <Uk>Він іде́</Uk>, but <Uk>Вона́ йде</Uk> and <Uk>Я йду</Uk>. The *you* (<Uk>ти</Uk>) form after <Uk>ти</Uk> is <Uk>Ти йдеш</Uk>. The same choice applies in the past tense: <Uk>ішо́в</Uk>, <Uk>ішла́</Uk>, <Uk>ішли́</Uk> or <Uk>йшов</Uk>, <Uk>йшла</Uk>, <Uk>йшли</Uk> [src: goroh-dictionary]. The short forms of one syllable, like <Uk>йду</Uk>, have no stress mark.

### Ability

The second verb also says what a person or an animal *can* do. The dictionary gives "to be able to swim" as a meaning of <Uk>пла́вати</Uk>, and "to have the ability to fly" for <Uk>літа́ти</Uk> [src: goroh-dictionary].

<ExampleList title="Being able to">
  <Example uk="Моя́ сестра́ до́бре пла́ває." en="My sister swims well." />
  <Example uk="Пінгві́ни не літа́ють." en="Penguins can't fly." />
</ExampleList>

### Носити: also "to wear"

<Uk>Носи́ти</Uk> also means "to wear" or "to have on you" [src: goroh-dictionary]: <Uk>Він но́сить окуля́ри</Uk> (He wears glasses). <Uk>Нести́</Uk> never has this meaning.

## In the past: went, and went there and back

In the past tense the difference matters. <Uk>Ходи́в</Uk> or <Uk>ї́здив</Uk> usually reports a trip that is over: you went and came back. The dictionary gives this sense of <Uk>ходи́ти</Uk> as "to set off somewhere with a purpose" [src: goroh-dictionary]. <Uk>Ішо́в</Uk> or <Uk>ї́хав</Uk> describes the journey itself, while it was happening.

<ExampleList title="The past tense">
  <Example uk="Учо́ра я ходи́в у кіно́." en="I went to the cinema yesterday." note="Said by a man. The trip is over: he went and came back." />
  <Example uk="Ми ї́здили до Оде́си." en="We went to Odesa." note="And came back." />
  <Example uk="Я йшла додо́му й ду́мала про робо́ту." en="I was walking home, thinking about work." note="Said by a woman. The journey, while it was happening. й between a vowel and a consonant." />
</ExampleList>

## Prefixes: arrive, leave, go in, go out

A prefix on <Uk>іти́</Uk> makes a **perfective** verb with a more exact meaning: <Uk>прийти́</Uk> (to arrive, to come). In most of them the root is spelled <Uk>-йти</Uk> (<Uk>прийти́</Uk>, <Uk>ви́йти</Uk>); <Uk>піти́</Uk> is an exception. The imperfective partner is made from <Uk>ходи́ти</Uk>: <Uk>прихо́дити</Uk>. See [aspect](/glossary/#aspect) in the glossary.

    ['в-, у- go in, enter', 'увійти́, ввійти́', 'вхо́дити', 'в, у + accusative; до + genitive'],
    ['ді- reach, get as far as', 'дійти́', 'дохо́дити', 'до + genitive'],
    ['пере- cross', 'перейти́', 'перехо́дити', 'accusative; че́рез + accusative'],
    ['від- move away', 'відійти́', 'відхо́дити', 'від + genitive'],
    ['за- drop in, call in', 'зайти́', 'захо́дити', 'до + genitive; в, на + accusative'],
    ['під- come up to, approach', 'підійти́', 'підхо́дити', 'до + genitive'],
    ['по- set off, leave', 'піти́', '—', 'до + genitive; в, на + accusative'],
  ]}
  ukColumns={[1, 2]}
/>

All the verbs in the table are in the dictionary [src: goroh-dictionary]. A few points:

- **The stress of <Uk>ви́йти</Uk>** is on the prefix, in every form: <Uk>ви́йду</Uk>, <Uk>ви́йдеш</Uk>, <Uk>ви́йшов</Uk>.
- **<Uk>Увійти́</Uk> or <Uk>ввійти́</Uk>** follows the <Uk>у</Uk> / <Uk>в</Uk> rule: <Uk>в-</Uk> after a vowel, <Uk>у-</Uk> after a consonant. The orthography's own example is <Uk>Го́сті ввійшли́ до за́ли</Uk> (The guests came into the hall) [src: pravopys-2026, §23].
- **<Uk>Піти́</Uk>** is the usual perfective of <Uk>іти́</Uk>: "to set off, to go" [src: goroh-dictionary]. <Uk>Він пішо́в</Uk> means "he has left, he has gone".
- **<Uk>Захо́дити</Uk>** has the stress on <Uk>-хо́-</Uk>. With the stress on <Uk>-ди́-</Uk>, <Uk>заходи́ти</Uk> is a different verb: "to start walking up and down" [src: goroh-dictionary].

Perfective verbs have no present tense. Their present-looking forms refer to the future: <Uk>Я прийду́ о шо́стій</Uk> means "I will come at six". The [future tense](/grammar/future-tense/) page explains this, and [Aspect](/grammar/aspect/) explains the pairs.

The stress moves: <Uk>прийду́</Uk>, but <Uk>при́йдеш</Uk>, <Uk>при́йде</Uk>. <Uk>Перейти́</Uk>, <Uk>увійти́</Uk> and <Uk>дійти́</Uk> work the same way: <Uk>перейду́</Uk>, <Uk>пере́йдеш</Uk>; <Uk>увійду́</Uk>, <Uk>уві́йдеш</Uk>; <Uk>дійду́</Uk>, <Uk>ді́йдеш</Uk> [src: goroh-dictionary].

  <Example uk="Він прийшо́в додо́му пі́зно." en="He came home late." />
  <Example uk="Вона́ ви́йшла з кімна́ти." en="She went out of the room." note="з + genitive: out of." />
  <Example uk="Учи́тель увійшо́в до кла́су." en="The teacher came into the classroom." note="увійшо́в after a consonant." />
  <Example uk="Як дійти́ до вокза́лу?" en="How do I get to the station on foot?" note="до + genitive." />
  <Example uk="Ми перейшли́ ву́лицю." en="We crossed the street." note="ву́лицю: accusative, no preposition." />
  <Example uk="Я зайду́ до те́бе за́втра." en="I'll drop in on you tomorrow." note="Informal: ти." />
  <Example uk="Ми прихо́димо на робо́ту о дев’я́тій." en="We get to work at nine." note="Every day, so imperfective." />
</ExampleList>

### The same prefixes with їхати

The prefixes work with <Uk>ї́хати</Uk> too. The imperfective partners end in <Uk>-їжджа́ти</Uk>: <Uk>приї́хати</Uk> / <Uk>приїжджа́ти</Uk> (to arrive by transport), <Uk>ви́їхати</Uk> / <Uk>виїжджа́ти</Uk> (to drive out, to leave). The perfective of <Uk>ї́хати</Uk> itself is <Uk>пої́хати</Uk> (to set off, to go) [src: goroh-dictionary].

<ExampleList title="By transport">
  <Example uk="Ма́ма приї́хала зі Льво́ва." en="Mum has arrived from Lviv." note="зі before льв-." />
  <Example uk="За́втра ми пої́демо до мо́ря." en="Tomorrow we're going to the seaside." note="Future: пої́демо is perfective." />
</ExampleList>

## Which preposition: to, into, out of

Direction "to" usually takes <Uk>до</Uk> with the [genitive](/grammar/genitive/), or <Uk>в</Uk>/<Uk>у</Uk> or <Uk>на</Uk> with the [accusative](/grammar/accusative/). Words that take <Uk>на</Uk> for "at" take <Uk>на</Uk> for "to" as well: <Uk>на робо́ті</Uk> (at work), <Uk>на робо́ту</Uk> (to work). The [prepositions](/grammar/prepositions/) page and [Directions](/sentences/directions/) explain this difference between a place and a direction.

## Common mistakes and limits

<Note type="mistake">
  **Using <Uk>ходи́ти</Uk> for one trip happening now.** "I'm going to the shop (now)" is <Uk>Я йду до магази́ну</Uk>. <Uk>Я ходжу́ до магази́ну</Uk> means you go there regularly.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Walking when you are driving.** <Uk>Іти́</Uk> means moving on foot, by taking steps [src: goroh-dictionary]. For a person travelling by car, train or bus, use <Uk>ї́хати</Uk>: <Uk>Я ї́ду до Ки́єва по́тягом.</Uk>
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **The locative after a verb of motion.** A direction takes the accusative or <Uk>до</Uk> + genitive: <Uk>Я йду на робо́ту</Uk>, not *<Uk>Я йду на робо́ті</Uk>*. The locative (<Uk>на робо́ті</Uk>) says where you are.
</Note>

<Note type="remember">
  **<Uk>Ходи́в</Uk> often means there and back.** <Uk>Я ходи́в у кіно́</Uk> (said by a man) reports a finished outing. To describe the walk while it was happening, use <Uk>ішо́в</Uk> or <Uk>йшов</Uk>.
</Note>

<Note>
  This page covers the most common pairs and prefixes. There are more prefixes, such as <Uk>з-</Uk> and <Uk>об-</Uk>, and some prefixed verbs have more than one meaning. Check each new prefixed verb in a dictionary.
</Note>
