After this page you will be able to recognise the main Ukrainian participles when you read them: verb forms that work like adjectives, such as <Uk>прочи́таний</Uk> (read, as in *a read book*), and verb forms that work like adverbs, such as <Uk>чита́ючи</Uk> (while reading) and <Uk>прочита́вши</Uk> (having read). You will know which of them are common and which ones usage guides advise against.

**Beginners mostly need to recognise these forms, not produce them.** You will meet them in written texts, news, instructions and on signs. In everyday speech, Ukrainians often use a clause with <Uk>яки́й</Uk> (which, who) or <Uk>коли́</Uk> (when) instead.

## Two kinds of form

Ukrainian grammar distinguishes two families [src: pravopys-2026, §119, §120]:

- **Participles** (<Uk>дієприкме́тники</Uk>) work like adjectives. They describe a noun and change their endings to agree with it: <Uk>прочи́тана кни́га</Uk> (a book that has been read).
- **Adverbial participles** (<Uk>дієприслі́вники</Uk>) work like adverbs. They never change, and they describe a second action of the subject: <Uk>Чита́ючи, він слу́хає му́зику</Uk> (While reading, he listens to music).

## Passive participles: -ний and -тий

These are the participles you will meet most often. They describe something that an action was done to, like English *done*, *written*, *opened*. Most end in <Uk>-ний</Uk> (<Uk>-аний</Uk>, <Uk>-яний</Uk>, <Uk>-ений</Uk>); a smaller group ends in <Uk>-тий</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §119].

### They agree like adjectives

A passive participle takes the same endings as a hard adjective such as <Uk>нови́й</Uk>. It **[agrees](/glossary/#agreement)** with its noun in gender, number and case.

### The form in -но and -то

Change the ending of a passive participle to <Uk>-о</Uk> and you get the unchanging form used in sentences without a subject: <Uk>зачи́нений</Uk> → <Uk>зачи́нено</Uk>. This is the form on shop doors: <Uk>Зачи́нено</Uk> (Closed), <Uk>Відчи́нено</Uk> (Open). [Impersonal sentences](/grammar/impersonal-sentences/) explains how it is used.

## Active participles: -учий, -ючий, -ачий, -ячий

Active participles describe someone or something doing the action, like English *-ing* before a noun. In the present they end in <Uk>-чий</Uk>: <Uk>-учий</Uk>, <Uk>-ючий</Uk>, <Uk>-ачий</Uk> or <Uk>-ячий</Uk>. The orthography notes that they are used mainly as adjectives and nouns, and gives <Uk>живу́чий</Uk> (hardy, tenacious), <Uk>квіту́чий</Uk> (blooming), <Uk>лежа́чий</Uk> (lying) and <Uk>стоя́чий</Uk> (standing) [src: pravopys-2026, §119].

Usage guides advise against many active participles in <Uk>-учий</Uk> and <Uk>-ючий</Uk>, especially those copied from Russian, and suggest a noun, an adjective or a clause with <Uk>яки́й</Uk> [src: goroh-usage].

The guides quoted by the dictionary give examples such as <Uk>На пля́жі бага́то відпочива́льників</Uk> (There are many holidaymakers on the beach) and <Uk>чи́нне законода́вство</Uk> (current legislation) [src: goroh-usage]. Note that <Uk>заві́дувач</Uk> takes the genitive: <Uk>заві́дувач ка́федри</Uk> (head of the department).

Some active participles are fine and common, because they have become ordinary adjectives, like <Uk>квіту́чий</Uk> above.

There is also a small group of past active participles in <Uk>-лий</Uk>, made from verbs that describe a change of state: <Uk>почорні́лий</Uk> (blackened), <Uk>осі́лий</Uk> (settled) [src: pravopys-2026, §119; goroh-dictionary]. You will meet them mostly in literature.

## Adverbial participles

An adverbial participle describes a second action done by the same person as the main verb. It never changes its ending.

### While doing: -учи, -ючи, -ачи, -ячи

These are made from imperfective verbs. First-group verbs take <Uk>-учи</Uk> or <Uk>-ючи</Uk>, and second-group verbs take <Uk>-ачи</Uk> or <Uk>-ячи</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §120]. The easiest way to find the form is to start from the *they* form of the present tense.

### Having done: -ши, -вши

These are made from perfective verbs. Take the masculine past tense and add <Uk>-вши</Uk> after a vowel, or <Uk>-ши</Uk> after a consonant [src: pravopys-2026, §120]: <Uk>прочита́в</Uk> → <Uk>прочита́вши</Uk>, <Uk>зроби́в</Uk> → <Uk>зроби́вши</Uk>, <Uk>прині́с</Uk> → <Uk>прині́сши</Uk>.

From verbs in <Uk>-ся</Uk>, the adverbial participle ends in <Uk>-сь</Uk>, less often <Uk>-ся</Uk>: <Uk>сміючи́сь</Uk> (laughing), <Uk>узя́вшись</Uk> (having taken up) [src: pravopys-2026, §120].

<ExampleList title="Adverbial participles in use">
  <Example uk="Чита́ючи, він слу́хає му́зику." en="He listens to music while reading." />
  <Example uk="Ідучи́ додо́му, я зустрі́в дру́га." en="On my way home, I met a friend." note="A man speaking: зустрі́в." />
  <Example uk="Прочита́вши лист, вона́ відповіла́." en="Having read the letter, she replied." />
  <Example uk="Зроби́вши все, ми пішли́ додо́му." en="Having done everything, we went home." />
  <Example uk="Він працю́є не поспіша́ючи." en="He works without hurrying." />

Two spelling rules apply. A phrase with an adverbial participle is usually set off by commas, as in the first four examples [src: pravopys-2026, §158]. And <Uk>не</Uk> is written separately: <Uk>не поспіша́ючи</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §44].

## Where you will meet them

- **Signs and notices:** <Uk>Зачи́нено</Uk>, <Uk>Відчи́нено</Uk>, <Uk>Вхід заборо́нено</Uk> (No entry). These are the forms in <Uk>-но</Uk>.
- **Descriptions:** <Uk>відчи́нене вікно́</Uk>, <Uk>ку́плений квито́к</Uk> (a bought ticket), <Uk>зро́блена робо́та</Uk> (finished work).
- **Written narrative and news:** adverbial participles like <Uk>прочита́вши</Uk> and <Uk>ка́жучи</Uk> are much more common in writing than in conversation.

## Common mistakes and limits

<Note type="mistake">
  **Translating every English *-ing* with a participle in <Uk>-учий</Uk> or <Uk>-ючий</Uk>.** *The people resting on the beach* is better as <Uk>відпочива́льники</Uk> or <Uk>лю́ди, які́ відпочива́ють</Uk>. Usage guides recommend these over <Uk>відпочива́ючі</Uk> [src: goroh-usage].
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Changing the ending of an adverbial participle.** <Uk>Чита́ючи</Uk> and <Uk>прочита́вши</Uk> never agree with anything. Only participles like <Uk>прочи́таний</Uk> change for gender and number.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **Using an adverbial participle for a different person.** The adverbial participle describes the subject of the main verb. In <Uk>Ідучи́ додо́му, я зустрі́в дру́га</Uk>, it is the speaker who is walking home, not the friend.
</Note>

<Note type="remember">
  Participles and adverbial participles belong mainly to written Ukrainian. In speech, a clause usually does the same job: <Uk>Коли́ я йшов додо́му, я зустрі́в дру́га</Uk> (When I was walking home, I met a friend; a man speaking). Learn to recognise them first, and use them yourself later.
</Note>
