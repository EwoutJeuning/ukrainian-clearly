After this page you will be able to say *this book*, *that house*, *such a nice day*, *the whole family* and *I'll do it myself* in any case. You will know the full forms of <Uk>цей</Uk> (this), <Uk>той</Uk> (that), <Uk>таки́й</Uk> (such), <Uk>уве́сь</Uk> or <Uk>весь</Uk> (all, the whole) and <Uk>сам</Uk> (oneself), the difference between <Uk>це</Uk> and <Uk>цей</Uk>, and when to say <Uk>той са́мий</Uk> (the same).

All of these words **agree with their noun** in gender, number and case, just like adjectives. [Adjectives in all cases](/grammar/adjective-cases/) explains the endings they share.

## цей and той: this and that

<Uk>Цей</Uk> points to something near the speaker, and <Uk>той</Uk> to something further away. The dictionary defines <Uk>цей</Uk> as the opposite of <Uk>той</Uk> [src: goroh-dictionary].

<ExampleList title="Agreement with the noun">
  <Example uk="Цей буди́нок стари́й." en="This building is old." note="буди́нок is masculine." />
  <Example uk="Ця кни́га ціка́ва." en="This book is interesting." note="кни́га is feminine." />
  <Example uk="Те мі́сто дале́ко." en="That town is far away." note="мі́сто is neuter." />
  <Example uk="Ті лю́ди — мої́ сусі́ди." en="Those people are my neighbours." note="Plural." />
</ExampleList>

### це: "this is", "these are"

<Uk>Це</Uk> is also used on its own to point at something and name it: *this is…*, *these are…*, *it is…*. Used like this, it does **not** change, whatever the gender or number of the noun that follows. Compare it with <Uk>цей</Uk>, <Uk>ця</Uk> and <Uk>ці</Uk>, which stand directly before a noun and agree with it.

<ExampleList title="це or цей?">
  <Example uk="Це мій брат." en="This is my brother." note="це: “this is”. No agreement." />
  <Example uk="Це моя́ сестра́." en="This is my sister." note="Still це." />
  <Example uk="Це мої́ батьки́." en="These are my parents." note="Still це." />
  <Example uk="Ця кни́га моя́." en="This book is mine." note="ця agrees with кни́га." />
</ExampleList>

## цей in all cases

The forms below follow the spelling standard [src: pravopys-2026, §111] and the dictionary [src: goroh-dictionary]. As with adjectives, the masculine accusative is like the genitive for people and animals and like the nominative for things; the plural accusative works the same way.

**The dative and the locative differ only in stress:** <Uk>цьому́</Uk> (to this) but <Uk>у цьо́му</Uk> (in this). The spelling standard gives <Uk>цьо́му</Uk> as the locative for both masculine and neuter [src: pravopys-2026, §111]. The dictionary gives it for the masculine but <Uk>цьому́</Uk> for the neuter [src: goroh-dictionary], so the neuter locative is left unmarked in the table.

The feminine forms <Uk>то́ї</Uk> and <Uk>то́ю</Uk> are rare; the standard gives <Uk>тіє́ї</Uk> and <Uk>тіє́ю</Uk> first [src: pravopys-2026, §111]. <Uk>Цей</Uk> and <Uk>той</Uk> have the same pattern: <Uk>ціє́ї</Uk> and <Uk>тіє́ї</Uk>, <Uk>цьому́</Uk> and <Uk>тому́</Uk>, <Uk>ци́ми</Uk> and <Uk>ти́ми</Uk>.

### Stress after a preposition

As with <Uk>до ме́не</Uk> on [Personal pronouns in all cases](/grammar/pronoun-cases/), a preposition pulls the stress forward in forms such as <Uk>від то́го</Uk>, <Uk>у цьо́го</Uk>, <Uk>на то́му</Uk>, <Uk>при цьо́му</Uk>, <Uk>з усьо́го</Uk>, <Uk>в усьо́му</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §113, note 2].

<ExampleList title="цей and той in different cases">
  <Example uk="Я зна́ю цього́ лі́каря." en="I know this doctor." note="Accusative of a person: like the genitive." />
  <Example uk="Ми живемо́ в цьо́му буди́нку." en="We live in this building." note="Locative after в." />
  <Example uk="Да́йте, будь ла́ска, ту кни́гу." en="Could you give me that book, please?" note="Formal or plural: да́йте is the ви form of the command. Accusative." />
  <Example uk="У цьо́го студе́нта нема́є ча́су." en="This student has no time." note="After у: цьо́го." />
  <Example uk="Ти зна́єш тих люде́й?" en="Do you know those people?" note="Informal: ти. Accusative plural of people: like the genitive." />
</ExampleList>

## такий: such, so

<Uk>Таки́й</Uk> means *such*, *this kind of*, or, before an adjective, *so* [src: goroh-dictionary]. It takes the endings of hard adjectives such as <Uk>нови́й</Uk>: <Uk>тако́го</Uk>, <Uk>тако́му</Uk>, <Uk>таки́м</Uk>, <Uk>тако́ї</Uk>, <Uk>таки́ми</Uk> [src: goroh-dictionary].

## весь, увесь: all, the whole

<Uk>Уве́сь</Uk> (also <Uk>весь</Uk> and <Uk>ввесь</Uk>) means *all*, *the whole*. In the plural, <Uk>усі́</Uk> or <Uk>всі</Uk> also means *everyone*, and the neuter <Uk>усе́</Uk> or <Uk>все</Uk> means *everything*. Every form has a version with <Uk>у-</Uk> and one with <Uk>в-</Uk>, chosen by the usual <Uk>у</Uk>/<Uk>в</Uk> rule [src: pravopys-2026, §113, note 1; §23].

The genitive and dative masculine and neuter are left unmarked: the spelling standard stresses the ending (<Uk>усього</Uk>, <Uk>усьому</Uk>) when there is no preposition, while the dictionary stresses the stem [src: pravopys-2026, §113; goroh-dictionary]. After a preposition both stress the stem: <Uk>з усьо́го</Uk>, <Uk>в усьо́му</Uk>.

<ExampleList title="уве́сь, усі́, усе́">
  <Example uk="Я працюва́в уве́сь день." en="I worked all day." note="Said by a man. Accusative of time, like the nominative." />
  <Example uk="Уся́ сім’я́ вдо́ма." en="The whole family is at home." />
  <Example uk="Усі́ вже тут." en="Everyone is already here." />
  <Example uk="Дя́кую за все!" en="Thank you for everything!" note="все after a vowel." />
</ExampleList>

## сам: oneself, on one's own

<Uk>Сам</Uk> (feminine <Uk>сама́</Uk>, plural <Uk>самі́</Uk>) stresses who does something or who is meant: *myself*, *yourself*, *himself* and so on. It can also mean *alone, on one's own* [src: goroh-dictionary]. It changes like a hard adjective [src: pravopys-2026, §113, note 1]. The neuter has two forms, <Uk>саме́</Uk> and <Uk>само́</Uk> [src: goroh-dictionary].

<Uk>Сам</Uk> agrees with the person it refers to. A woman says <Uk>я сама́</Uk>, a man <Uk>я сам</Uk>, a group <Uk>ми самі́</Uk>.

  <Example uk="Я сам зроблю́." en="I'll do it myself." note="Said by a man." />
  <Example uk="Я сама́ зроблю́." en="I'll do it myself." note="Said by a woman." />
  <Example uk="Вона́ живе́ сама́." en="She lives alone." />
  <Example uk="Ми самі́ не зна́ємо." en="We don't know ourselves." />
  <Example uk="Ти сам це написа́в?" en="Did you write this yourself?" note="Informal: ти, said to a man." />
</ExampleList>

## сам and самий

The spelling standard lists two more words alongside <Uk>сам</Uk>: <Uk>сами́й</Uk>, stressed on the ending, and <Uk>са́мий</Uk>, stressed on the stem, as in <Uk>той са́мий</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §113, note 1]. The dictionary treats <Uk>сами́й</Uk> as another form of <Uk>сам</Uk> [src: goroh-dictionary]. <Uk>Са́мий</Uk> has its own uses [src: goroh-dictionary]:

- **After <Uk>той</Uk>, <Uk>цей</Uk> or <Uk>таки́й</Uk>, it means *the same*:** <Uk>той са́мий</Uk> (the same, the very one), <Uk>таки́й са́мий</Uk> (the same kind of). Usage guides recommend <Uk>той са́мий</Uk> rather than *<Uk>той же</Uk>* or *<Uk>один і той же</Uk>* [src: goroh-usage].
- **With a preposition and a word for a time or a place, it means *the very*:** <Uk>до са́мого ве́чора</Uk> (right up until the evening).

## Common mistakes and limits

<Note type="mistake">
  **Making <Uk>це</Uk> agree in *this is*.** Say <Uk>Це моя́ сестра́</Uk> and <Uk>Це мої́ батьки́</Uk>, not *<Uk>Ця моя́ сестра́</Uk>* or *<Uk>Ці мої́ батьки́</Uk>* when you mean *this is* or *these are*.
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **<Uk>Самий</Uk> for *the most*.** Ukrainian forms the superlative with <Uk>най-</Uk>: <Uk>найкра́щий</Uk> (the best), not *<Uk>самий кра́щий</Uk>* [src: goroh-usage].
</Note>

<Note type="mistake">
  **<Uk>Той же</Uk> for *the same*.** Usage guides prefer <Uk>той са́мий</Uk>: <Uk>Це був той са́мий хло́пець</Uk> (It was the same boy) [src: goroh-usage].
</Note>

<Note type="remember">
  <Uk>Цей</Uk>, <Uk>той</Uk>, <Uk>таки́й</Uk>, <Uk>уве́сь</Uk> and <Uk>сам</Uk> agree with the noun or person like adjectives. Only <Uk>це</Uk> in *this is* stays the same.
</Note>

<Note>
  The standard also lists <Uk>оце́й</Uk> and <Uk>ото́й</Uk>, emphatic forms of <Uk>цей</Uk> and <Uk>той</Uk> that change in the same way, and the rare <Uk>сей</Uk> [src: pravopys-2026, §111]. This page does not cover them.
</Note>
